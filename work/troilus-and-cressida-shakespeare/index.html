Play · c. 1602

Troilus and Cressida

by William Shakespeare

Seven years into the siege of Troy, the young prince Troilus, sick with love, enlists Cressida's uncle Pandarus to bring him to her bed, and the two swear eternal faith. Almost at once Cressida is traded to the Greek camp in exchange for the captured Antenor, where she yields to Diomedes while Troilus looks on. Around this affair the Greek leaders quarrel, Achilles sulks in his tent with Patroclus, Ajax is puffed up as a champion, and Thersites rails at the whole war. Hector is killed by Achilles' Myrmidons, and the play closes on Troy's coming fall and Pandarus' bitter epilogue.
