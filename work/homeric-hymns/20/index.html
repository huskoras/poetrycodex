The Homeric Hymns · 1914

Hymn XXI — To Apollo

by Homer fl. c. 8th century BC

Translated by Hugh G. Evelyn-White (1914)

Also in this archive

George Chapman’s translation (c. 1624) · Hymn XXI — To Apollo Compare side by side

(ll. 1-4) Phoebus, of you even the swan sings with clear voice to the
beating of his wings, as he alights upon the bank by the eddying river
Peneus; and of you the sweet-tongued minstrel, holding his high-pitched
lyre, always sings both first and last.
(l. 5) And so hail to you, lord! I seek your favour with my song.

Source Hesiod and Homer, Hesiod, The Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, with an English translation by Hugh G. Evelyn-White, trans. Hugh G. Evelyn-White (Loeb Classical Library) (London: William Heinemann; Macmillan (New York), 1914). Digital text: Project Gutenberg 348.
