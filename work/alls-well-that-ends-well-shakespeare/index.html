Comedy · c. 1604–1605

All's Well That Ends Well

by William Shakespeare

Helena, the orphaned daughter of a famous physician, loves Bertram, the young Count of Rossillon, who is far above her in rank and will not look at her. When she cures the dying King of France with her father's remedy, she asks for Bertram as her reward, but he flees to the Florentine wars rather than accept her. She follows him, and by a bed-trick and a stolen ring she fulfils the impossible conditions he sets, while his braggart companion Parolles is exposed as a coward and a liar. This so-called problem comedy makes a bitter, unsettled mirror of the marriage plot, and its title is shadowed by doubt about whether all can truly be well.
