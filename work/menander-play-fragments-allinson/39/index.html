The Shorter Play-Fragments · 1921

The Women who Would Drink Hemlock

by Menander 4th century BC (Paton’s date)

Translated by Francis G. Allinson (1921)

(A) Is this then a dream?

(B) If we are sleeping—nay, but he offers three talents as dowry, and along with them five mnae for dress and adornment.

(A) I am not awake!

(B) Well, rouse up. He’s making ready the wedding now. . . a tick.

(A) What’s that you say? I, a tick?

(B) . . . last evening was present.

(A) What of it?

(B) . . . seated he talks.

(A) With what one of his companions?

(B) With Chaereas.

(A) Where? I wish to see them.

(B) There is an exedra near . . .

(A) Here somewhere on the right.

(B) They are chattering there now.

(A) I'll go in and see them.

(Exeunt A and B. Enter C (one of the women?).)

I suffered terribly, but by my sufferings I was working out these present blessings, for I had never gained this had 1 not then suffered. Therefore, by the gods, let no one ever be excessively despondent because he fares ill, for just this, perhaps, turns out to be the occasion of blessing.

Know Thyself.
This “Know Thyself” means if thou wilt investigate thy own circumstances and what should be thy own line of action.
