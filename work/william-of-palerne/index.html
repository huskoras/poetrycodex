Verse romance · c. 1350-1361

William of Palerne

Middle English alliterative romance (William and the Werwolf), ed. W. W. Skeat (EETS Extra Series 1, 1867)

by Anonymous (Middle English)

William, the child-prince of Apulia, is carried off by a werwolf who is really a Spanish prince bewitched by his stepmother. Raised by a cowherd and then at the Roman emperor's court, William falls in love with the emperor's daughter Melior, and the two flee in bearskins and then in hartskins, guided and guarded by the werwolf, until William wins a kingdom, the werwolf is restored to his own shape, and William is crowned emperor of Rome. The poem, about 5,500 lines, was made c. 1350 for Humphrey de Bohun, Earl of Hereford, from the French romance Guillaume de Palerne.
