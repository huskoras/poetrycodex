The Poems of Shemseddin Mohammed Hafiz of Shiraz, Volume I (Payne) · 1901

Ghazel 139

by Hafiz c. 1315–1390

Translated by John Payne (1901)

Wine without mixture and skinker gent The twin snares of the Way are,
To the springes whereof the wise of the world Delivered for a prey are.

Though lover and drunkard and debauchee And black of book myself am,
Thank God that our friends of the city free From sin (or so they say) are !

Thy foot in the tavern set not thou. Except in the way of breeding,
For the folk of its doorway confidants Of the king whom all obey are.

No fashion fit for wayfarer Or dervish is oppression;
Bring wine, for no Sons of the Path, in truth, The zealots of the day are.

Do no oppression, for shattered is The glory of heart-bewitchers,
When bondmen flee from before their face And servants gone astray are.

View not the beggars of Love with scorn ; For know thou that these people
Monarchs uncrowned and kings without Girdle and bright array are.

Beware of the wind of pride; for, when It blows, the fair unminded,
For crops of devotion a thousand, half A barley com to pay are.

The slave of the high intent am I Of topers of one colour
And not of the crew who blue of gown And black of heart and way are.

August is the majesty of Love : Pluck up a spirit, Hafiz;
For lovers to them but those admit Who pure of all affray are.
