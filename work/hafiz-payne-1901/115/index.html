The Poems of Shemseddin Mohammed Hafiz of Shiraz, Volume I (Payne) · 1901

Ghazel 121

by Hafiz c. 1315–1390

Translated by John Payne (1901)

Now that the rose in the meads To life is returned from the dead.
The violet prone at her feet Layeth in homage its head.

The cup of the morning quaff To the clamour of tabret and harp;
Yea, kiss thou the cup-bearer's chin. To the warble of rebeck and reed.

Sit never in rose-time without Beloved and ghittem and wine.
For a week, like the season of life. Is the time of the roses red.

The earth, with the zodiac-signs Of the florets, is grown as bright,
By the happy auspice of Spring, As the firmament overhead.

Arise; in the garden renew The rites of the Magians' creed,
Now Nimrod his fire once more Hath lit in the tulip-bed.

At the hand of a lovely maid, A soft-cheeked one, Jesus-breathed,
Drink wine and of Aad and Themóud Be never a word more said!

As the Garden Etern is the world In the season of lily and rose:
But alack! what a vaileth ? Therein Was never abiding-stead.

Since, Solomon-like, on the air A rider the rose is grown,
At dawn, from the throat of the bird When David's ditties are sped,

Brim, brim thou the cup ! To the health Of the Asef of this our age,
The Vizier Imad-ed-din, The blood of the grape be shed !

Come, Hafiz, from life in his time Eternal felicity seek.
The shade of his grace o'er us For ever and aye be spread !

Bring wine; for Hafiz's trust On the mercy of God most High,
Forgiver of sins, is still And will be establish
