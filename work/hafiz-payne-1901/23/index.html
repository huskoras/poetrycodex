The Poems of Shemseddin Mohammed Hafiz of Shiraz, Volume I (Payne) · 1901

Ghazel 25

by Hafiz c. 1315–1390

Translated by John Payne (1901)

Of the august Friend's pardon Hopeful, indeed, am I;
Sinned though I have, in her kindness, Natheless, my hope doth lie.

Yea, nly default, I know it. Will she o'erpass; for, though
Fay-like, ihe hath the nature Of angels from on high.

So have we wept for sorrow That, when our streaming tears
See they, "Lo, what is this river?" Say all who pass us by.

Our head, like the ball in the horse-course, We play at the end of thy street;
But none is aware what that street is Nor what is the ball we ply.

Thy tress, without word of gainsayal, All hearts to itself doth draw :
For who with thy heart-drawing ringlets Conclusions dareth try?

A life-time it is since I scented The perfume of that thy tress;
Yet still in my spirit's nostrils The traces thereof aby:

A hair is thy waist; I know not What manner of hair it is;
And as for thy mouth, 'tis nothing; No sign of it I descry.

Indeed, at thine image I marvel, That, though, without cease, my tears
Still flood it and wash and scour it. It goeth not from mine eye !

Nay, ill is thy plight, o Hafiz, Distracted as thou art;
But yet in the scent of her tresses Fair hope for thee is nigh.
