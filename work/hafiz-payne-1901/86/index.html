The Poems of Shemseddin Mohammed Hafiz of Shiraz, Volume I (Payne) · 1901

Ghazel 90

by Hafiz c. 1315–1390

Translated by John Payne (1901)

Who, o celestial loveling. Thy face-veil's knot unties ?
Who gives thee seed and water, O Bird of Paradise ?

For this thought heart-consuming, Whose bosom is the place
And homestead of thy slumber. Sleep hath forsworn mine eyes.

All suddenly thou wentest From me, heart-wounded one :
I wonder, in what quarter Thy place of slumber lies !

'Tis plain, o fair, that lofty Thy dignity is grown.
Since not a jot thou heark'nest To my complaining sighs.

Thou ask'st not of the wretched : I fear me, no concern
Of pardon or requital Thy spirit occupies.

palace, heart-enkindling, That art my love's abode,
God grant thou be not ruined Of Time's calamities !

Far, in this waste, 's the well-spring ; Beware ! thy mazed sight
Lest, with the mocking mirage, The desert-ghoul surprise.

The shaft, that at my bosom Thou launch'd'st, went astray ;
I wonder what thy malice will after this devise !

I wonder how the pathway Of eld thou'lt fieu-e, o heart !
God knoweth that youth's season Thou'st spent on idle wise.

Thine eye wine-selling stoppeth The way on lovers' hearts ;
Forsooth, 'tis mortal poison, The wine which it supplies.

Hafiz no slave that fleeth His lord is : show thou grace ;
Come back, or e'er, of grieving For thy despite, he dies.
