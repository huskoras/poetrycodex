Epic poem · 1905–1925

The Shahnama of Firdausi

Done into English by Arthur George Warner and Edmond Warner

by Firdausi

Translated by Arthur George Warner and Edmond Warner (1905–1925)

Also in this archive

James Atkinson’s translation (1832) →

The Warners' complete blank-verse English translation of Firdausi's Persian national epic; all nine volumes, from the creation of kingship under Gaiumart to the war with Mazandaran, Suhrab, Siyawush and the coming of Kai Khusrau, then Kai Khusrau's long wars with Turan (Piran, Kamus, the Khan of Chin, Bizhan and Manizha, the Twelve Rukhs, the fall of Afrasiyab) and the reign of Luhrasp, then the reign of Gushtasp (the coming of Zarduhsht, the wars with Arjasp, Asfandiyar's Seven Stages, the fight of Rustam and Asfandiyar, the death of Rustam), Bahman, Humai and Darab, and the last two reigns of the Kaianian line, Dara and Sikandar, the legend of Alexander; then the Sasanian dynasty, from the Ashkanians and the rise of Ardshir Papakan through the reigns of his successors, Bahram Gur's hunts and adventures, Nushirwan's justice with the tales of Buzurjmihr and Mahbud and the invention of chess, and Hurmuzd, to the long reign of Khusrau Parwiz (the revolt of Bahram Chubina, Khusrau's flight to Rum, Shirin, and his fall), then the brief reigns of his successors (the lament of Barbad, the death of Shirin, the women Shahs Purandukht and Azarmdukht), and last the reign of Yazdagird: the Arab conquest, the death of Rustam at Kadisiya, the flight to Khurasan and the murder of Yazdagird by the miller, ending with Firdausi's own closing words.
