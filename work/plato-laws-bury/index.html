Philosophical dialogue · c. 348 BC

Laws

by Plato

Translated by R. G. Bury (1926)

Plato's last and longest dialogue: an Athenian Stranger, a Cretan and a Spartan walk from Knossos to the cave of Zeus, talking the whole way, and draw up the laws of a new colony, from education, marriage and drinking parties to crime, religion and the Nocturnal Council that is to guard the state.
