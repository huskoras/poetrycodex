The Castle of Perseverance · c. 1425

Scene VII: Death and the World

by Anonymous (Middle English) c. 13th–16th century

Mors. ow, now it is tyme hye
to castyn Mankynde to dethys dynt.
In all hys werkis he is vnslye;
mekyl of hys lyf he hath myspent.
to Mankynde I ney ny;
with rewly rappys he schal be rent.
whanne I com, Iche man drede forþi,
but ȝyt is þer no geyn [i]-went,
hey hyl, holte, nyn hethe.
ȝe schul me drede, euery-chone;
whanne I come, ȝe schul grone;
My name in londe is lefte a-lone:
I hatte 'drery Dethe.'

drery is my deth-drawth;
a-geyns me may no man stonde;
I durke, & down [I] brynge to nowth,
lordys & ladys in euery londe.
whom-so I haue a lessun tawth,
onethys sythen schal he mowe stonde;
In my carful clothys he schal be cawth,
ryche, pore, fre & bonde:
whanne I come, þei goo no more.
where-so I wende in any lede,
euery man of me hat drede;
lette I wyl, for no mede,
to smyte sadde & sore.

dyngne dukys arn a-dred
whanne my b[l]astys arn on hem blowe;
lordys in londe arn ouer-led;
with þis launce I leye hem lowe.
kyngys kene, & knytys kyd,
I do hem deluyn in a throwe;
In banke I buske hem a bed;
sad sorwe to hem I sowe;
I tene hem as I trowe.
as kene koltys þow þey kynse,
a-geyns me is no defens:
In þe grete pestelens,
þanne was I wel knowe.

but now al-most I am for-ȝete;
men, of deth, holde no tale;
in coveytyse here good þey gete;
þe grete fyschys ete þe smale;
but whane I dele my derne dette,
þo prowde men I schal a-vale:
hem schal helpyn, noþer mel nor mete,
tyl þey be drewyn to dethys dale:
my lawe þei schul lerne.
þer ne is peny nor pownde
þat any of ȝou schal saue sownde;
tyl ȝe be grauyn vndyr grownde,
þer may no man me werne.

to Mankynde now wyl I reche;
he hathe hole hys hert on Coveytyse;
a newe lessun I wyl hym teche,
þat he schal bothe grwcchyn & gryse.
no lyf in londe schal ben his leche;
I schal hym proue of myn empryse;
with þis poynt I schal hym broche,
& wappyn hym in a woful wyse;
no body schal ben hys bote. [Goes to Mankind, who enters.]
I schal þee schapyn a schenful schappe:
now I kylle þee with myn knappe!
I reche to þee, Mankynde, a rappe
to þyne herte rote.

Humanum Genus. A, Deth, Deth! drye is þi dryfte.
ded is my desteny!
myn hed is cleuyn al in a clyfte!
for clappe of care now I crye;
myn eye-ledys may I not lyfte;
myn braynys waxyn al emptye;
I may not onys myn hod up schyfte.
with Dethys dynt[e] now I dey!
Syr Werld, I am hent.
Werld, Werld! haue me in mende!
goode syr Werld! helpe now Mankende!
but þou me helpe, Deth schal me schende;
he hat dyth to me a dynt.

Werld! my wyt waxyt wronge;
I chaunge boþe hyde & hewe;
myn eye-ledys waxyn al outewronge;
but þou me helpe, sore it schal me rewe.
now holde þat þou haste be-hete me longe,
for all felechepys olde & newe,
lesse me of my peynys stronge!
sum bote of bale þou me brewe,
þat I may of þee ȝelpe.
Werld, for olde aqweyntawns,
helpe me fro þis sory chawns!
Deth hathe lacchyd me with his launce!
I deye but þou me helpe.

Mundus. owe, Mankynde! hathe Dethe with þee spoke?
a-geyns hym helpyth no wage.
I wold þou were in þe erthe be-loke,
& a-noþer hadde þyne erytage!
oure bonde of loue schal sone be broke;
In colde clay schal be þy cage;
now schal þe Werld on þee be wroke,
for þou hast don so gret outrage;
þi good þou schalt for-goo.
Werldlys good þou hast for-gon,
& with tottys þou schalt be torn:
þus haue I seruyd here be-forn,
a hundryd thousand moo.
Humanum Genus. ow, Werld! Werld euere worthe wo!
& þou, synful Coveytyse,
whanne þat a man schal fro ȝou go,
ȝe werke with hym on a wonder wyse.
þe wytte of þis werld is sorwe & wo:
be ware, good men, of þis gyse!
þus hathe he seruyd many on mo.
In sorwe slakyth al his a-syse;
he beryth a tenynge tungge.
Whyl I leyd with hym my lott,
ȝe seyn whou fayre he me be-hett;
& now he wolde I were a clott,
In colde cley for to clynge.

[Mundus calls to Garcio.]

Mundus. how, boy, a-ryse! now þou muste wende
on myn erdyn, be steppe & stalle;
go brewe Mankynde a byttyr bende,
& putte hym out of hys halle!
lete him þer-Inne no lenger lende!
For-brostyn, I trowe, be hys galle.
For þou art not of hys kende,
all hys erytage wyl þee wele be-falle:
þus faryth myn fayre feres.
oftyn tyme I haue ȝou told,
þo men, þat ȝe arn to lest be-hold,
comynly schal ȝoure wonnynge wold,
& ben ȝoure next eyrys.

Garcio. Werld worthy, in wedys wounde,
I þanke þee for þi grete ȝyfte.
I go glad up-on þis grounde,
to putte Mankynde out of his þryfte.
I trowe he stynkyth þis ilke stounde;
In-to a lake I schal hym lyfte;
hys parkys, placys, & penys rounde,
with me schul dryuen, in þis dryfte,
In baggys as þei ben bownde.
for I þynke for to dele,
I vow to God, neythyr corn nor mele.
if he haue a schete, he beryth hym wele,
where-Inne he may be wo[unde]. [tunc iet ad humanum genus.

whou faryst, Mankynde? art þou ded?
be Goddys body, so I wene,
he is heuyer þanne any led.
I wold he were grauyn vnder grene.

Humanum Genus. a-byde, I breyd uppe with myn hed.
what art þou? what woldyst þou mene?
wheydyr comyst þou for good or qwed?
with peynys prycke þou dost me tene,
þe sothe for to sey.
telle me now, so God þee saue,
fro whom comyst þou, good knaue!
what dost þou here? wha[t] woldyst þou haue?
telle me or I deye.

Garcio. I am com to haue al þat þou hast.
poundys, parkys, & euery place,
al þat þou hast gotyn fyrst & last,
þe Werld hathe grauntyd it me of his grace,
for I haue ben his page.
he wot wel þou schalt be ded,
neuere-more to ete bred;
þerfore he hath for þee red
who schal haue þyne erytage.

Humanum Genus. what, deuyl! þou art not of my kyn;
þou dedyst me neuere no maner good;
I hadde leuer sum nyfte, or sum cosyn,
or sum man hadde it, of my blod;
In sum stede I wold it stod.
now schal I in a dale be delue,
& haue no good þer of my selue.
be God & be hys apostelys twelue,
I trowe þe Werld be wod.

Garcio. ȝa, ȝa! þi parte schal be þe leste.
deye on! for I am maystyr here.
I schal þee makyn a nobyl feste,
& þanne haue I do myn deuere.
þe Werld bad me þis gold a-reste,
holt & hallys, & castell clere.
þe Werldis joye & hys jentyl jeste
Is now þyne, now myn, boþe fere & nere.
go hens! for þis is myne.
Syn þou art ded, & browth of dawe,
of þi deth, syr, I am ryth fawe.
þou þou knowe not þe werldys lawe,
he hath ȝoue me al þat was þyne.

Humanum Genus. I preye þee now, syn þou þis good schalt gete,
telle þi name or þat I goo.

Garcio. Loke þat þou it not forȝete:
my name is 'I wot neuere whoo.'

Humanum Genus. 'I wot neuere who,' so wele say,
now am I sory of my lyf:
I haue purchasyd, many a day,
londys & rentys with mekyl stryf;
I haue purchasyd holt & hay,
parkis & poundys, & bouris blyfe,
goode gardeynys, with gryffys gay,
to myne chyldyr & to myn wyfe,
In dethe whanne I were dyth.
of my purchas I may be wo;
for, as þout, it is not so,
but a gedelynge, 'I wot neuere who,'
hath al þat þe Werld me be-hyth.
now, alas, my lyf is lak;
bitter balys I gynne to brewe;
Certis, a vers þat Dauid spak
I þe sawter, I fynde it trewe:
Tesaurizat, & ignorat cui congregabit ea.
tresor, tresor, it hathe no tak;
it is oþer mens, olde & newe.
ow, ow! my good gothe al to wrak!
sore may Mankynde rewe!
God kepe me fro dyspayr!
al my good, with-out[en] fayle,
I haue gadryd with gret trauayle,
þe Werld hathe ordeynyd of his entayle;
I wot neuere who to be myn eyr.

now, good men, takythe example at me!
do for ȝoure self whyl ȝe han spase!
for many men þus seruyd be,
þorwe þe Werld, in dyuerse place.
I bolne & bleyke in blody ble,
& as a flour, fadyth my face.
to helle I schal bothe fare & fle,
but God me graunte of his grace.
I deye certeynly:
now my lyfe I haue lore;
myn hert brekyth; I syhe sore;
a word may I speke no more;
I putte me in Godys mercy. [Dies.]

[Enter Anima.]

Anima. Mercy, þis was my last tale
þat euere my body was a-bowth.
but Mercy helpe me in þis vale,
of dampnynge drynke, sore I me doute.
body! þou dedyst brew a byttyr bale,
to þi lustys whanne gannyst loute;
þi sely sowle schal ben a-kale;
I beye þi dedys with rewly rowte;
& al it is for gyle.
euere þou hast be coueytows,
falsly to getyn londe & hows;
to me þou hast brokyn a byttyr jows;
so welaway þe whyle!

[To the Good Angel.]
now, swet aungel, what is þi red?
þe ryth red, þou me reche!
now my body is dressyd to ded,
helpe now me, & be my leche!
dyth þou me fro deuelys drede!
þy worthy weye, þou me teche!
I hope þat God wyl helpyn & be myn hed,
For Mercy was my laste speche:
þus made my body his ende.
[. . .]

[Malus Angelus.] wyttnesse of al þat ben a-bowte,
Syr Coueytyse, he had hym owte;
þerfor he schal, with-outyn dowte,
with me to helle pytt.

Bonus Angelus. ȝe, a-las, & welawo!
a-ȝeyns Coueytyse can I not telle.
resun wyl I fro þee goo,
for, wrechyd sowle, þou muste to helle.
Coueytyse, he was þi fo;
he hathe þee schapyn a schameful schelle;
þus hathe seruyd many on mo,
tyl þei be dyth to dethys delle,
to byttyr balys bowre.
þou muste to peyne, be ryth resun,
with Coveytyse, for he is chesun;
þou art trappyd ful of tresun,
but Mercy be þi socowre.

For, ryth wel þis founde he haue.
a-ȝeyns Rythwysnesse may I not holde;
þou muste with hym to careful caue,
for grete skyllys þat he hathe tolde.
fro þee a-wey I wandyr & waue;
for þee I clynge in carys colde;
a-lone now I [must] þee laue,
whylyst þou fallyst in fendys folde,
In helle to hyde & hylle.
Rytwysnesse wyl þat þou wende
forthe a-wey with þe fende.
but Mercy wyl to þee sende,
of þee can I no skylle.

Anima. alas, Mercy! þou art to longe!
of sadde sorwe now may I synge;
holy wryt, it is ful wronge,
but mercy pase alle þynge.
I am ordeynyd to peynys stronge;
In wo is dressyd myn wonnynge;
In helle, on hokys I schal honge.
but mercy fro a welle sprynge,
þis deuyl wyl haue me a-way.
weleaway! I was ful wod
þat I forsoke myn Aungyl good,
& with Coueytyse stod
tyl þat day þat I schuld dey.

Malus Angelus. ȝa! why woldyst þou be coueytous,
& drawe þee a-gayn to synne?
I schal þee brewe a byttyr jous;
In bolynynge bondys þou schalt brenne;
In hyȝe helle schal be þyne hous;
In pycke & ter, to grone & grenne,
þou schalt lye drenkelyd as a mous;
þer may no man þer-fro þee werne
for þat ilke wyll.
þat day þe ladys þou for-soke,
& to my counsel þou þee toke,
þou were betyr an-hangyn on hoke
up on a jebet hyll.
farter fowle! þou schalt be frayed
tyl þou be frettyd & al for-bled;
foule mote þou be dysmayed,
þat þou schalt þus ben ouyrled;
for Coueytyse þou hast a-sayed,
In byttyr balys þou schalt be bred;
al mankynde may be wel payed,
whon Coueytyse makyth þee a-dred
with rappys I þee rynge.
we schul to helle, bothe to,
& bey [for euer] in inferno;
Nulla est redempcio,
for no kynnys þynge.

Now dagge we hens a dogge trot;
In my dongion I schal þee dere;
on þee is many a synful spot;
þerfore þis schame I schal þee schere
whanne þou comyst to my neste.
why woldyst þou—schrewe schalt neuere þee;—
but in þi lyue don aftyr me?
& þi good aungyl [he] tawth þee
al-wey to þe beste.

ȝa! but þou woldyst hym not leue;
to Coueytyse al-wey þou drow;
þerfore schalt þou euyl preue;
þat foul synne þi soule slow.
I schal fonde þee to greue,
& putte þee in peynnys plow.
haue þis, & euyl mote þou scheue,
for þou seydyst neuere 'I-now I-now:
þus lacche I þee þus lowe.
þow þou kewe as a kat,
for þi coueytyse, haue þou þat!
I schal þee bunche with my bat,
& ronge þee on a rowe.
lo! synful tydynge,
boy, on þi bak I brynge.
spedely þou sprynge;
þi placebo I schal synge;
to deuelys delle
I schal þee bere to helle.
I wyl not dwelle:
haue good day! I goo to helle. [Exeunt.]
