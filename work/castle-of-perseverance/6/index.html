The Castle of Perseverance · c. 1425

Scene VI: The Seven Deadly Sins and the Castle (part 1 of 6)

by Anonymous (Middle English) c. 13th–16th century

[Enter Backbiter.]

Detraccio. all þyngis I crye a-gayn þe pes
to knyt & knaue; þis is my kende.
ȝa! dyngne dukis on her des,
In bytter balys I hem bynde;
cryinge & care, chydynge & ches
& sad sorwe, to hem I sende.
ȝa! lowde lesyngis lachyd in les,
of talys vn-trewe is al my mende;
Mannys bane a-bowtyn I bere.
I wyl þat ȝe wetyn, all þo þat ben here,
for I am knowyn, fer & nere,
I am þe werldys messengere;
my name is Bacbytere.

with euery wyth I walke & wende,
& euery man now louyth me wele;
with lowde lesyngis vndyr lende,
to dethys dynt I dresse & dele.
to speke fayre be-forn, & fowle be-hynde,
a-mongis men at mete & mele,
trewly, lordys, þis is my kynde.
þer I renne up-on a whele
I am feller þanne a fox.
fleterynge & flaterynge is my lessun;
with lesyngis I tene boþe tour & town,
with letterys of defamacyoun
I bere here in my box.

I am lyth of lopys þorwe euery londe;
myn holy happys may not ben hyd;
to may not to-gedyr stonde,
but I, Bakbyter, be þe thyrde.
I schape ȝone boyis to schame & schonde,
all þat wyl bowyn whanne I hem bydde;
to lawe of londe in feyth I fonde;
Whanne talys vntrewe arn be-tydde,
Bakbyter is wyde spronge:
þorwe þe werld, be downe & dalys,
all a-bowtyn I brewe balys;
Euery man tellyth talys
Aftyr my fals tunge.

þer-fore I am mad massenger
to lepyn ouer londis leye,
þorwe all þe world, fer & ner,
vnsayd sawys for to seye.
In þis holte I hunte here
for to spye a preuy pley;
For whanne Mankynde is cloþyd clere,
þanne schal I techyn hym þe wey
to þe dedly synnys seuene.
Here I schal a-bydyn with my pese,
þe wronge to do hym for to chese,
for I þynke þat he schal lese
þe lyth of hey heuene.

Voluptas [re-entering with the World, Mankind, and Folly]. Worthy World, in welthys wonde,
here is Mankynde ful fare in folde!
In bryth besauntys he is bownde,
& bon to bowe to ȝou so bolde.
he leuyth in lustys euery stounde;
holy to ȝou he hathe hym ȝolde.
for to makyn hym gay on grounde,
worthy World, þou art be-holde;
þis werld is wel at ese;
For to God I make a-vow,
Mankynde had leuer now
greue God with synnys row,
þanne þe World to dysplese.

Stulticia. dysplese þee, he wyl for no man;
On me, Folye, is al hys þowth.
trewly Mankynde nowth nen can
þynke on God þat hathe hym bowth.
worthy World, wyth as swan,
In þi loue lely is he lawth;
sythyn he cowde, & fyrste be-gan.
þee forsakyn wolde he nowth,
but ȝeue hym to folye;
& syþyn he hathe to þee be trewe,
I rede þee forsakyn hym for no newe;
lete vs plesyn hym tyl þat he rewe,
In hell to hangyn hye.

Mundus. now, Folye, fayre þee be-fall!
& Luste, blyssyd be þou ay!
ȝe han browth Mankynde to myn hall,
Sertis in a nobyl a-ray.
with werldys welthys, with-Inne þese wall,
I schal hym feffe of þat I may.
welcum Mankynde! to þee I call,
clenner cloþyd þanne any clay,
be downe, dale, & dyche.
Mankynde! I rede þat þou reste
with me, þe Werld, as it is beste;
loke þou holde myn hende feste,
& euere þou schalt be ryche.

Humanum Genus. Whou schul I, but I þi hestis helde?
þou werkyst with me holy my wyll;
þou feffyst me with fen & felde,
& hye hall, be holtis & hyll.
In werldly wele my wytte I welde;
In joye I jette, with juelys jentyll;
on blysful banke my boure is bylde,
In veynglorye I stonde styll;
I am kene as a knyt.
Who-so a-geyn þe werld wyl speke,
Mankynde schal on hym be wreke;
In stronge presun I schal hym steke,
be it wronge or ryth.

Mundus. a! Mankynde! wel þee be-tyde,
þat þi loue on me is sette!
In my bowris þou schalt a-byde,
& ȝyt fare makyl þe bette.
I Feffe þee in all my wonys wyde
In dale of dros, tyl þou be deth;
I make þee lord of mekyl pryde:
syr, at þyn owyn mowthis mette,
I fynde in þee no tresun.
In all þis worlde, be se & sonde,
parkis, placis, lawnde & londe,
here I ȝyfe þee with myn honde,
syr, an opyn sesun.

go to my tresorer, Syr Couetouse!
loke þou tell hym as I seye!
bydde hym make þee mayster in his house,
with penys & powndis for to pleye.
loke þou ȝeue not a lous
of þe day þat þou schalt deye.
messenger, do now þyne vse!
Bakbytere, teche hym þe weye!
þou art swetter þanne mede.
Mankynde! take with þee Bakbytynge!
lefe hym for no maner thynge!
Flepergebet, with hys flaterynge,
standith mankynde in stede.

Detraccion. Bakbytynge & Detraccion
schal goo with þee fro toun to toun.
haue don, Mankynde, & cum doun!
I am þyne owyn page.
I schal bere þee wyttnesse with my myth,
whanne my lord þe Werlde it behyth.
lo, where syr Coueytyse sytt, [Points to his Scaffold on the N.-East.]
& bydith us in his stage.

Humanum Genus. Syr Worlde, I wende, in Coueytyse to chasyn my kende.
Mundus. haue hym in mende! & I-wys þanne schalt þou be ryth þende.

Bonus Angelus. alas, Jhesu, jentyl justyce!
wheder may mans Good Aungyl wende?
now schal careful Coueytyse,
Mankende trewly al [to-]schende;
hys sely goste may sore a-gryse;
Bakbytynge bryngyth hym in bytter bonde.
worldly wyttis, ȝe are not wyse;
ȝour louely lyfe, a-mys ȝe spende,
& þat schal ȝe sore smert.
parkys, poundys, & many pens,
þei semyn to ȝou swetter þanne sens;
but Goddis seruyse, nyn hys commaundementis,
stondyth ȝou not at hert.

Malus Angelus. ȝa! whanne þe fox prechyth, kepe wel ȝore gees!
he speckyth as it were a holy pope.
goo, felaw, & pyke of þe lys
þat crepe þer up-on þi cope!
þi part is pleyed al at þe dys
þat þou schalt haue here, as I hope;
tyl Mankynde fallith to podys prys,
Coueytyse schal hym grype & grope
tyl sum schame hym schende.
tyl man be dyth in dethys dow,
he seyth neuere he hath I-now;
þer-fore, goode boy, cum blow
at my neþer ende!

Detraccio [entering]. Syr Coueytyse, God þee saue,
þi pens & þi poundys all!
I, Bakbytere, þyn owyn knaue,
haue browt Mankynde vn-to þine hall.
þe Worlde bad þou schuldyst hym haue,
& feffyn hym, what-so be-fall.
In grene gres tyl he be graue,
putte hym in þi precyous pall,
Coueytyse! it were all rewthe.
whyl he walkyth in worldly wolde,
I, Bakbyter, am with hym holde;
Lust & Folye, þo barouns bolde,
to hem he hath plyth hys trewthe.

Auaricia [entering]. Ow, Mankynde! blyssyd mote þou be!
I haue louyd þee derworthly many a day,
& so I wot wel þat þou dost me;
cum up & se my ryche a-ray!
it were a gret poynte of pyte
but Coueytyse were to þi pay.
Sit up ryth here in þis se;
I schal þee lere of werldlys lay,
þat fadyth as a flode.
with good I-now I schal þee store;
& ȝyt oure game is but lore,
but þou coueyth mekyl more,
þanne euere schal do þee goode.

þou muste ȝyfe þee to symonye,
extorsion & false asyse;
helpe no man but þou haue why;
pay not þi serwauntys here serwyse!
þi neyborys, loke þou dystroye;
tythe not on non wyse!
here no begger, þou he crye,
& þanne schalt þou ful sone ryse.
& whanne þou vsyste marchaundyse,
loke þat þou be sotel of sleytys,
& also swere al be deseytys,
bye & sell be fals weytys,
for þat is kynde coueytyse.

be not a-gaste of þe grete curse;
þis lofly lyfe may longe leste;
be þe peny in þi purs,
lete hem cursyn, & don here beste.
What, deuyl of hell, art þou þe wers
þow þou brekyste Goddys heste?
Do after me! I am þi nors.
all-wey gadyr, & haue non reste;
in wynnynge be al þi werke!
to pore men take none entent,
for þat þou haste longe tyme hent;
In lytyl tyme it may be spent:
þus seyth Caton, þe grete clerke:
'labitur exiguo quod partum tempore longo.'

Humanum Genus. A, Auaryce! wel þou spede!
of werldly wytte þou canst I-wys;
þou woldyst not I hadde nede,
& schuldyst be wrothe if I ferd a-mys;
I schal neuere begger bede
mete nyn drynke, be heuene blys;
rather or I schulde hym cloþe or fede,
he schulde sterue, & stynke I-wys:
Coueytyse, as þou wylt, I wyl do.
where-so þat I fare, be fenne or flod,
I make a-vow, be Goddys blod,
of Mankynde, getyth no man no good,
but if he synge 'si dedero.'

Auaricia. Mankynde! þat was wel songe:
Sertis now þou canst sum skyll.
blyssyd be þi trewe tonge!
In þis bowre þou schalt byde & byll.
Moo synnys I wolde þou vnderfonge;
with coveytyse þee Feffe I wyll;
& þanne sum Pryde I wolde spronge,
hyȝe in þi hert to holdyn & hyll,
& a-bydyn in þi body.
here I feffe þee in myn heuene
with gold & syluer, lyth as leuene;
þe dedly synnys, alle seuene,
I schal do comyn in hy.

Pryde, Wrathe, & Envye,
Com forthe, þe deuelys chyldryn þre!
Lechery, Slawth, & Glotonye,
to mans flesch ȝe are fendis Fre;
Dryuyth downne ouer dalys drye;
beth now blyþe as any be;
ouer hyll & holtys ȝe ȝou hyȝe,
to com to Mankynde & to me,
fro ȝoure dowty dennys!
as dukys dowty, ȝe ȝou dresse!
whanne ȝe sex be comme, I gesse,
þanne be we seuene, & no lesse,
of þe dedly synnys. [Enter Pride, Wrath and Envy.]

Superbia. Wonder hyȝe howtis, on hyll, herd I houte:
Koueytyse kryeth; hys karpynge I kenne.
Summe lord, or summe lordeyn, lely schal loute
to be pyth with perlys of my proude penne.
bon I am to braggyn, & buskyn a-bowt,
raþely & redyly, on rowte for to renne;
be doun, dalys, nor dennys, no dukis I dowt;
also fast for to Fogge, be flodys & be fenne,
I rore whanne I ryse.
Syr Belyal, bryth of ble!
to ȝou I recomaunde me:
haue good day, my fader fre,
For I goo to Coveytyse.
Ira. Whanne Coveytyse cried, & carpyd of care,
þanne must I, wod wreche, walkyn & wende
hyȝe ouer holtis, as hound aftyr hare.
If I lette, & were þe last, he schuld me sore schende;
I buske my bold baston, be bankis ful bare,
Sum boy schal be betyn, & browth vnder bonde;
Wrath schal hym wrekyn, & weyin his ware;
for-lorn schal al be, for lusti laykys, & londe,
as a lythyr page.
Syr Belyal blak & blo,
haue good day! now I goo
for to fell þi foo
With wyckyd wage.

Invidia. Whanne Wrath gynnyth walke in ony wyde wonys,
Envye flet as a fox, & folwyth on faste.
Whanne þou sterystis or starystis, or stumble up-on stonys,
I lepe as a lyon: me is loth to be þe laste.
ȝa, I breyde bytter balys in body & in bonys;
I frete myn herte, & in kare I me kast.
goo we to Coveytyse, all þre at onys,
with oure grysly gere, a grome for to gast;
þis day schal he deye.
Belsabubbe! now haue good day!
for we wyl wendyn in good a-ray,
al þre in fere, as I þe say,
Pride, Wrath, & Envye.

Belial. Fare-wel now, chyrdryn fayre to fynde!
Do now wel ȝoure olde owse
whanne ȝe com to Mankynde!
make hym wroth & Envyous;
leuyth not lytly vnder lynde:
to his sowle brewyth a bytter jous.
whanne he is ded, I schal hym bynde
In hell, as Catte dothe þe mows:
now buske ȝou forþe on brede!
I may be blythe as any be,
for mankynde, in euery cuntre,
Is rewlyd be my chyldyr þre,
Envye, Wrath, & Pryde. [Exit Belial.]

[Enter Gluttony, Lechery, Sloth and Flesh.]

Gula. a grom gan gredyn gayly on grounde;
of me, gay Glotoun, gan al hys gale.
I stampe & I styrte, & stynt up-on stounde;
to a staunche deth I stakyr & stale.
what boyes, with here belys, in my bondis be bownde,
boþe here bak & here blod, I brewe al to bale;
I fese folke to fyth, tyl here flesch fonde;
Whanne summe han dronkyn a drawth, þei dropyn in a dale;
in me is here mynde.
mans Florchynge flesch,
Fayre, frele & Fresch,
I rape to rewle in a rese,
to kloy et in my kynde.

Luxuria. In mans kyth, I cast me a castel to kepe.
I, Lechery, with lykynge am lovyd in iche a londe.
With my sokelys of swettnesse, I sytte & I slepe;
Many berdys I bryng to my bytter bonde.
In wo & in wrake, wyckyd wytis schal wepe,
þat in my wonys wylde wyl not out wende.
Whanne Mankynde is castyn, undyr clouris to crepe,
þanne þe ledrouns, for here lykynge, I schal al to-schende,
trewly to tell.
Syr Flesch, now I wende,
With lust in my lende,
to cachyn Mankynde
to þe devyl of hell.

Accidia. ȝa! waht seyst þou of Syr Slawth, with my soure syth?
Mankynde louyth me wel wys, as I wene;
men of relygyon, I rewle in my ryth;
I lette Goddis seruyse, þe soþe may be sene.
In bedde I brede brothel, with my berdis bryth;
lordys, ladys, & lederounnys, to my lore leene;
mekyl of mankynde, in my clokis schal be knyth,
tyl deth dryuyth hem down in dalys be-dene:
We may non lenger a-byde.
Syr Flesch, comly kynge,
in þee is al oure bredynge:
ȝeue us now þi blyssynge,
For Coveytyse hath cryde.

Caro. Glotony & slawth, Fare-wel in fere!
louely in londe is now ȝour lesse;
& Lecherye, my Dowter so dere,
Dapyrly ȝe dresse ȝou so dyngne on desse.
All þre, my blyssynge ȝe schal haue here;
goth now forth, & gyue ȝe no fors;
it is no nede, ȝou for to lere
to cachyn Mankynde to a care-ful clos
Fro þe bryth blysse off heuene.
þe Werld, þe Flesch, & þe Devyl, are knowe
grete lordis, as we wel owe,
& þorwe Mankynde we settyn & sowe
þe dedly synnys seuene.
[tunc ibunt Superbia, Ira, Invidia, Gula, Luxuria, & Accidia, ad Auariciam; & dicat Superbia:

Superbia. What is þi wyll, Syr Coveytyse?
Why hast þou afftyr vs sent?
Whanne þou Creydyst, we ganne a-gryse,
& come to þee now par asent;
oure loue is on þee lent.
I, Pryde, Wrath, & Envye,
Gloton, Slawth, & Lechery,
We arn cum all sex for þi Crye,
to be at þi commaundement.
Auaricia. Welcum be ȝe, breþeryn all,
& my sy[s]tyr, swete Lecherye!
wytte ȝe why I gan to call?
for ȝe must me helpe, & þat in hy.
Mankynde is now com to myn hall,
with me to dwell, be downys dry;
þerfore ȝe must, what so be-fall,
Feffyn hym with ȝoure foly,
& ellis ȝe don hym wronge.
For whanne Mankynde is kendly koueytous,
he is provd, wrathful, & Envyous;
Glotons, slaw, & lecherous,
þei arn oþer whyle amonge.
