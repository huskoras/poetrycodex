The Castle of Perseverance · c. 1425

The Banns

by Anonymous (Middle English) c. 13th–16th century

Primus Vexil[lator]. Glorious God! in all degres, lord most of myth,
þat heuene & erthe made of nowth, boþe se & londe,
þe aunȝelys in heuene, hym to serue bryth,
& [man]-kynde in mydylerd he made with hys honde,
& [our lo]fly lady, þat lanterne is of lyth,
Save oure lege lord, þe kynge, þe leder of þis londe,
& all þe ryallis of þis revme, & rede hem þe ryth,
& all þe goode comowns of þis towne þat be-forn us stonde
In þis place!
We mustyr ȝou with menschepe,
& Freyne ȝou of Frely frenchepe,
Cryst safe ȝou all fro schenchepe,
þat knowyn wyl oure case!

Secundus Vexillator. þe case of oure comynge, ȝou to declare,
euery man in hym self, for sothe he it may fynde.
whon mankynde in-to þis werld born is ful bare,
& bare schal beryed be at [t]he [l]ast ende,
God hym ȝeuyth to aungelis Ful ȝep & ful ȝare,
þe goode aungel & þe badde, to hym for to lende:
þe goode techyth hym goodnesse; þe badde, synne & sare;
Whanne þe ton hath þe victory, þe toþer goth be-hende,
be skyll.
þe goode aungel coueytyth euermore mans saluacion,
& þe badde bysytyth hem euere to hys dampnacion;
& God hathe govyn man fre arbritracion
Wheþer he wyl hymse[lf] saue or his soule per[yll.]
Primus Vexillator. spylt is man spetously, whanne he to synne asent;
þe bad aungel þanne bryngyth hym iij enmys so stout:
þe Werlde, þe Fende, þe foul Flesch so joly & jent;
þei ledyn hym Ful lustyly with synnys al a-bowt,
Pyth with Pride & Coueytyse, to þe werld is he went,
to meynten his manhod; all men to hym lout.
aftyre Ire & Envye, þe Fend hath to hym lent
Bakbytynge & endytynge, with all men for to route,
Ful evyn;
but þe fowle Flesch, homlyest of all,
Slawth, Lust & Leccherye, gun to hym call
Glotony, & oþer synnys, boþe grette & small:
þis mans soule is soylyd with synnys moo þanne seuyn.

Secundus Vexillator. whanne mans sowle is soylyd with synne & with sore,
þanne þe goode aungyl makyth mykyl mornynge
þat þe lofly lyknesse of God schulde be lore
þorwe þe badde aungellis fals entysynge.
He sendyth to hym concyens, pryckyd Ful pore,
& clere confescyon, with penauns doynge:
þei mevyn man to mendement þat he mys-dyd be-fore;
þus þei callyn hym to clennesse & to good levynge,
with-outyn dystaunce.
Mekenesse, Pacyense, & Charyte,
Sobyrnesse, Besynesse, & Chastyte,
& Largyte, uertuys of good degre,
Man callyth to þe Castel of good Perseueraunce.

Primus Vexillator. þe Castel of Perseuerans, wanne Mankynde hath tan,
Wel armyd with vertus, & ouercome alle vycys,
þere þe Good Aungyl makyth ful mery þanne
þat Mankynde hath ouercome his gostly e[n]mijs.
þe Badde Aungyl mornyþ þat he hath myssyd man;
He callyth þe Werld, þe Fende, & þe foule Flesch, I-wys,
& all þe seuene synnys to do þat þey canne
to brynge Mankynde a-geyn to bale out of blys,
With wronge.
Pride a-saylyth Meknesse with all his myth;
Ire, a-geyns Paciensse, ful fast ganne he fyth;
Envye, a-geyn Charyte strywyth ful ryth;
but Coveytyse a-geyns Largyte fytyth over longe.

ijus Vexillator. Coveytyse, Mankynde euere coveytyth for to qwell:
he gaderith to hym Glotony, a-ȝeyns Sobyrnesse;
Leccherye, with Chastyte ffytith ful fell,
& Slawthe in Goddis seruyse, a-geyns Besynesse.
þus vycys, a-geyns vertues fytyn ful snelle;
euery buskith to brynge man to dystresse;
but Penaunce & Confescion, with Mankynde wyl melle;
þe vycys arn ful lyckely, þe vertues to opresse:
sann dowte;
þus in þe Castel of good Perseuerance
Mankynde is maskeryd with mekyl varyaunce;
þe Goode Aungyl & þe Badde be euere at dystaunce;
þe Goode holdith hym Inne; þe Badde wolde brynge
hym owte.

jus Vexillator. Owt of good perseueraunce, whanne Mankynde wyl not come,
ȝyt þe Badde Aungyl, with Coveytyse hym gan a-sayle,
fyndende hym in pouerte & penaunce so be-nome,
& bryngyth hym in beleue, in defaute for to fayle,
þanne he profyrth hym good & gold, so gret a sowme,
þat if he wyl com a-geyn, & with þe world dayle,
þe Badde Aungyl to þe Werld tollyth hym downe,
þe Castel of Perseueraunce to Fle fro þe dayle
& blysse.
þanne þe Werld be-gynnyth hym to restore;
haue he neuere so mykyl, ȝyt he wold haue more:
þus þe badde aungyl leryth hym hys lore:
þe more a man agyth, þe harder he is.

Primus Vexillator. Hard a man is in age, & Covetouse be kynde;
Whanne all oþer synnys man hath for-sake,
Euere þe more þat he hath, þe more is in his mynde
to gader & to gete good with woo & with wrake:
þus þe Good Aungyl caste is be-hynde,
& þe Badde Aungyl, man to hym takyth,
þat wryngyth hym wrenchys to his last ende,
tyl Deth comyth foul dolfully, & loggyth hym in a lake ful lowe.
þanne is man on molde maskeryd in mynde;
he sendith afftyr his sekkatours, ful fekyl to fynde;
& his eyr aftyrward comyth euere be-hynde:
I wot not who is his name, for he hym nowt knowe.

Secundus Vexillator. Man knowe not who schal be his eyr,
& gouerne his good;
he caryth more for his catel þanne for his cursyd synne;
to putte his good in gouernaunce, he mengyth his mod;
he wolde þat it were scyfftyd a-mongis his ny kynne;
but þer schal com a lythyr ladde with a torne hod,—
I wot neuere who schal be his name, his clopis be ful þynne,—
schal eryth þe erytage þat neuere was of hys blod,
whanne al his lyfe is lytyd up-on a lytyl pynne,
at þe laste,
On lyue whanne [he] may no lenger lende,
Mercy he callyth at hys laste ende:
"Mercy, God! be now myn frende!"
with þat, mans spyryt is paste.

Primus Vexillator. whanne manis spyryt is past, þe Badde Aungyl ful fell
cleymyth þat, for couetyse, mans sowle schuld ben hys,
& for to bere it ful boystowsly with hym in-to hell.
þe Good Aungyl seyth "nay! þe spyryt schal to blys,
For, at his laste ende, of mercy he gan spell,
& þerfore, of mercy schal he nowth mysse;
& oure lofly lady, if sche wyl for hym mell,
be mercy & be menys, in purgatory he is,
in Ful bytter place.
þus mowthys confession
& his hertys contricion
schal saue man fro dampnacion,
be Goddys mercy & grace.

Secundus Vexillator. Grace, if God wyl graunte us, of hys mykyl myth,
þese parcellis in propyrtes we purpose us to playe
þis day seuenenyt, be-fore ȝou in syth,
At [ ] on þe grene, in ryall a-ray.
ȝe haste ȝou þanne þedyrward, syris, hendly in hyth,
All goode neyboris, ful specyaly we ȝou pray,
& loke þat ȝe be þere be-tyme, luffely & lyth,
for we schul be onward be vnderne of þe day.
dere Frendys,
we thanke ȝou of all good dalyaunce
& of all ȝoure specyal sportaunce,
& preye ȝou of good contynnaunce
to oure lyuys endys.

Primus Vexillator. Deus, oure lyuys we loue ȝou, þus takande oure leue.
ȝe manly men of [ ], þus Crist saue ȝou all!
he maynten ȝoure myrthis, & kepe ȝou fro greve,
þat born was of Mary myld in an ox stall.
Now, mercy be all [ ], & wel mote ȝe cheve!
All oure feythful frendys, þus fayre mote ȝe fall!
ȝa, & welcum be ȝe whanne ȝe com, prys for to preve,
& worthyi to be worchepyd in boure, & in hall,
& in euery place.
fare-wel, fayre frendys,
þat lofly wyl lystyn & lendis!
Cryste kepe ȝou fro fendis!
trumpe up, & lete vs pace!
