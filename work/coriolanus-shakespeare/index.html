Tragedy · c. 1608

The Tragedy of Coriolanus

by William Shakespeare

Caius Martius, the proudest soldier in Rome, wins the name Coriolanus by taking the Volscian city of Corioles, but his contempt for the common people makes him unfit for the consulship he is pressed to seek. Tricked and banished by the tribunes Sicinius and Brutus, he goes over to his old enemy Tullus Aufidius and marches on Rome at the head of a Volscian army. Only the pleading of his mother Volumnia, his wife Virgilia and his son turns him back, and the peace he makes costs him his life at Aufidius's hands.
