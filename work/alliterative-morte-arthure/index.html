Morte Arthure

The alliterative Morte Arthure

by Anonymous (Middle English)

An alliterative Middle English romance of about 4,350 long lines, preserved in Robert Thornton's manuscript, telling the end of King Arthur's reign: the Roman emperor Lucius demands tribute, Arthur crosses to the Continent and kills the giant of Mont-Saint-Michel, defeats the Romans and sets out for Rome, but is recalled by Mordred's treason, loses Gawain at the landing, and meets Mordred in a last battle before he is carried to Avalon. It is one of the finest English alliterative poems and a source for Malory.
