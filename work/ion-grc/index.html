Verse play · c. 413 BC

Ἴων (Ion)

by Euripides

At Delphi the foundling Ion serves as a temple attendant of Apollo, unaware that he is the child Kreousa once bore to the god and exposed in a cave beneath the Athenian Acropolis. When Kreousa and her childless husband Xouthos consult the oracle, Apollo's answer hands Ion to Xouthos as his son, and Kreousa, believing herself betrayed, sends a poisoned cup against the young man. The plot fails, and Ion, pursuing her to the altar, is stopped when the Pythia produces the basket and tokens in which he was found. Athena then appears to confirm the recognition and to foretell the Ionian descent of his line. The play is given here in Greek, in Nauck's Teubner text, so that it pairs with the English translations.
