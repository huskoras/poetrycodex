German

Heinrich Heine

1797–1856

Heinrich Heine (1797–1856) was born in Düsseldorf to a Jewish family and studied law at Bonn, Berlin and Göttingen, taking his degree in 1825 after converting to Protestant Christianity. His Book of Songs (Buch der Lieder, 1827), with its plain, musical lyrics of love and disappointment, made him one of the most widely read German poets, and many of its poems were set to music by Schumann, Schubert and Mendelssohn.

In 1831 Heine moved to Paris, where he lived for the rest of his life, writing travel pieces, criticism and satirical verse, among them Germany: A Winter's Tale and Atta Troll. His work was banned in several German states. From the mid-1840s he suffered from a progressive paralysis and spent his last years confined to what he called his mattress-grave, producing the Romancero (1851) and the late poems. The translations here are by Edgar Alfred Bowring (1908 edition of the complete poems, in the original metres).
