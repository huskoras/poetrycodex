Giles Fletcher the Elder

c. 1549–1611

Giles Fletcher the Elder (c. 1549–1611) was an English diplomat, civil servant and poet. Educated at Eton and King's College, Cambridge, he served in Parliament and as a commissioner abroad, and in 1588–89 was Queen Elizabeth's ambassador to Russia. His account of that embassy, Of the Russe Common Wealth (1591), was suppressed at first and later became a much-read description of Muscovy.

His sonnet sequence Licia, or Poemes of Love (1593), issued with a verse dedication, an ode, a dialogue from Lucian, a love-maze and three elegies, is a modest, conventional Elizabethan love cycle; it was printed together with a historical complaint, The Rising to the Crowne of Richard the third. He was the father of the poets Giles Fletcher the Younger and Phineas Fletcher, and uncle of the dramatist John Fletcher.
