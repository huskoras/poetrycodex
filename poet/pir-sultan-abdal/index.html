Turkish & Ottoman

Pir Sultan Abdal

16th century

Pir Sultan Abdal (his given name was Haydar) was an Alevi-Bektashi poet of the sixteenth century, born in the village of Banaz, in the district of Sivas. A leading figure among the Kızılbaş, the Anatolian devotees of Ali and the Twelve Imams, he was close in sympathy to the Safavid shah Tahmasb and took part in the political risings of his community. By tradition he was put to death at Sivas around 1560, on the order of the Ottoman governor Hızır Paşa.

He sang to the saz in the syllabic metre of the âşık tradition, in plain, vivid Turkish, and his nefes (devotional songs) praise Ali, Hüseyin and the Twelve Imams, mourn exile and separation, and call the lover to the way of the pir. Many songs circulate under his name and the attribution of single poems is often uncertain, since the work of poets such as Kul Himmet and Hatayî became mixed with his. The texts here are the eighteen nefes Sadettin Nüzhet Ergun printed in Bektaşî Şairleri ve Nefesleri (1930), with the oral-tradition text of his best-known song, Gurbet elde bir hâl geldi başıma.

Poems (19)

  1. Gurbet elde bir hal geldi başıma
  2. Çeke çeke ben bu derdden ölürüm
  3. Şu yalan dünyâya geldim geleli
  4. Hak Muhammed Ali geldi dilime
  5. Yemen ellerinden beri gelirken
  6. Derdim çoktur Kangısına yanayım
  7. Sabahtan cemâlin seyrân eyledim
  8. Seyrân ettim erenlerin demini
  9. Şecaatin varsa kalbinde sakla
  10. Muhammed Ali’nin kurduğu yoldur
  11. Hak içün kendini kurbân eyleyen
  12. Şu karşıki karlı dağı gördün mü
  13. Hey erenler bir müşkilim var benim
  14. Gidi Yezid bize Kızılbaş demiş
  15. Gözün açık ise gel gir katara
  16. Hey gaziler mürvet kaldı mı şunda
  17. Güzel âşık çevrimizi
  18. Serseri girme meydana
  19. Yol içinde yol ararsın
