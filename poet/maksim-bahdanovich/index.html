Maksim Bahdanovich

1891-1917

Maksim Bahdanovich (Maksim Adamavich Bahdanovich, 1891-1917) was born in Minsk and grew up in Nizhny Novgorod and Yaroslavl, where his family moved when he was a child. He wrote his first Belarusian verse in his teens and, from 1912, published regularly in the Vilnia newspaper Nasha Niva. He studied law at the Demidov Lyceum in Yaroslavl, wrote critical essays and translations (from Verlaine, Ovid and others) as well as poems, and is counted among the founders of modern Belarusian literature.

Wreath (Vianok), his only book to appear in his lifetime, was printed in Vilnia in 1914 and gathers his verse in cycles, among them In the Enchanted Kingdom, Echoes of the Fatherland, Old Belarus, City, and Old Inheritance, with the verse tale Veronika at its close. His strict forms (sonnets, triolets, rondeau, terza rima, octave) were rare in Belarusian poetry of the day. Consumptive from youth, he moved to Yalta in 1916 and died there on 25 May 1917, aged twenty-five. The posthumous poem Pahonia (The Chase), written in 1916, became one of the best-loved poems of the Belarusian national movement.
