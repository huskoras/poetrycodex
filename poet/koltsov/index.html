Russian & Slavic

Alexey Koltsov

1809-1842

Alexey Vasilyevich Koltsov (1809-1842) was a Russian poet born in Voronezh, the son of a cattle dealer. He had only a little formal schooling and spent most of his short life in his father's trade, travelling the southern steppe with herds. He began writing verse in his teens, and his talent was recognised by the Voronezh circle of Nikolay Stankevich and then by the critic Vissarion Belinsky, who helped bring out the first collection of his poems in 1835.

Koltsov's best work consists of songs (pesni) and meditative poems he called dumy, written in the rhythms and diction of Russian folk song and often without rhyme. They give voice to peasant labour, love and sorrow, the open steppe and the weight of fate, and they became the model for a whole tradition of Russian literary folk song. He died of consumption at thirty-three; many of his poems were set to music, and several, such as 'Ne shumi ty, rozh', passed into popular repertoire.
