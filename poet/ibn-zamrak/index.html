Ibn Zamrak

1333–c. 1393

Ibn Zamrak (ابن زمرك), Muhammad ibn Yusuf ibn Zamrak, was born in Granada in 1333 and became one of the leading court poets of the Nasrid kingdom. A pupil of the polymath Ibn al-Khatib, he served the sultans Muhammad V and his successors as secretary and minister, and he died about 1393.

His verses are carved into the walls of the Alhambra, among them the poems around the Fountain of the Lions and in the Hall of the Two Sisters. His surviving poems include long panegyric qasidas, elegies, love lyrics, descriptions of gardens and buildings, and muwashshahat. The texts here are the classical Arabic originals as transcribed on Arabic Wikisource.
