Attic Scolia (Anonymous)

6th–5th c. BC

The Attic scolia are the drinking-songs of Athenian banqueting, sung at the symposium by guests who passed a myrtle branch or a lyre from couch to couch, each taking up a short song in turn. They are anonymous, brief and sung from memory: hymns to Athena, Demeter, Leto and Pan, maxims about friendship and health, wishes to be a lyre or a jewel, and a warning about the scorpion under every stone.

The collection survives in the fifteenth book of Athenaeus, which quotes some two dozen of them in a fixed order. Its best-known members are the songs on Harmodius and Aristogeiton, the tyrannicides of 514 BC, one of which is traditionally ascribed to Callistratus; a few songs carry other names (Simonides, Alcaeus, Praxilla). The Greek is given here from Bergk’s Anthologia lyrica of 1868, collated with the 1904 Hiller-Crusius edition, with J. M. Edmonds’s English of 1927.
