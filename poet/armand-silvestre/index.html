Armand Silvestre

1837–1901

Armand Silvestre (Paul-Armand Silvestre, 1837–1901) was born in Paris, trained at the École polytechnique, and made his career in the civil service, rising to the post of inspector of fine arts, while writing poetry, criticism, opera libretti and a great many comic tales. He first appeared among the Parnassian poets in the second and third series of Le Parnasse contemporain, and his collections include Rimes neuves et vieilles (1866), Les Renaissances (1870), Le Pays des roses (1882) and Les Ailes d’or (1880).

His verse is lyrical and sensuous, given to the love-sonnet, to starlit and sunset imagery and to a pagan cult of beauty, and its smooth, singable quality drew composers: Gabriel Fauré and Jules Massenet, among others, set his poems to music. The comic tales he published under his own name made him better known to the general public than his poetry did. The poems here come from Le Pays des roses (1882) and from his contributions to Le Parnasse contemporain.
