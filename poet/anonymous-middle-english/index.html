Anonymous (Middle English)

c. 13th–16th century

Most of the short poems that survive from medieval England carry no author’s name. They are songs, hymns, carols, ballads and charms, copied by clerks, friars and minstrels onto the spare leaves of larger books, and they reach us through a handful of manuscripts: the Reading Abbey volume that preserves ‘Sumer is icumen in’, the Harley miscellany (MS Harley 2253) with its love-songs and spring-songs, the song-book Sloane 2593 with its carols, and the Balliol commonplace book of Richard Hill, which holds ‘Lully, lulley’.

The poems gathered here run from about 1225 to the edge of the sixteenth century, with one later record of a far older custom. They include some of the earliest English love-songs, the plain and piercing carols of the fifteenth century, early ballads such as ‘Judas’ and ‘Robyn and Gandelyn’, the ‘Erthe upon erthe’ tradition of the memento mori, and the Yorkshire ‘Lyke-Wake Dirge’. The spelling is Middle English as the editors printed it.
