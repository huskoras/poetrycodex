Ada Negri

1870–1945

Ada Negri was born at Lodi, in Lombardy, on 3 February 1870, into a poor family; her mother worked in a mill. She trained as an elementary teacher and taught in village schools near Lodi and Pavia, writing poems of hardship and protest in the evenings. Her first book, Fatalità (1892), made her famous almost at once as the poet of the Milanese poor and of women who work, and it led to her being taken up by the Milan publisher Treves.

Fatalità sets harsh scenes of the slum, the factory and the hospital beside songs of love, jealousy, storm and loneliness, in short regular stanzas and a voice that is often defiant and sometimes tender. Her next book, Tempeste, followed in 1896, and many more collections of verse and prose came after it. She died in Milan on 11 January 1945, having been the first woman admitted to the Accademia d'Italia.
