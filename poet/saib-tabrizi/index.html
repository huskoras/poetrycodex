Sa'ib Tabrizi

1601–1677

Sa'ib Tabrizi (صائب تبریزی, Mirza Muhammad Ali Sa'ib, 1601–1677) was a Persian poet of the Safavid age, born in Tabriz or Isfahan, and the most celebrated master of the 'Indian style' (sabk-e hendi). He spent several years in India in his youth, returned to Iran, and became poet laureate (malek al-sho'ara) at the court of Shah Abbas II. He died at Isfahan, where his tomb is still visited.

He is said to have composed well over a hundred thousand verses, almost all of them ghazals, prized for their fresh conceits, proverbial turns and the ease of their language. This selection takes his Persian ghazals from his Divan as given on Ganjoor, ranked by the number of recitations and linked songs recorded there. Each bayt stands on one line, the two hemistichs separated by a wide space. The Persian text is public domain.
