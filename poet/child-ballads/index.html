Traditional Ballads (Child collection)

traditional; texts recorded c. 13th–19th century

The English and Scottish Popular Ballads is the great scholarly collection of traditional ballads in English and Scots, edited by the Harvard professor Francis James Child (1825–1896) and published in ten parts between 1882 and 1898. Child gathered 305 ballads, each given a number that is still how scholars cite it, and printed every version he could find, lettered A, B, C and so on, from manuscripts, broadsides, printed miscellanies and oral tradition.

The ballads range from elfin knights, ghosts and riddling lovers to Border raids, Robin Hood and sea-fights. Their texts were written down between about the 13th and the 19th century, and most were sung long before that. This archive gives version A of every ballad, with later versions added where they tell the story in substantially different words, and keeps Child's titles and spellings; his headnotes, sources and variant readings are left out.
