Restoration & Augustan

Aphra Behn

1640–1689

Aphra Behn (1640–1689) was among the first Englishwomen to earn a living by her pen. Little is certain about her early life; she is said to have spent time in Surinam, and in 1666 she was sent to Antwerp as a spy for Charles II, writing under the code name Astrea. Back in London she turned to the stage, and from The Forc’d Marriage (1670) to The Rover (1677) and beyond she became one of the most successful playwrights of the Restoration, later adding prose fiction such as Oroonoko (1688). She was buried in the cloisters of Westminster Abbey.

Her verse was published in Poems upon Several Occasions (1684) and in A Miscellany (1685), and further pieces appeared after her death. It ranges from songs written for her plays, Pindaric odes and pastoral dialogues to satires, epistles to friends and patrons, and elegies for Rochester and Waller. This selection takes her own short and occasional poems from the sixth volume of Montague Summers’s edition (1915); her translations from Tallemant, A Voyage to the Isle of Love and Lycidus, are left out.
