Francisca Júlia

1871–1920

Francisca Júlia da Silva (Xiririca, now Eldorado, São Paulo, 1871 – São Paulo, 1920) was a Brazilian poet, among the foremost women writers of the Parnassian school in Brazil. She grew up in the interior of São Paulo state and made her name in the capital's newspapers and reviews in the 1890s.

Her first book, Mármores (São Paulo, 1895), with a preface by João Ribeiro, is her best-known work: chiselled sonnets and narrative pieces in the impersonal, sculptural manner of the Parnassians, opening with the manifesto-sonnet 'Musa impassivel', together with free adaptations of Goethe and Heine and a closing group of 'Ballada' narratives. She followed it with Esfinges (1903) and, with her brother Júlio César da Silva, the children's verse Alma infantil (1912). This archive holds the 1895 edition, in its original spelling.
