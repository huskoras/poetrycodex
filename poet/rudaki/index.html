Rudaki

c. 858–941

Rudaki (رودکی; Abu Abdullah Ja'far ibn Muhammad Rudaki, c. 858–941) was born in the village of Panjrud in the Rudak district near Samarkand, and is regarded as the first great poet of the New Persian language. He rose to fame at the court of the Samanid emir Nasr II at Bukhara, where he was a celebrated singer and harpist as well as a poet, and is said to have gone blind, whether from childhood or later. He died in his native region in 941.

Only a small part of his work survives: the great majority of the poems said to have run to many thousands of couplets is lost, and what remains is scattered through anthologies and old histories. This selection gives the qasidas and qit'as that Ganjoor collects as his Divan, among them the old ode in praise of wine and the celebrated lyric on the scent of the river Muliyan, together with his quatrains (ruba'is). Fragments of fewer than four couplets, and the few quatrains that Ganjoor prints with a line missing, are left out.
