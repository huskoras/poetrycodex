Li Ling

d. 74 BC

Li Ling (李陵, style name Shaoqing) was a general of the Western Han dynasty, grandson of the famous commander Li Guang. In 99 BC he led a small infantry force against the Xiongnu, was surrounded and, after resisting to the end, surrendered. Emperor Wu, enraged, had his family put to death, and Li Ling remained among the Xiongnu until his death in 74 BC. His birth year is not recorded; c. 134 BC is an approximation.

Three five-character poems addressed to his friend Su Wu, who had been held captive by the Xiongnu and was allowed to return home, are preserved in the Wenxuan (Selections of Refined Literature), together with a letter. The attribution of these poems to Li Ling is traditional and has long been doubted; many scholars regard them as later compositions in the style of the anonymous Han poems, so they are given here under his name on the authority of the anthology only. The text is taken from the Wikisource edition of Wenxuan with Li Shan's commentary, with the commentary and collation notes removed.
