Restoration & Augustan

Henry Jones

1721–1770

Henry Jones (1721–1770) was born at Beaulieu, near Drogheda in County Louth, and was brought up to the trade of bricklayer, which earned him the label "the bricklayer poet". He taught himself to write verse, and lines of his on the death of Pope won the notice of Lord Chief Justice Singleton, who lived at Beaulieu.

Through Singleton his verses reached the Earl of Chesterfield, then Lord Lieutenant of Ireland, who became his patron and took him to London in 1748. His Poems on Several Occasions (1749) was published by subscription, with Chesterfield's name at its head; his tragedy The Earl of Essex followed at Covent Garden in 1753. Later pieces, among them Merit, The Relief, The Royal Vision, Clifton and The Isle of Wight, are occasional and topographical poems. This selection omits the tragedy. Clifton and The Isle of Wight are given as separate works.
