Gérard de Nerval

1808–1855

Gérard de Nerval (1808–1855), born Gérard Labrunie in Paris, was a French Romantic poet, translator and prose writer. He made his name in his twenties with a French version of Goethe's Faust (1828), and wrote verse, travel narrative and fiction through the 1830s and 1840s, among them the Voyage en Orient (1851).

His best-known poems are the eight sonnets of Les Chimères, printed in 1854 at the end of Les Filles du feu, opening with "El Desdichado"; dense with myth and private symbol, they were later admired by the Symbolists and the Surrealists. Subject to periods of mental illness, he wrote Aurélia, an account of his visions, and was found dead in a Paris street in January 1855. The poems here are from French Wikisource, beginning with Les Chimères, followed by the Odelettes and his other verse.
