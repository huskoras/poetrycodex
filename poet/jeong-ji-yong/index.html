Korean

Jeong Ji-yong 정지용

1902–1950

Jeong Ji-yong (정지용 鄭芝溶, 1902–1950) was a Korean poet, born in Okcheon, North Chungcheong Province. He studied English literature at Doshisha University in Kyoto and later taught in Seoul, and he is counted among the founders of modern Korean poetry for his precise, image-driven lyrics.

His first collection, 정지용 시집 (Jeong Ji-yong sijip), was published by Simunhaksa in 1935 in five parts, with an afterword by Park Yong-chul. It gathers sea poems, poems of travel and Kyoto, children's songs and Catholic poems. He disappeared during the Korean War in 1950, and his death is traditionally given as that year. The texts here keep the 1935 orthography, including the ㅅ-genitive and old spellings, as transcribed on Korean Wikisource.
