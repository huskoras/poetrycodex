Medieval

William Langland

c. 1332–c. 1386

William Langland is the name traditionally given to the presumed author of Piers Plowman, one of the great alliterative poems of medieval England, on the strength of a fifteenth-century manuscript note naming a “William de Langlond” as its maker.

Little is known of his life beyond what can be inferred from the poem: he seems to have received a clerical education, lived for a time in London, and moved in the world of minor clergy and the poor rather than the court.

Piers Plowman survives in three successive versions (the A, B, and C texts), which Langland appears to have revised and expanded over some twenty years. It follows a dreamer, Will, through a series of visions in search of Do-well, Do-bet, and Do-best — a sweeping allegorical satire of fourteenth-century church and society.
