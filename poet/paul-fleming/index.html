German

Paul Fleming

1609–1640

Paul Fleming was born in 1609 in Hartenstein in Saxony and studied medicine at Leipzig, where he became a follower of Martin Opitz and the new German art of poetry. Leaving the Thirty Years' War behind him, he joined the embassy sent by Duke Frederick III of Holstein-Gottorf to Russia and Persia, and spent the years 1633 to 1639 on those long journeys.

He returned to Germany, took a medical doctorate at Leiden, and died at Hamburg in 1640, aged thirty. His German poems, among them sonnets, odes and occasional verse, were printed after his death as Teutsche Poemata (1642). The sonnet here, 'An sich', is the best known of them; the text follows Lappenberg's 1865 edition, with its spelling.
