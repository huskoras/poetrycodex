Mahmud Sami al-Barudi

1839–1904

محمود سامي البارودي (Maḥmūd Sāmī al-Bārūdī, 1839–1904) was born in Cairo into a family of Circassian origin and followed a military and political career. He served as an officer, held office under Khedive Ismāʻīl, and became Minister of War and then Prime Minister during the Urabi revolt of 1882. After the British defeat of the nationalists at Tel el-Kebir he was tried and exiled to Ceylon, where he spent some seventeen years, and was allowed to return to Egypt in 1899, blind in old age. He died in Cairo in 1904.

He is counted the father of the neoclassical revival of Arabic poetry (al-iḥyāʼ): having read deeply in the odes of the Abbasid masters, he restored their diction, forms and vigour to a poetry that had grown ornamental and weak, and he made it speak of his own life, of friendship, the homeland, love, war and exile, a path followed by Ahmad Shawqi and Hafiz Ibrahim. The poems given here are the longest in his divan as transcribed on Arabic Wikisource, in the original Arabic, each bayt (verse of two hemistichs) on one line; they include his poems of nostalgia in Ceylon, his elegies, and his odes of praise and of love.
