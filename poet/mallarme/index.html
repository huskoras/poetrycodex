French

Stéphane Mallarmé

1842–1898

Stéphane Mallarmé (1842–1898) was a French poet and critic, born in Paris. For most of his working life he taught English in provincial and Parisian lycées, and from the 1880s he received writers and artists at his Tuesday evenings (les mardis) in the rue de Rome. He is the central figure of French Symbolism.

His poems are few, dense and syntactically daring. L’Après-midi d’un faune (1876) later inspired Debussy’s orchestral Prélude, and Un coup de dés (1897) experimented with the printed page itself. He died at Valvins in 1898; the Poésies he had prepared appeared posthumously in Brussels in 1899. The texts here follow that edition, as transcribed by Wikisource (CC BY-SA).
