Dutch & Flemish

Jacques Perk

1859–1881

Jacques Perk (Jacques Fabrice Herman Perk, 1859–1881) was a Dutch poet, born in Batavia in the Dutch East Indies and raised in Haarlem and Amsterdam. He died of typhoid fever at the age of twenty-two.

His one work, the sonnet cycle Mathilde, written in 1880 and published after his death in 1882, was championed by Willem Kloos and Lodewijk van Deyssel and became a founding text of the Tachtigers, the Dutch literary movement of the 1880s. The texts here are from the thirteenth printing of his Gedichten (Amsterdam, S. L. van Looy, 1916), beginning with the first sixty sonnets of the cycle.
