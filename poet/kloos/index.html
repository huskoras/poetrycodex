Dutch & Flemish

Willem Kloos

1859–1938

Willem Kloos (Amsterdam, 1859 – The Hague, 1938) was the most uncompromising voice of the Tachtigers, the Movement of Eighty that renewed Dutch literature in the 1880s. In 1885 he helped to found the journal De Nieuwe Gids, and as its critic he demanded that poetry be the most individual expression of the most individual emotion.

His sonnets, many of them written in the 1880s and gathered in Verzen, are intense, self-absorbed meditations on love, pride, solitude and death, in a language of hyphenated compounds and charged adjectives. After Jacques Perk's early death he became the guardian of Perk's work, and from 1893 he led De Nieuwe Gids as editor-in-chief.

  17. Ik hield den spiegel van uw Zonden op
