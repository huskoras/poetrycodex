Frederik Paludan-Müller

1809–1876

Frederik Paludan-Müller (1809–1876) was a Danish poet, born at Kerteminde on the island of Funen. He made his name with the verse drama The Dancer (Danserinden, 1832), and his later work includes the narrative poem Adam Homo (1841–48), a long satirical verse novel of a man's worldly rise and moral decline, and the dramatic poem Ahasverus, the Eternal Jew (1853).

He is counted among the leading Danish poets of the Golden Age, remembered for his command of rhyme and stanza and for the religious and moral seriousness of his later writing. The poems here are English versions from A Book of Danish Verse (1922), edited by Oluf Friis, in the translations of S. Foster Damon and Robert Silliman Hillyer.
