Jovan Dučić

1871–1943

Jovan Dučić (Јован Дучић) was a Serbian poet and diplomat, born in Trebinje in Herzegovina. His year of birth is disputed: sources give both 1871 and 1874 (the Serbian Wikisource author page has 1874), while the date of his death, 7 April 1943, is not in doubt. He studied in Mostar and Sarajevo and then in Geneva and Paris, where he absorbed the French Parnassian and Symbolist poets, and he entered the Yugoslav foreign service, representing his country in a number of European capitals.

His first book, Pjesme (1901), made him a leading voice of the Serbian lyric, and his mature work is marked by formal discipline, sonnet and quatrain forms, and a cool, meditative treatment of sunsets, the sea, silence, love and death. Later collections include Pesme sunca and Pesme ljubavi i smrti (both 1929) and the Carski soneti (1930); he also wrote essays and the prose poems of Plave legende. He died in exile in Gary, Indiana, and his remains were returned to Trebinje in 2000.
