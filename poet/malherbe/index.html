French

François de Malherbe

1555–1628

François de Malherbe (1555–1628), born at Caen in Normandy, spent his early years as a provincial gentleman and soldier before his poetry brought him to the court of Henri IV in the first years of the seventeenth century. He went on to serve as court poet to Henri IV and then to Marie de Médicis and Louis XIII, writing odes and stances for public occasions, consolations, sonnets and short pieces for court entertainments.

He is remembered above all as a reformer of French verse and diction, valued for clarity, strict metre and rhyme, and the refusal of the ornate manner of the preceding generation, and his Consolation à M. du Perier (1599), with its line about the rose that lived but a morning, remains his best-known poem. The poems here follow Prosper Blanchemain's 1897 edition of his Œuvres poétiques, as transcribed on French Wikisource, with the printed spelling kept.
