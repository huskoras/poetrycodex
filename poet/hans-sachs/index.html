German

Hans Sachs

1494–1576

Hans Sachs (1494–1576) was a shoemaker of Nuremberg and the most prolific German poet of the sixteenth century: a master of the Nuremberg Meistersinger guild who wrote some 6,000 pieces, among them Meisterlieder, rhymed Spruchgedichte, fables, Schwänke, and plays for Shrovetide and the stage.

He was an early and public supporter of Luther, whose cause he served in the verse allegory The Wittenberg Nightingale (1523). The poems here are given in the spelling of their old prints and manuscripts, with the printed virgules kept: a Meistersang of 1532 on the legend of the female pope, and the verses of the Ständebuch (Frankfurt, 1568), in which each trade and estate of the age, from the Pope to the Schalcksnarr, speaks for itself beneath a woodcut by Jost Amman.
