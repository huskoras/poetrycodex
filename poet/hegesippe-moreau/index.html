Hégésippe Moreau

1810–1838

Hégésippe Moreau (born Pierre-Jacques Roulliot, 1810–1838) was a French poet and song-writer, born in Paris. He was brought up at Provins and trained there as a printer’s apprentice, later working in the printing trade in Paris.

A liberal of republican sympathies and an admirer of Béranger, he wrote political verse and songs of the years after the July Revolution of 1830, and tender elegies of his childhood, above all La Voulzie, named for the little stream near Provins. His life was one of poverty and illness.

He died at the Charité hospital in Paris in December 1838, aged twenty-eight, the year his book Le Myosotis appeared. This selection of the verse follows the posthumous Œuvres (Garnier, 1864), as transcribed on French Wikisource, and leaves out the prose tales, letters and editors’ notices.
