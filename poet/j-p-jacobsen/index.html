J. P. Jacobsen

1847–1885

Jens Peter Jacobsen (1847-1885) was a Danish novelist, poet and botanist, born in Thisted in Jutland. Trained as a scientist, he was among the first Danish translators of Darwin, and he won a university gold medal for a study of the desmids. He turned to fiction with the story Mogens (1882) and the novels Fru Marie Grubbe (1876) and Niels Lyhne (1880), works of the Modern Breakthrough that Rilke and many others later admired. He died of tuberculosis at thirty-eight.

His poetry is small in bulk and was mostly left unpublished in his lifetime; it was collected after his death in Digte og Udkast (1886), edited by Edvard Brandes and Vilhelm Møller. The best known are the Gurresange, songs of King Valdemar and his murdered love Tove, which Arnold Schoenberg set in his Gurrelieder. The English versions here come from A Book of Danish Verse (1922), translated by S. Foster Damon and Robert Silliman Hillyer; the Danish originals follow Kalliope's transcription of the 1886 edition.
