Hungarian

Endre Ady

1877–1919

Endre Ady (1877–1919) was a Hungarian poet and journalist, the central voice of the Nyugat ("West") generation and of Hungarian modernism. Born in Érmindszent, in the Szilágy region of what was then Hungary, he trained as a lawyer, worked as a newspaper journalist in Nagyvárad and Budapest, and spent long periods in Paris, where he came under the influence of French Symbolism.

His collection Új versek ("New Poems", 1906) broke with the folk-song manner of nineteenth-century Hungarian verse, and was followed by Vér és arany (1907), Az Illés szekerén (1908), Szeretném, ha szeretnének (1909), A Minden-Titkok versei (1910) and A menekülő Élet (1912). Love poems for "Léda", visionary religious verse and sharp poems of national self-reproach stand side by side in these books. He died in Budapest in 1919. This selection takes the poems of the first five books whose Hungarian Wikisource transcriptions are rated 75% or better for text quality, together with A menekülő Élet from Project Gutenberg.
