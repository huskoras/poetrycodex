Colin Muset

fl. c. 1230–1250

Colin Muset (fl. c. 1230–1250) was a trouvère of Champagne and Lorraine, a professional minstrel who sang and played the vielle in the halls of the nobility. Nothing is known of his life beyond what his songs say; the name Muset ("piper") is probably a nickname, and he appears to have served lords in Champagne, Lorraine and Burgundy, among them the count whom he addresses in his best-known song.

His songs are remarkable for speaking in the first person about the minstrel's trade: the pay he is owed, the generosity or meanness of patrons, and the comforts of home, with its roast capons, good wine and a wife who is glad to see him come back with a full purse. Alongside these he wrote courtly love songs, a tenson with Jacques d'Amiens and a satire on overbearing wives and nurses. The fifteen songs here follow Joseph Bédier's 1912 edition; his introduction, notes, variants and the musical transcriptions are not reproduced.
