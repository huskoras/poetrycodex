Yü Ch'ien

1398–1457

Yü Ch'ien (Yu Qian, 于謙, 1398–1457), courtesy name T'ing-i, was born in Ch'ien-t'ang (modern Hangzhou) and rose to be Minister of War under the Ming dynasty. In 1449, when the Emperor Zhengtong was captured by the Mongols at Tumu and their army advanced on Beijing, he organised the city's defence and helped place a new emperor on the throne.

After the captured emperor returned and regained the throne in 1457, Yü Ch'ien was executed on a charge of treason; he was later rehabilitated and honoured as a model of integrity. His best-known poem, on lime, is a short statement of unspotted loyalty that Chinese schoolchildren still learn; the rest of his verse is mostly plain, clear lyric of the seasons and of women's longing.
