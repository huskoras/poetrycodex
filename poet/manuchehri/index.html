Manuchehri Damghani

d. c. 1040

Manuchehri Damghani (منوچهری دامغانی, Abu'l-Najm Ahmad ibn Qus) was a Persian court poet of the Ghaznavid age, born at Damghan in the late tenth century and dead around 1040. He is said to have taken his pen-name from his first patron, the Ziyarid prince Manuchehr, before serving at the court of the Ghaznavid sultan Mas'ud (r. 1031-1041). Dates are approximate.

He is remembered above all for his vivid descriptions of nature, of seasons, gardens, rain, wine and the festivals of Nowruz and Mehregan, and as the poet who gave the musammat, a stanzaic form with a refrain, its place in Persian verse. His Divan, from which the poems here are taken, consists of qasidas, qita and musammats, many of them panegyrics for the sultan and his courtiers.
