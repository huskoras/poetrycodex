Milan Rakić

1876-1938

Milan Rakić (Belgrade, 1876 – Zagreb, 1938) was a Serbian poet and diplomat, and the leading Parnassian voice of Serbian poetry in the early twentieth century. Schooled in French verse, he prized clarity, restraint and exact form, and his stanzas keep the polished rhyme and measured tone of Leconte de Lisle and the French Parnassians while turning on a melancholy of his own.

He published Песме (Poems) in 1903 and Нове песме (New Poems) in 1912, and later became a member of the Serbian Royal Academy. Serving as a diplomat in the Ottoman lands, he wrote the Kosovo poems for which he is best remembered, among them “Симонида” (on the fresco of Queen Simonida at Gračanica), “Јефимија” and “На Газиместану”, which join personal grief to the memory of the medieval Serbian state; his “Долап” (The Water-Wheel) stands among his best-known meditations on human fate.
