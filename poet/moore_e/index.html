Edward Moore

1712–1757

Edward Moore (1712-1757) was born at Abingdon, Berkshire, the son of a dissenting minister. He was apprenticed to a linen-draper and set up in trade in London, but his business failed and he turned to writing. His Fables for the Ladies (1744), light verse apologues on love, vanity and the manners of the town, won him wide popularity and a long afterlife in the anthologies.

Moore also wrote for the stage, notably the comedy The Foundling (1748) and the domestic tragedy The Gamester (1753), and he conducted the periodical The World (1753-56) with Lord Chesterfield and other wits. This selection follows the verse in the 1782 Bell's Poets edition: the sixteen Fables, two Odes, the satirical and occasional pieces of the Miscellanies, and the Songs.
