Giuseppe Giusti

1809–1850

Giuseppe Giusti (1809–1850) was born at Monsummano in Tuscany and studied law at Pisa, though he never made a career of it. He became the best-known Italian satirical poet of the Risorgimento: short, racy poems in the speech of Tuscan streets, aimed at petty officials, courtiers, pedants, reactionary clergy and the foreign rulers of the peninsula, which circulated widely in manuscript and in pirated editions before and after the political thaw of the 1840s. He died in Florence in 1850.

The forty poems here are those that Giusti himself published or acknowledged during his lifetime, in the order of the posthumous collection Versi editi ed inediti (Florence: Le Monnier, 1852), whose own bibliographical note records which pieces he allowed into print; the unpublished pieces of that volume are left out. The text is the Wikisource transcription of that edition, in its printed spelling. A satire on a pedantic neighbour, “I Brindisi”, which is set in a prose letter, is not included.
