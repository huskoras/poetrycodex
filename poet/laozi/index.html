Laozi (Lao Tzu)

traditionally 6th century BC

Laozi, "the Old Master", is the traditional author of the Tao Te Ching (Daodejing), the foundational book of Daoism. Chinese tradition places him in the 6th century BC and calls him an archivist at the court of Zhou, but nothing of his life can be established, and scholars disagree about whether he existed as one person or whether the book was assembled over generations. The attribution of the work to him is traditional.

The book is a collection of eighty-one short chapters on the Tao (the Way), on government, and on the conduct of the sage, written in a terse and largely rhymed Chinese. The text here is James Legge's translation, published in 1891 in the Sacred Books of the East (vol. 39) as the Tao Teh King. It is in prose, with Legge's occasional rhymed renderings of the verse passages; the words in parentheses are his own supplied additions.
