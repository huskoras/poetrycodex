Ancient Greek & Roman

Procopius

c. 500–c. 565

Procopius was born at Caesarea in Palestine around the year 500 and was trained as a rhetorician and lawyer. In 527 he became secretary and adviser to the general Belisarius, and in that capacity he accompanied him on campaigns against the Persians, the Vandals in Africa and the Goths in Italy.

He is the principal historian of the reign of the Emperor Justinian. His History of the Wars, in eight books, covers the Persian, Vandal and Gothic wars down to 552 or 553, and is followed by the Buildings, a panegyric on Justinian's construction works. The Secret History, a hostile account of the emperor, his wife Theodora and Belisarius, is also attributed to him.

The date of his death is unknown; he is last securely attested in the 550s, and he may have lived into the 560s. The text on this site is the English translation of H. B. Dewing, published in the Loeb Classical Library in 1914.
