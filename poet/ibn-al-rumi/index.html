Ibn al-Rumi

836–896

ابن الرومي (علي بن العباس بن جريج، 836–896) (Ibn al-Rumi, ʻAli ibn al-ʻAbbas ibn Jurayj, 836–896) was born in Baghdad, the son of a Greek (Rumi) father and a Persian mother, and lived there under the Abbasid caliphs. He is remembered as one of the most inventive poets of the ninth century: a master of the long, closely argued qasida, and of satire, elegy, praise-poems, descriptions and bitter lampoons, who followed a single theme through every turn of its imagery.

The poems given here are drawn from his Diwan in the vocalised Arabic transcription of Arabic Wikisource, which is credited as the source of the text. They are the longest poems of the Wikisource category that could be read cleanly as lines of two hemistichs; each bayt (verse line of two hemistichs) stands on one line.
