Sheikh Farid

1173–1266

Sheikh Farid (ਸ਼ੇਖ਼ ਫ਼ਰੀਦ, 1173–1266), known in full as Baba Farid ud-Din Mas'ud Ganjshakar, was a Sufi saint of the Chishti order and one of the earliest poets of the Punjabi language. Born near Multan and a disciple of Khwaja Qutb ud-Din Bakhtiyar Kaki in Delhi, he settled at Ajodhan, now Pakpattan, where he taught until his death. He is revered across South Asia by Muslims, Sikhs and Hindus alike.

His Punjabi verse reached the Sikh scripture through a line of his successors and was entered by Guru Arjan in the Adi Granth (1604), the Sri Guru Granth Sahib: four shabads, in the ragas Asa and Suhi, and a sequence of saloks on mortality, patience, the transience of worldly things and longing for the Beloved. The texts here come from the open BaniDB database, in the Gurmukhi script. The salok sequence in the Granth also holds verses by the Sikh Gurus, printed there as commentary; those are left out, and each salok keeps its number in the sequence.
