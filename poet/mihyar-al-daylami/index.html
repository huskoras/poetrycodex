Mihyar al-Daylami

d. 1037

Mihyar al-Daylami (مهيار الديلمي, d. 1037) was a Persian from the Daylam region who was born a Zoroastrian and converted to Islam, becoming one of the leading Arabic poets of eleventh-century Baghdad. His birth year is unknown; the date 970 is used here only to place him in order among the poets of the archive.

He studied under the great poet al-Sharif al-Radi, whom he mourned in many elegies, and wrote court panegyrics for the Buyid viziers and the Abbasid caliph. His qasidas, on the classical model, open with long nasibs of desert lament and love, and they are collected in his Diwan. This selection gathers the longest poems of his Diwan as transcribed on Arabic Wikisource.
