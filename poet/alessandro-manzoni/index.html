Italian

Alessandro Manzoni

1785–1873

Alessandro Manzoni (1785–1873) was born in Milan and became the foremost Italian writer of the Romantic age. After years in Paris and a return to the Catholic faith around 1810, he began the Inni sacri, a cycle of hymns on the festivals of the Church year, which he published from 1812 to 1822.

His odes Marzo 1821, written on the Piedmontese rising of that year, and Il cinque maggio, on the death of Napoleon, are among the best-known patriotic poems in Italian. The choruses of his verse tragedies Il conte di Carmagnola (1820) and Adelchi (1822) are often read as independent lyrics. His novel I promessi sposi (1827; revised 1840–42) became a cornerstone of modern Italian prose. He was made a senator of the Kingdom of Italy in 1860 and died in Milan.
