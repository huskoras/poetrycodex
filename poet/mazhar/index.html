Indian

Mazhar Jan-i Janan

d. 1780 (per the source)

Mazhar of Delhi, of a family originally from Bokhara, is described in the book as learned in jurisprudence as well as poetry, with many favourite poets among his pupils, a Sunni who professed spiritual poverty and was reputed able to work miracles. The book says he was killed by a fanatic because he disagreed with the Shiah mourning for the death of Hussein, and died in 1780, aged nearly a hundred years.

The 5 pieces given here are drawn from Hindustani Lyrics (London: The Sufi Publishing Society, 1919), in the English verse of Inayat Khan and Jessie Duncan Westbrook. They are free renderings, not literal translations, and the original Urdu poems are untitled; each is named here by its first line.

Poems (5)

  1. No fault is thine, Beloved, I do not blame thee
  2. What thou hast done, never an enemy
  3. Mayhap my sorrowful heart
  4. She lightly laughed--And so is Mazhar dead?
  5. If I behold her, I am mad
