Jean de La Fontaine

1621–1695

Jean de La Fontaine (1621–1695) was a French poet born at Château-Thierry in Champagne. After an unsettled early career in law and the administration of forests and waters, he found patrons in Paris, among them Nicolas Fouquet and later Madame de La Sablière, and became one of the glories of the age of Louis XIV; he was elected to the Académie française in 1684.

His Fables, published in collected books between 1668 and 1694, retell the stories of Aesop, Phaedrus and the Indian tradition in supple, irregular verse of great wit and economy. Under the guise of animals they observe the manners, vanities and cruelties of court and town. This selection follows the English verse translation by Walter Thornbury (London, 1886), printed with Gustave Doré's illustrations.
