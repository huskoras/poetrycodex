Umar ibn Abi Rabi'a

644–c. 712

Umar ibn Abi Rabi'a (عمر بن أبي ربيعة) was born in 644 into a wealthy family of the Makhzum clan of Quraysh at Mecca. Unlike most Arab poets of his age he wrote almost nothing but love poetry, and he is regarded as the foremost master of the urban ghazal of the Umayyad period. The biographer Ibn Khallikan called him the best poet the tribe of Quraysh ever produced.

His verses take the pilgrimage season at Mecca and Medina as their stage: he meets noble women among the pilgrims, plays out brief encounters and exchanges with them in dialogue and narrative, and often presents himself as the one who is courted rather than the courtier. Nu'm, Hind, Zaynab, Thurayya and 'Uthayma are among the names that recur. The date of his death is uncertain; it is placed around 712.

This selection gives his poems from the diwan on Arabic Wikisource, beginning with the long ode 'A-min Al Nu'm anta ghadin fa-mubkir', in order of length (the longest first). Each verse is printed on one line with its two hemistichs apart, and the text keeps the vowel marks of that source, which has scattered slips of its own. Fragments of under four verses are left out.
