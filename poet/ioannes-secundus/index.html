Ioannes Secundus

1511–1536

Janus Secundus (Jan Nicolaas Everaerts, also called Joannes Secundus; 1511-1536) was a Netherlandish Neo-Latin poet, born at The Hague, the son of Nicolaas Everaerts, a senior judge and president of the Council of Holland. He studied law in France, served for a time in Spain as secretary to the archbishop of Toledo, and died young, at about twenty-five, at the abbey of Saint-Amand near Tournai.

His fame rests on the Basia ("Kisses"), nineteen short Latin poems to a mistress he calls Neaera, modelled on Catullus's kiss-poems and printed after his death. They were imitated by many later poets in Latin and in the vernaculars, and were translated into English by John Nott (1775; revised 1812). This archive carries the nineteen Basia in the Latin of the 1812 edition and in Nott's English verse.
