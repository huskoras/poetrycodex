Russian & Slavic

Marina Tsvetaeva

1892–1941

Марина Ивановна Цветаева (1892–1941), born in Moscow, the daughter of the art historian Ivan Tsvetaev and the pianist Maria Meyn, published her first book, Вечерний альбом (“Evening Album”), at her own expense in 1910, when she was eighteen. Волшебный фонарь (“The Magic Lantern”, 1912) and the selection Из двух книг (1913) followed; in 1912 she married Sergey Efron, and her early verse is full of childhood, family rooms, books and young love.

After the revolution she stayed in Moscow, where the poems collected in Вёрсты (“Versts”, 1921–1922) were written in 1916–1920, and in 1922 she left Russia to join her husband, living in Berlin, Prague and, from 1925, Paris. Ремесло and Психея (both 1923) and После России (1928) are the later books published in her lifetime. She returned to the Soviet Union in 1939 and died in Yelabuga on 31 August 1941. This selection draws on the books she published herself, in the texts held by Russian Wikisource.
