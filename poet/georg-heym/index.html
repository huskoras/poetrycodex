German

Georg Heym

1887–1912

Georg Heym (1887–1912) was born in Hirschberg in Silesia and studied law in Würzburg, Jena and Berlin. He belonged to the Berlin circle of young writers around the Neuer Club, where he read his poems in 1910 and 1911, and he is counted among the first voices of German Expressionism.

His only book published in his lifetime, Der ewige Tag, appeared with Rowohlt in Leipzig in 1911. Its poems turn on the great city, war and revolution, the drowned and the dead, and a sky heavy with omens. Heym drowned in the Havel near Berlin on 16 January 1912, trying to rescue a friend who had fallen through the ice; he was twenty-four. The poems here are those of Der ewige Tag in book order, from the transcription on German Wikisource.
