Yi Sang-hwa 이상화

1901–1943

Yi Sang-hwa (이상화, 1901–1943) was a Korean poet born in Daegu. He made his debut in 1922 in the literary magazine Baekjo (White Tide) and became one of the leading voices of Korean poetry under Japanese rule, moving from the feverish, decadent romanticism of his early work toward poems of national feeling and social protest.

His best-known poem, "Will Spring Come to Stolen Fields?" (1926), laments the loss of the homeland in the colonial period. He wrote relatively little and died in Daegu in 1943. The texts here follow the first magazine and newspaper printings as transcribed on Korean Wikisource, with the original spelling and old-hangul letters kept.
