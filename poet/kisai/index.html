Kisa'i of Merv

953 – after 1003

Kisa'i of Merv (کسایی مروزی; Majd al-Din Abu'l-Hasan or Abu Ishaq Kisa'i) was born at Merv in 341 AH (953 CE). He began as a court poet in the late Samanid and early Ghaznavid age, composing praise of the rulers of his day, but in later life he repented of panegyric and turned to ascetic, devotional verse. His poems show him to have been a Shi'i, and they praise 'Ali and the family of the Prophet.

Only some two hundred couplets of his poetry survive, preserved in anthologies and dictionaries and gathered into a modern collection, which Ganjoor presents here. They include an elegy, short descriptive pieces on spring, wine and flowers, and meditations on old age and death. His death is placed shortly after 394 AH (1003/4 CE).
