German

Georg Trakl

1887–1914

Georg Trakl (1887–1914) was born in Salzburg, the son of a hardware dealer, and trained as a pharmacist. His first poems appeared in local papers and, from 1912, in Der Brenner, the Innsbruck review edited by Ludwig von Ficker, who became his closest literary friend and supporter.

His collection Gedichte appeared in 1913 with the Kurt Wolff Verlag, and Sebastian im Traum followed in 1915, after his death. Trakl is counted among the chief voices of German Expressionism: his short, colour-saturated lines of blue, silver and purple, his images of decay, autumn and twilight, and his recurring figures of the sister, the boy Elis and the wanderer make a poetry of dream and dissolution that few have imitated.

In the First World War he served as a medical pharmacist in the Austrian army. After the battle of Grodek in Galicia in September 1914 he was left to tend the wounded, suffered a breakdown, and was taken to the military hospital in Kraków, where he died on 3 November 1914, aged 27, of an overdose of cocaine. The poems here are taken from Die Dichtungen (1919), the first collected edition, arranged by Karl Röck and published by Kurt Wolff in Leipzig.
