German

Paul Heyse

1830–1914

Paul Heyse (1830–1914) was born in Berlin, the son of the philologist Karl Wilhelm Ludwig Heyse, and studied in Berlin and Bonn. A journey to Italy in his early twenties gave him the country and its folk songs as a lifelong subject. In 1854 King Maximilian II of Bavaria called him to Munich, where he lived for the rest of his life and became the centre of a circle of writers.

He was prolific in every form: novellas, novels, plays and a great deal of verse, and he was a gifted translator of Italian and Spanish poetry. His short stories won him the widest fame; his Gedichte went through many editions and range from songs and love lyrics to Italian sketches and the epigrammatic Sprüche gathered at the end of the volume. In 1910 he received the Nobel Prize in Literature, the first German writer to do so. The poems here are taken from the fourth edition of the Gedichte (Berlin, 1889), checked against the seventh (1901).
