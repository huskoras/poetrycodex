Anonymous (Man'yōshū)

7th–8th century

These are the unattributed poems of the Man'yōshū (万葉集, "Collection of Ten Thousand Leaves"), the oldest anthology of Japanese poetry, compiled in its present form around 759 and drawing on poems of the 7th and 8th centuries. Many of its poems are signed by named courtiers, but a large share of the twenty books carries no composer: love songs, travel poems, laments and verses of the seasons whose makers are unknown or unrecorded.

The group also includes poems from two famous sections of the anthology. The Azuma-uta (東歌, Eastland songs) of Book 14 are folk-flavoured verses from the eastern provinces, many in the local speech of the region; the sakimori-uta (防人歌, frontier-guard songs) are the poems of conscripts from the east sent to defend northern Kyushu, and of their families. Only poems whose headnote and left-note name no composer are gathered here; the selection follows the poem numbers (Kokka taikan numbering) discussed in Saitō Mokichi's Man'yō shūka (1938), and the text is that of the 1891 Kogi reading text of Kamochi Masazumi, as transcribed on Japanese Wikisource, with its reading-aids removed.
