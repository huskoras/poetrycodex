Olavo Bilac

1865–1918

Olavo Brás Martins dos Guimarães Bilac (1865–1918) was born in Rio de Janeiro and became the best-known poet of Brazilian Parnassianism. He abandoned medical and legal studies for journalism and verse, and wrote as a chronicler and critic as well as a poet. Poesias, first collected in 1888 and revised and enlarged in later editions, gathers the books on which his reputation rests, among them Panóplias, Via Láctea, Sarças de fogo and Alma inquieta.

In 1907 a Rio magazine named him the "Prince of Brazilian Poets". He helped to found the Academia Brasileira de Letras and campaigned for civic education and military service in his later years. His sonnet sequence Via Láctea, with its lines on hearing and understanding the stars, is among the most quoted poems in the Portuguese language. The texts here follow the Wikisource transcription of the second edition of Poesias (Garnier, Rio de Janeiro, 1902), in the modern spelling of that transcription.
