Jean-Antoine de Baïf

1532–1589

Jean-Antoine de Baïf (1532–1589) was a French poet of the Pléiade, born in Venice, the natural son of the diplomat and humanist Lazare de Baïf. He was educated in Paris under the Hellenist Jean Dorat, with Ronsard as a fellow pupil at the Collège de Coqueret, and wrote love sonnets to Méline and Francine, as well as songs, eclogues, translations and a long collection of moral epigrams, the Mimes.

He is best remembered for his experiments with French verse measured by long and short syllables on the classical model, and for founding, with the musician Joachim Thibault de Courville, the Académie de poésie et de musique in 1570, which sought to unite verse and music. He died in Paris in 1589.
