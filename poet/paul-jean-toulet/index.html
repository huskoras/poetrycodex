Paul-Jean Toulet

1867–1920

Paul-Jean Toulet (1867–1920) was a French poet and novelist, born in Pau and raised partly in Mauritius, where his mother's family lived. After an unfinished law course he settled in Paris, where he belonged to the literary circles of the Left Bank and the cafés, and he travelled to the Indian Ocean, Indochina and Algeria. His novels, among them Monsieur du Paur, homme public and La Jeune Fille verte, are urbane and ironic; he also wrote the Dixains and the Chansons that appear beside his best-known verse.

Les Contrerimes appeared in 1921, the year after his death in Guéthary, and gave its name to a form Toulet invented: short stanzas whose rhymes are crossed against the line lengths, so that the rhyme arrives where the ear does not expect it. The book gathers the Contrerimes proper, the Chansons, the Dixains and the Coples (loose Alexandrine couplets and quatrains). Admirers have included Paul Valéry and Francis Carco, and his tone, at once offhand and elegiac, was widely imitated. The texts here follow the 1929 edition published by Émile-Paul frères, as proofread on French Wikisource.
