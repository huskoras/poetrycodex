Russian & Slavic

Sergei Esenin

1895–1925

Сергей Александрович Есенин (1895–1925), known in English as Sergei Esenin (or Yesenin), was a Russian poet born into a peasant family in the village of Konstantinovo in the Ryazan province. He moved to Moscow in 1912 and to Petrograd in 1915, where the poets Alexander Blok and Nikolai Klyuev took notice of him, and his first book, Радуница (“Radunitsa”), appeared in 1916. He became the best-known voice of the peasant poets and later belonged to the Imaginist circle; he died in Leningrad on 28 December 1925.

This selection gathers the lyric poems of 1910–1918 that were printed in his lifetime, in the order in which Wikisource lists them: folk-styled songs, village landscapes, the religious imagery of the Rus’ of his youth, and the verse of the First World War years. The texts are taken from Wikisource, which follows the collected works of 1995–2002; one poem is given in the pre-reform spelling of its early printing. The titles are followed by a plain transliteration, and poems without a title are named by their first line.
