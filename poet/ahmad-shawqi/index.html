Ahmad Shawqi

1868–1932

أحمد شوقي (Aḥmad Shawqī, 1868–1932) was born in Cairo and educated in law; he studied in France, at Montpellier and Paris, before returning to serve as court poet to the Khedive ʻAbbās Ḥilmī II. He was exiled to Spain during the First World War and came home in 1919. In 1927 Egyptian and Arab poets acclaimed him Amīr al-Shuʻarāʼ, the Prince of Poets, and he is generally counted the foremost poet of the Arabic neoclassical revival (al-iḥyāʼ), which took the odes of the Abbasid masters as its model while turning them to modern public themes.

The poems given here are taken from his collected verse, al-Shawqiyyāt, in the Arabic transcription of Arabic Wikisource, which is credited as the source of the text; each bayt (verse line of two hemistichs) stands on one line. They include political and occasional odes, elegies, love lyrics, and the verse fables for children modelled on La Fontaine. His verse plays and prose are not included.
