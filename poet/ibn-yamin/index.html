Ibn Yamin of Faryumad

1286–1368

Ibn Yamin of Faryumad (ابن یمین فریومدی, Amir Fakhr al-Din Mahmud, 1286–1368) was a Persian poet born at Faryumad, a village near Sabzavar in Khorasan, the son of the poet and chancery clerk Amir Yamin al-Din Tughra'i. He wrote court poetry in the troubled years after the fall of the Ilkhanids and was attached to the Sarbadar rulers of Sabzavar, to whom several of his qasidas are addressed. He died in 1368.

He is remembered above all as the master of the Persian qit'a, the short fragment of ethical and occasional verse, of which his Divan holds some hundreds. In them he turns, with plain speech and a worldly humour, to contentment with little, the dignity of the free man, the dangers of serving the great and the folly of hoarding. This selection gives forty of his qit'as and the first ten qasidas of his Divan, in the order of the Ganjoor edition.
