Hungarian

Mihály Tompa

1817–1868

Mihály Tompa (1817–1868) was born in Rimaszombat (now Rimavská Sobota) and became a Calvinist pastor in the Gömör region, serving at Beje and then at Hanva, where he died. He was a leading voice of the Hungarian popular-national school, a friend of Sándor Petőfi and János Arany, and a corresponding member of the Hungarian Academy of Sciences from 1858.

After the defeat of the 1848–49 revolution he wrote, under Habsburg censorship, allegories of national grief in the guise of birds, flowers and animals; "A gólyához" (To the Stork, 1850) and "A madár, fiaihoz" (The Bird to Her Sons, 1852) are the best known. He also wrote many verse legends drawn from the folklore and history of the Hungarian uplands. This selection of about fifty poems is in Hungarian, from the Hungarian Wikisource.
