Kahlil Gibran

1883–1931

Kahlil Gibran (Jubran Khalil Jubran) was born in 1883 in Bsharri, in the mountains of what is now Lebanon. His family emigrated to Boston when he was a boy, and he later studied art in Paris and lived in New York, working as both a painter and a writer.

A leading figure of the Mahjar, the Arabic literature of the emigrant communities in the Americas, he wrote in Arabic and, from the 1910s, in English. In 1920 he helped found the Pen League (al-Rabita al-Qalamiyya) in New York. The Madman (1918), a book of parables and poems in English prose, was his first English-language volume. He died in New York in 1931.
