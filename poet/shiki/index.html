Masaoka Shiki

1867–1902

正岡子規 (Masaoka Shiki, 1867–1902) was born in Matsuyama on the island of Shikoku and died in Tokyo at thirty-four, after years of spinal tuberculosis that kept him largely bedridden. He took the pen-name Shiki, the hototogisu or cuckoo, a bird said to cough blood as it sings, after his first haemorrhage in 1889.

Working as a journalist, he set out to renew haiku and tanka on the principle of shasei, a sketch from life, and he abandoned the older word-play and conventional images in favour of direct observation. The Aozora Bunko text of his Kanzan rakuboku, volume one, is a manuscript record of his early hokku arranged by season; the selection here is drawn from its final year, 1892, and includes none of the later volumes.
