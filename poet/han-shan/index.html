Han Shan

fl. 8th-9th century

Han Shan, whose name means Cold Mountain, is the legendary recluse of the Tiantai mountains in Zhejiang, a hermit who is said to have lived among the cliffs and written his verses on rocks, trees and the walls of the temple at Guoqing. Nothing certain is known of his life, and even his century is disputed; he is also the name by which the poems are known, since the verses speak in the voice of the mountain-dweller himself.

The poems were gathered into a collection credited to the official Lüqiu Yin, and later passed into the Quan Tang shi. Plain in diction and Buddhist in outlook, they became favourites of Zen tradition and painters, and in the twentieth century won new readers in the West, notably through Gary Snyder's translations and their place in Beat writing.
