Lactantius

c. 250-c. 325

Lucius Caelius Firmianus Lactantius was a Latin Christian writer of North African origin, born around 250. A pupil of the rhetorician Arnobius, he taught rhetoric at Nicomedia under Diocletian, lost his post when the persecution began, and later became tutor to Crispus, son of the emperor Constantine. His chief works are the Divine Institutes and On the Deaths of the Persecutors.

The poem on the Phoenix, a Latin elegiac account of the fabulous bird that is reborn from its own ashes, is traditionally attributed to him, but its authorship is uncertain, and it is printed here as the work of an unknown author attributed to Lactantius. It is given in the Latin text of Riese (1870) and in William Fletcher's prose translation (1871).
