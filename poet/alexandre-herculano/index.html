Alexandre Herculano

1810–1877

Alexandre Herculano de Carvalho e Araújo (1810–1877) was the foremost historian of nineteenth-century Portugal and, with Almeida Garrett, the leader of Portuguese Romanticism. His História de Portugal and his historical novels made him the chief literary figure of his generation, and his poems are gathered in A Harpa do Crente (1838) and the later Poesias.

Exiled in England and France after the liberal troubles of the 1830s, he came back to Portugal and took a leading part in public life before retiring to his farm at Vale de Lobos. His verse is religious and patriotic in tone and often meditative; the two poems here come from the Oxford Book of Portuguese Verse (1925), edited by Aubrey F. G. Bell.
