Cláudio Manuel da Costa

1729–1789

Cláudio Manuel da Costa (1729–1789), born near Mariana in Minas Gerais, studied law at Coimbra and returned to Vila Rica, where he practised as a lawyer and served as secretary of the captaincy's government. Under the Arcadian name Glauceste Satúrnio he published his Obras (Coimbra, 1768), opening with a long section of sonnets in the manner of Petrarch and Camões, set among the rivers and hills of Minas Gerais, with the shepherd Nise as the beloved; the book is usually taken to open Brazilian Arcadism.

He was a friend of Tomás Antônio Gonzaga and a figure of the circle that took part in the Inconfidência Mineira, the failed conspiracy of 1789 against Portuguese rule. Arrested in that year, he was found dead in his cell in Ouro Preto in July 1789; the official verdict was suicide, though contemporaries and later historians have doubted it.
