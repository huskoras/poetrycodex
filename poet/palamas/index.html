Modern Greek

Kostis Palamas

1859–1943

Kostis (Kostes) Palamas was born in Patras in 1859 and, orphaned young, grew up in Missolonghi before settling in Athens, where he studied law without finishing and made his living as a clerk and later as secretary of the University of Athens. He became the leading figure of the New Athenian School, and the most influential Greek poet of his generation; his Hymn to the Olympic Games (1896) was set to music for the first modern Olympics.

His collections include Songs of My Fatherland (1886), The Twelve Songs of the Gypsy (1907) and The King's Flute (1910). Life Immovable, published in 1904, gathers poems written over the preceding decade, among them the Fatherlands sonnets, The Return, Fragments from the Song to the Sun and Verses of a Familiar Tune. Palamas helped establish demotic Greek as a language of high poetry. He died in Athens in 1943. The English versions here are by Aristides E. Phoutrides, from Life Immovable, First Part (Harvard University Press, 1919).
