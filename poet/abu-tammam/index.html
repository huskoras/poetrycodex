Abu Tammam

c. 804–845

Abu Tammam (أبو تمام), whose full name was Habib ibn Aws al-Ta'i (حبيب بن أوس الطائي), was born around 804 at Jasim in Syria and died in 845, probably at Mosul. He made his career as a panegyrist at the Abbasid court, above all under the caliph al-Mu'tasim, and he is the foremost poet of the "new style" (badi'), whose dense figures, conceits and learned allusions divided the critics of his own and later times.

He is best known for his ode on the taking of Amorium (838), which opens with the line "The sword is more truthful than books", and for compiling al-Hamasa, a famous anthology of earlier Arabic poetry, which is not part of this selection. These poems are the longest in the Arabic text of his divan as transcribed on Wikisource, given in the original with the two halves of each line set apart.
