Abdul Rahim Khan-i-Khanan

1556–1627

Abdul Rahim Khan-i-Khanan (1556–1627), usually called Rahim, was a Mughal nobleman, soldier and poet. Born at Lahore, he was the son of Bairam Khan, the guardian and regent of the emperor Akbar, and rose under Akbar to the high rank of Khan-i-Khanan, commander-in-chief, serving afterwards under Jahangir. He is traditionally counted among the nine jewels of Akbar's court, and was a patron of poets as well as a poet himself. He translated the memoirs of Babur from Turki into Persian.

He wrote in Braj and Awadhi as well as in Persian and Sanskrit. His fame rests on his Hindi couplets (dohas), which draw on ordinary life, friendship, money, humility and the hardness of fortune in plain, proverbial speech, and are still widely quoted. The texts here are taken from the Rahim-Kavitavali, edited by Surendranath Tiwari (1926): the dohas, the sorathas, the Barvai Nayika-bheda and the other barvai, and the Madanashtak.
