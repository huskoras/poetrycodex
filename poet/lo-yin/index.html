Lo Yin

AD 833–910

Lo Yin (Luo Yin 羅隱, AD 833–910), courtesy name Zhaojian, came from Yuhang in Zhejiang. He failed the imperial examination some ten times, changed his name in the attempt, and wandered for years as a clerk in provincial service before attaching himself to Qian Liu, the warlord of Wu-Yue, under whom he rose to modest office at Hangzhou. He is counted among the last notable poets of the late Tang.

His verse is chiefly satirical, its tone sharp and colloquial. The four-line poem on the bee, asking for whom all that labour is sweet, is his best-known piece, and others turn the same edge on the rich, on the vanity of fame and on the fall of dynasties. Here are the bee poem and forty others from the eleven volumes (juan 655–665) of the Complete Tang Poems; his prose is not included.
