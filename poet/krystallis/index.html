Kostas Krystallis

1868-1894

Kostas Krystallis (Κώστας Κρυστάλλης) was born in 1868 in Syrrako, a mountain village of Epirus, into a family of shepherds and herdsmen. He studied in Ioannina and Athens and became a member of the New Athenian School, but his heart stayed in the Pindos: he wrote in the demotic Greek of the Epirote highlands, in the cadence of the folk song.

His poems of shepherds, flocks, spring waters, nymphs and eagles are gathered in Agrotika (1891), which won first praise in the Philadelphios poetry competition of 1890, and in The Singer of the Village and the Fold (1893). He also wrote prose. He died of tuberculosis in Arta in 1894, aged twenty-five, and is remembered as the bucolic voice of the mountains of Epirus.
