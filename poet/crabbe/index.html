Romantic

George Crabbe

1754–1832

George Crabbe (1754–1832) was born at Aldeburgh on the Suffolk coast, trained as a surgeon and apothecary, and went to London in 1780 to try his fortune as a poet. Edmund Burke took him up, and he afterward became a clergyman. The Village (1783), which Samuel Johnson read in manuscript, set out to show the real life of the rural poor against the conventions of pastoral.

His later narrative poems, The Parish Register, The Borough and the Tales, tell the lives of ordinary people in plain, exact couplets and were widely read in his day. Peter Grimes, from The Borough, is the source of Benjamin Britten’s opera.
