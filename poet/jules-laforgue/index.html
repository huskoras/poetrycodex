French

Jules Laforgue

1860–1887

Jules Laforgue (1860–1887) was a French poet born in Montevideo, Uruguay, to French parents, and brought up from the age of six in Tarbes and then in Paris. From 1881 he lived in Berlin as French reader to the Empress Augusta, a post he held until 1886, the year he married the Englishwoman Leah Lee. He died of tuberculosis in Paris in August 1887, aged twenty-seven.

He published only two collections of verse in his lifetime, Les Complaintes (1885) and L'Imitation de Notre-Dame la Lune (1886), whose ironic, colloquial voice, moon-struck Pierrots and early experiments in free verse made him one of the most original of the Symbolists. Des Fleurs de bonne volonté and the Derniers vers appeared after his death. These texts follow the Mercure de France editions of 1917 and 1922 as transcribed and validated on French Wikisource.
