Restoration & Augustan

Samuel Johnson

1709–1784

Samuel Johnson (1709–1784) — lexicographer, essayist, critic, and the dominant literary figure of mid-18th-century England — also wrote poetry of severe, weighty moral force.

London and The Vanity of Human Wishes, both close imitations of Juvenal’s satires, and his plain elegy for his friend Robert Levet, show the same unflinching realism that marks his prose.

Poems (3)

  1. London: A Poem, in Imitation of the Third Satire of Juvenal
  2. The Vanity of Human Wishes: In Imitation of the Tenth Satire of Juvenal
  3. On the Death of Mr. Robert Levet
