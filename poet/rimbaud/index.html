Arthur Rimbaud

1854–1891

Arthur Rimbaud (1854–1891) was born in Charleville in the Ardennes and wrote nearly all of his poetry as a schoolboy and young man, between about 1870 and 1875. His early verse, among it “Le Dormeur du val”, “Ma Bohème”, “Voyelles” and the long “Le Bateau ivre”, circulated in manuscript and through the poet Paul Verlaine, with whom he lived and travelled in France, Belgium and England until 1873.

He published a single book himself, the prose-and-verse Une saison en enfer (Brussels, 1873), and the prose poems of the Illuminations, written in the same years, first appeared in 1886 without his involvement. He gave up writing, travelled in Europe, Cyprus, Egypt and Ethiopia as a trader, and died in Marseille in 1891. The short poems here follow the Vanier edition of 1895, with Une saison en enfer and the Illuminations given as separate works.
