Yi Yuk-sa 이육사

1904–1944

Yi Yuk-sa (이육사, 1904–1944) was the pen name of Yi Wŏllok, born in Andong, North Gyeongsang, into a family of the Yi of Jinseong with a Confucian scholarly tradition. He joined the independence movement in his youth, was repeatedly arrested by the Japanese colonial police (the name Yuk-sa is traditionally linked to his prisoner number 264), and spent long periods in China, where he also wrote for newspapers and magazines.

He began to publish poems in the 1930s, in periodicals such as Sin Joseon, Munjang and Insang pyeongnon, and wrote essays and criticism as well. His few poems, among them "Cheongpodo" (The Green Grapes) and "Jeoljeong" (The Zenith), join a controlled, classical diction to a steadfast resistance to colonial rule. He was arrested for the last time in Beijing in 1943 and died in prison there in January 1944. His collection Yuksa sijip appeared in 1946, edited by his brother; this archive takes only poems whose Wikisource page names a printing in his lifetime.
