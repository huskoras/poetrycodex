German

Martin Opitz

1597-1639

Martin Opitz (1597-1639) was born in Bunzlau in Silesia and studied at Frankfurt an der Oder and Heidelberg, travelling later to the Netherlands and to Transylvania. He served noble patrons in Silesia, was crowned poet laureate by the Emperor Ferdinand II in 1625, and was ennobled as Opitz von Boberfeld in 1627. He died of the plague in Danzig in 1639.

His Buch von der Deutschen Poeterey (1624) set out rules for German verse, among them a regular alternation of stressed and unstressed syllables, and gave the Baroque age in Germany its poetic model. His Teutsche Poemata, published at Strasbourg in 1624, gathered love songs, sonnets, odes and epigrams in the manner of the French Pleiade, the Dutch poets and the Italian Petrarchists. Ach Liebste, laß uns eilen, a carpe diem song in this collection, remains his best-known lyric.

  1. Ach Liebste, laß uns eilen

  23. An der Liebsten Vatterland

  26. An die Augen seiner Jungfrauen

  31. Du güldene Freyheit du

  33. Ich machte diese Verß
  34. An die Deutsche Nation

  36. Nachtklage
