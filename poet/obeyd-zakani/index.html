Arabic & Persian

Obeyd Zakani

d. c.1370

Nizam al-Din Ubayd Allah Zakani, known as Obeyd Zakani, was a Persian satirist of the fourteenth century. He came of an Arab family settled at Qazvin and spent much of his career at Shiraz, where he served at the courts of the Injuid and Muzaffarid rulers. He died around 1370. His prose and verse turn their wit on the vices of court, clergy and officials, and his short comic pieces and epigrams are among the best-known satire in classical Persian.

This selection offers the fable Mouse and Cat, a narrative poem of 190 verses in which a cat who professes repentance preys on the mice; its attribution to Obeyd is traditional. It is followed by thirty ghazals from his Divan, in the order of the Ganjoor edition. His prose works, such as Akhlaq al-Ashraf, and the Oshaqnameh are left out.

  21. مرا ز وصل تو حاصل به جز تمنا نیست (Ghazal 20)
  22. دگر برون شدنم زین دیار ممکن نیست (Ghazal 21)
  23. جانا بیا که بی تو دلم را قرار نیست (Ghazal 22)
  24. حاصل ز زندگانی ما جز وبال نیست (Ghazal 23)
  25. هرگز دلم ز کوی تو جائی دگر نرفت (Ghazal 24)
  26. در خانه تا قرابهٔ ما پر شراب نیست (Ghazal 25)
  27. دلی که بستهٔ زنجیر زلف یاری نیست (Ghazal 26)
  28. بیش از این برگ فراق رخ جانانم نیست (Ghazal 27)
  29. سر نخوانیم که سودا زدهٔ موئی نیست (Ghazal 28)
  30. نه به ز شیوهٔ مستان طریق ورائی هست (Ghazal 29)
  31. دوشم غم تو ملک سویدا گرفته بود (Ghazal 30)
