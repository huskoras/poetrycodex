Victorian

Charles Tennyson Turner

1808–1879

Charles Tennyson Turner (1808–1879) was an English clergyman and poet, born Charles Tennyson at Somersby, Lincolnshire, and the elder brother of Alfred Tennyson; the two brothers appeared together in Poems by Two Brothers. He added the surname Turner after inheriting property from a relative, and spent much of his life as a parish priest in Lincolnshire.

He is remembered chiefly for his sonnets, which turn small observed things—a lark, a rainbow, a drowned spaniel, the bell of a buoy—into brief pictures with a moral or elegiac turn. The poems here are taken from Collected Sonnets, Old and New (London: C. Kegan Paul, 1880), the posthumous gathering of the sonnets he published in 1830, 1864, 1868 and 1873 together with those first printed there, and the few lyrics at its end.
