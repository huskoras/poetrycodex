French

Charles Baudelaire

1821–1867

Charles Baudelaire (1821–1867) was a French poet, critic and translator of Edgar Allan Poe. Born in Paris, he spent his short life in the city's literary and artistic circles, and is widely regarded as a founder of modern poetry and a forerunner of the Symbolists.

His one major collection of verse, Les Fleurs du Mal (1857), drew a prosecution for offending public morality, and six of its poems were suppressed. Its themes of spleen, beauty, desire, the city and death shaped later poets across Europe. These versions are from Cyril Scott's English translation of 1909.
