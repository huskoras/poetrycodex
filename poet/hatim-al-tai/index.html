Hatim al-Tai

6th century

Hatim al-Tai (حاتم الطائي), Hatim ibn 'Abd Allah of the tribe of Tayyi' in northern Arabia, lived in the sixth century and is traditionally said to have died about 578; the dates are uncertain. In Arabic tradition his name stands for generosity, and the proverb "more generous than Hatim" keeps that fame alive. His verse praises open-handed hospitality, defends giving against the reproaches of wife and kin, and recalls the raids and quarrels of his tribe.

The pieces here are the poems of his diwan that run to three or more verses, in the Arabic Wikisource transcriptions, with the vowel marks of that source. Wikisource often carries two transcriptions of one poem, a plain one and a vocalised one; each poem appears once, in its fuller form. As with much pre-Islamic verse, the attribution of some pieces to Hatim is doubtful.
