Marceline Desbordes-Valmore

1786–1859

Marceline Desbordes-Valmore (1786–1859) was a French Romantic poet, born in Douai. She began as an actress and singer, married the actor Prosper Valmore in 1817, and lived by the stage and by her pen in Lyon, Brussels, Rouen and Paris. Her first elegies and romances appeared in 1819, and she went on to publish several volumes of verse as well as novels and tales for children.

Les Pleurs (1833), Pauvres fleurs (1839) and Bouquets et prières (1843) belong to the middle of her career. Their plain, musical lyrics of love, loss, motherhood and faith made her one of the most admired women poets of her century, praised by later poets from Baudelaire to Verlaine. The French texts here follow the first printed editions, with the spelling of the time.
