Naphtali Herz Imber

1856–1909

Naphtali Herz Imber (נפתלי הרץ אימבר, 1856–1909) was born in Złoczów in Galicia and wrote Hebrew verse from his youth. In the early 1880s he went to Palestine as secretary to the English writer Laurence Oliphant, and he lived for years as a wanderer between Europe, the Near East and the United States. He died in New York in 1909.

He is remembered above all for Tikvatenu, the Hebrew poem whose opening stanza became the words of Hatikvah, later the national anthem of Israel. It was printed in his collection Barkai in 1886. The poems here are in Hebrew with their vowel points, taken from the public-domain texts digitised by the volunteers of Project Ben-Yehuda (benyehuda.org): his Zionist hymns, lyrics, satires and elegies, with Tikvatenu first.
