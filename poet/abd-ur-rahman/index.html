Abd-ur-Rahman

fl. late 17th–early 18th century (Raverty)

Mulla Abd-ur-Rahman, of the Ghoriah Khel clan of the Mohmand tribe, lived in the village of Hazar-Khani near Peshawar. By Raverty's account he was a learned recluse who lived as a darwesh, absorbed in prayer, and he is counted among the most popular of the Afghan poets.

His odes, collected in a Diwan after his death, are religious and moral in character and chiefly concern divine love in the Sufi manner. Raverty notes their fiery energy and natural simplicity; he places the poet as a young man during Khushhal Khan's last years, and one poem refers to events of 1711.
