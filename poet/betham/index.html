Matilda Betham

1776–1852

Matilda Betham (1776–1852) was born at Stonham Aspal in Suffolk, the daughter of a clergyman and schoolmaster. She grew up among books and, in time, made a career as a miniature painter, exhibiting at the Royal Academy between 1804 and 1816, as well as a poet and writer. Her first volume, Elegies and Other Small Poems, appeared in 1797.

In London she moved among the writers of her day and counted Charles and Mary Lamb, Samuel Taylor Coleridge and Robert Southey among her friends. Her later books included a volume of Poems (1808), A Biographical Dictionary of the Celebrated Women of Every Age and Country (1804) and the long narrative poem The Lay of Marie (1816), which draws on the medieval lais of Marie de France, followed by the short lyrics of Vignettes in Verse (1818).

This selection gathers her verse from Elegies and Other Small Poems, Poems, The Lay of Marie (one entry for each of its four cantos) and Vignettes in Verse, with Betham's own prefaces, notes and the Italian originals of her translations left out.
