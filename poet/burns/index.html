Robert Burns

1759–1796

Robert Burns (1759–1796), Scotland’s national poet, turned the everyday life and speech of Ayrshire farming folk into poetry of remarkable warmth, wit, and force.

Writing in Scots and English by turns, he ranged from the comic terror of Tam O’ Shanter to the tenderness of A Red, Red Rose and the satire of Holy Willie’s Prayer; his spelling is kept exactly as printed here.
