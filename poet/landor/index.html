Romantic

Walter Savage Landor

1775–1864

Walter Savage Landor (1775–1864) was an English writer best known for Imaginary Conversations, a vast series of prose dialogues between historical figures, and for a small body of exquisitely polished short poems and epigrams that later poets — Swinburne, Pound, and others — prized above almost anything else of his era for their classical restraint.

Quarrelsome and litigious by temperament (he was expelled from Oxford and later drove himself into exile in Italy more than once), Landor lived to eighty-nine, still writing crisp, controlled verse almost to the end — including his own epitaph-poem, ‘I strove with none.’
