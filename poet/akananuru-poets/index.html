Akananuru Poets

Sangam era, c. 100 BCE–250 CE

The Akananuru (அகநானூறு, “Four Hundred Poems on the Inner Life”), also called the Netuntokai, is one of the eight anthologies (Ettuttokai) of Sangam literature, the earliest surviving poetry in Tamil. Its 400 akam poems, of thirteen to thirty-one lines each, are love poems spoken by lovers, confidantes and foster-mothers, set in the landscapes of the Tamil country and often rich in historical allusion to kings, towns and battles.

The anthology falls into three parts: Kalirriyanai nirai (poems 1–120), Manimitai pavalam (121–300) and Nittilakkovai (301–400). The poems are by many named poets and were gathered by later compilers. The Tamil text here is the classical text as transcribed on Tamil Wikisource; it begins with the invocation to Shiva, and poems 131 and 190, which that transcription does not supply, are not included.
