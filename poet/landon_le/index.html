Letitia Elizabeth Landon (L.E.L.)

1802–1838

Letitia Elizabeth Landon (1802–1838), who wrote under the initials L.E.L., was born in Chelsea, London. Her poems began to appear in William Jerdan’s Literary Gazette in 1820, signed with those initials, and The Improvisatrice (1824) made her one of the best-known poets of her day.

It was followed by The Troubadour (1825), The Golden Violet (1827) and The Venetian Bracelet (1829), long verse romances and tales that dwell on love, loss and the life of the woman poet. She also contributed constantly to the annuals and gift-books of the time and edited Fisher’s Drawing Room Scrap Book.

In June 1838 she married George Maclean, governor of Cape Coast Castle on the Gold Coast, and sailed with him to West Africa. She died there on 15 October 1838, at the age of thirty-six.
