Metaphysical & Cavalier

Charles Cotton

1630–1687

Charles Cotton (1630–1687) was an English poet and translator from Beresford Hall in Staffordshire, on the river Dove. He was the friend and adopted "son" of Izaak Walton, and wrote the second part of The Compleat Angler (1676), the treatise on fly-fishing added to Walton's book. His translation of Montaigne's Essays (1685) remained the standard English version for generations.

His burlesques, Scarronides (a travesty of Virgil, 1664) and A Voyage to Ireland in Burlesque, made him a leading comic poet of the Restoration, while his love lyrics, odes and "quatrains" of the countryside show a quieter, more personal voice. Poems on Several Occasions appeared posthumously in 1689; the text here follows that edition (EEBO-TCP), including its closing section of translations from Horace, Martial, Catullus, Johannes Secundus and French and Italian poets, and leaving out the commendatory verses by another hand.
