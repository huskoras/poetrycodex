German

Joseph Victor von Scheffel

1826–1886

Joseph Victor von Scheffel (1826–1886) was born in Karlsruhe and studied law at Munich, Heidelberg and Berlin before turning to literature. He lived for a time at Säckingen on the Upper Rhine, spent the late 1850s at Donaueschingen as librarian to the Prince of Fürstenberg, and was ennobled in 1876. His verse romance Der Trompeter von Säkkingen and his historical novel Ekkehard (1855) made him one of the most widely read German authors of the nineteenth century.

The poems here are his songs, as they live in the German student songbook tradition: the lyrics from the Allgemeines Deutsches Kommersbuch (Lahr: Schauenburg, c. 1896–98) that carry his name, among them “Alt-Heidelberg, du feine”, the Rodenstein songs and the mock-scientific Ichthyosaurus; “Trifels” from the Gaudeamus volume (1868); and “Maimorgengang” from Die Gartenlaube (1869). The Kommersbuch prints its songs as prose under the music, with sung syllable hyphens and repeat signs; here they are set back into their verse lines and the music’s repetitions are removed.
