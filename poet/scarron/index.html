French

Paul Scarron

1610–1660

Paul Scarron (1610–1660) was born in Paris into a family of magistrates and took minor orders as a young man. Around 1638 a disease left him crippled and bent, and he spent the rest of his life in a chair, which he turned into material for his humour. He kept a famous salon in the Marais, and in 1652 he married the young Françoise d’Aubigné, later Madame de Maintenon.

Scarron was the leading master of the French burlesque style, best known for the travesty epic Le Virgile travesti, the plays Jodelet and Don Japhet d’Arménie, and the novel Le Roman comique. This selection takes his short verse only: the epitaph he is said to have composed for himself, an address to the young Duke of Anjou, and a handful of chansons.

Poems (7)

  2. Au Duc d’Anjou
  3. Chanson: Je vous aymois
  4. Chanson: Ma raison me l’a dit aussi bien
  5. Chanson: Mes yeux, vous avez veu Cloris
  6. Chanson: Philis me traitte avec rigueur
  7. Chanson: Philis, vous vous plaignez
