Konstantin Balmont (Константин Бальмонт)

1867–1942

Konstantin Balmont (1867–1942) was one of the leading poets of Russian Symbolism and a central voice of the Silver Age. Born in the village of Gumnishchi in the Vladimir province, he made his name in the 1890s with collections such as Under the Northern Sky, In Boundless Space and Silence, and with Burning Buildings (1900) and Let Us Be Like the Sun (1903) became the most celebrated, and most imitated, poet of his generation. He was also a prolific translator, rendering Shelley, Poe, Whitman, Calderón and many others into Russian.

Balmont left Russia in 1906 after the events of the 1905 revolution, returned in 1913, and emigrated for good in 1920, settling in France. He died at Noisy-le-Grand, near Paris, on 23 December 1942. The poems here are from Let Us Be Like the Sun (Moscow: Scorpion, 1903), the first 150 poems in book order, in the pre-reform orthography of the printed book as transcribed on Russian Wikisource.
