Romantic

Henry Kirke White

1785–1806

Henry Kirke White (1785–1806) was born in Nottingham, the son of a butcher, and was put to work early in a stocking-loom and then a lawyer's office while he taught himself languages and wrote verse. His first volume, Clifton Grove (1803), won him the notice of Robert Southey, and with the help of patrons he went up to St John's College, Cambridge, to study for the church.

Overwork and the strain of competing for university honours broke his health, and he died of consumption at Cambridge at the age of twenty-one. Southey edited his Remains (1807), and Lord Byron's lines on his early death in English Bards and Scotch Reviewers made his name widely known. The poems here follow the Poetical Works with a memoir by Sir Harris Nicolas.
