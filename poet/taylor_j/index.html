Metaphysical & Cavalier

John Taylor

1580–1653

John Taylor (1580–1653), known as “the Water Poet”, was a Thames waterman who made himself a prolific pamphleteer and verse-writer. He worked the river as a ferryman and sculler, served the navy in his youth, and later enjoyed the notice of the court of James I.

The Sculler (1612) and The Nipping and Snipping of Abuses (1614), both gathered here, are early miscellanies of satires, epigrams, sonnets and anagrams. They mix anti-Catholic invective, jibes at the manners of the city, and verses of compliment to patrons and fellow writers.
