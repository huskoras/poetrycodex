Egill Skallagrímsson

c. 910–990

Egill Skallagrímsson (c. 910–990) was an Icelandic farmer, Viking raider and poet, the central figure of Egil’s Saga. Born into a powerful settler family at Borg in western Iceland, he spent much of his youth abroad in the service of Norwegian and English kings, and by the saga’s account fought for King Athelstan of England in the battle of Brunanburh (937). He was the enemy of King Eric Bloodaxe of Norway, and the saga tells how he made his peace with him at York.

He is the earliest Icelandic skald whose work survives in any quantity. The three poems given here are the ones the nineteenth-century editors Gudbrand Vigfusson and F. York Powell singled out as his ‘three great poems’: Höfuðlausn (‘Head-ransom’), the praise-poem that by tradition bought his life from Eric Bloodaxe at York; Arinbjarnarkviða, a tribute to his friend and protector Arinbjorn, preserved in damaged form; and Sonatorrek (‘The Sons’ Wreck’), his elegy for two sons, one lost to fever and one drowned, which is among the most personal poems of the Viking Age.

Each poem appears twice, as the Old Norse text and as the facing English prose version from the Oxford edition of Corpus Poeticum Boreale (1883). That edition’s bracketed explanations of the kennings are kept, and so are the gaps the editors mark where the manuscript is lost or illegible.
