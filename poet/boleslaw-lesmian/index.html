Bolesław Leśmian

1877-1937

Bolesław Leśmian (born Bolesław Lesman, 1877-1937) was a Polish poet, born in Warsaw and raised in Kiev, where he studied law. He lived for years in Russia and in Western Europe before settling in Poland, where he worked as a notary in Hrubieszów and Zamość and was elected to the Polish Academy of Literature in 1933. He also wrote ballads, tales and essays and translated the Arabian Nights and Poe.

This selection gathers the three books he published in his lifetime: Sad rozstajny (1912), Łąka (1920) and Napój cienisty (1936). His lyric of the folk ballad and the metaphysical, with its coined words and its animated fields, orchards and forests, has made him one of the most distinctive voices of twentieth-century Polish poetry. The posthumous Dziejba leśna (1938) is not included.
