Ancient Near East

Hammurabi

reigned c. 1792 – 1750 BC

Hammurabi was the sixth king of the First Dynasty of Babylon and reigned for about forty years, c. 1792 to 1750 BC. He made his city the head of a kingdom that took in most of Mesopotamia, and he is remembered above all as the king under whose name a great collection of laws was set down in Akkadian.

The laws were cut in cuneiform on a tall block of black diorite, topped by a relief of the king standing before the sun-god Shamash. The stele was carried off to Susa in antiquity, where French excavators found it in 1901-02. It is now in the Louvre. Several columns of the text had been chiselled away, and the gap is filled in part from clay-tablet copies. It is given here in C. H. W. Johns's translation of 1903.
