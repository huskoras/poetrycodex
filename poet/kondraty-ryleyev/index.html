Kondraty Ryleyev

1795–1826

Kondraty Fyodorovich Ryleyev (1795–1826) was a Russian poet and one of the leaders of the Decembrist rising of December 1825. Born at Batovo near St Petersburg, he served as an army officer, later sat as a judge in the capital and worked for the Russian-American Company, and with Aleksandr Bestuzhev he edited the almanac Polyarnaya zvezda (The Polar Star). A member of the Northern Society, he helped to organise the rising on Senate Square; after its failure he was arrested and in July 1826 was hanged in the Peter and Paul Fortress with four other leaders.

His best-known book is the Dumy (1825), a cycle of historical poems on heroes and villains of Russian and Ukrainian history, written to rouse patriotic and civic feeling; the opening of The Death of Yermak (Smert Ermaka) became a popular song. The narrative poem Voynarovsky (1825), on the exile in Yakutsk of Mazepa's nephew, and the short lyric beginning "Ya l budu v rokovoe vremya" (Citizen) is among the best-known civic poems of the Decembrists. The texts here are the Russian originals as transcribed on Russian Wikisource.
