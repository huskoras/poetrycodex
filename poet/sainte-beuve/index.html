Charles-Augustin Sainte-Beuve

1804–1869

Charles-Augustin Sainte-Beuve (1804–1869) was a French critic and poet, born in Boulogne-sur-Mer and trained in medicine in Paris before he turned to literature. He wrote for the Globe and later for the Revue des Deux Mondes and the Constitutionnel, and his weekly essays, the Causeries du lundi, made him the leading critic of his age; he became a member of the Académie française in 1844.

His verse belongs to his early career, in the Romantic circle of Victor Hugo. Vie, poésies et pensées de Joseph Delorme (1829) presents the poems of an invented young medical student, a melancholy, self-observing poet of the suburbs, and helped to bring a plainer, more intimate manner into French lyric; Les Consolations followed in 1830. The poems here are from the 1863 collected edition of his Poésies (Michel Lévy frères), as transcribed on French Wikisource: the first fifty poems of the Joseph Delorme volume.
