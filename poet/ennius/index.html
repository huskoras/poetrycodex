Ancient Greek & Roman

Quintus Ennius

239–169 BC

Quintus Ennius was born in 239 BC at Rudiae in Calabria, in the south of Italy, where Greek, Oscan and Latin were all spoken. Ancient writers report that he served in the Roman army, was brought to Rome, and made his living by teaching and by writing for the stage; he is said to have received Roman citizenship in 184 BC. He died in 169 BC. Later Romans honoured him as the father of their poetry.

His chief work was the Annales, a history of Rome in hexameters, from the fall of Troy down to his own time, in eighteen books. It was the first Roman epic in the metre of Homer, and Virgil and Lucretius drew on it. The poem was lost in antiquity and survives only in fragments, preserved as quotations by grammarians, antiquarians and other writers. Here the fragments are given in the order of Johannes Vahlen's edition of 1903.
