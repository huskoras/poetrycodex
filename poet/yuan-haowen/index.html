Yuan Haowen

1190–1257

Yuan Haowen (元好問, 1190–1257), courtesy name Yuzhi and literary name Yishan, was the leading poet of the Jin dynasty, the state the Jurchens founded in northern China. He came from Xinzhou in Shanxi, took the jinshi degree in 1221, and served as a local official and in the capital before the Jin fell to the Mongols in 1234.

After the fall he did not take office under the conquerors but devoted himself to preserving the culture of the lost state. His anthology of Jin poets, the Zhongzhou ji (中州集), keeps the work of many writers who would otherwise have been lost, and his poems of the years of ruin are among the great elegies of Chinese literature. He is also remembered for the ci lyric on the wild goose mound (雁丘詞) and for his Thirty Quatrains on Poetry (論詩絕句三十首), a sequence of verse criticism running from the Han poets to the Song.
