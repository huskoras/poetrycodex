Hungarian

János Arany

1817–1882

János Arany (1817–1882) is, with Sándor Petőfi, one of the two great poets of nineteenth-century Hungary. Born in Nagyszalonta, in the Great Hungarian Plain, the son of a farming family, he worked as a teacher and clerk before his epic Toldi (1846–47) won the prize of the Kisfaludy Society and brought him the friendship of Petőfi, who wrote the epistle to which several of the poems below reply.

After the defeat of the revolution of 1848–49 he withdrew into the quiet of Nagykőrös and Pest, where, in the 1850s, he wrote the ballads for which he is best known; he later served as secretary and general secretary of the Hungarian Academy. His last poems, the Őszikék (Autumn Crocuses) of 1877–82, are among the most personal in the language.

This selection holds the ballads and a group of his shorter poems and lyrics, in Hungarian, from the transcriptions of the Hungarian Wikisource (CC BY-SA). Only pages that the Wikisource proofreading does not rate below 75% are included, so some famous ballads, among them A walesi bárdok, Ágnes asszony and Szondi két apródja, are not yet here.
