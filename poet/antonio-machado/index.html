Antonio Machado

1875–1939

Antonio Machado (1875–1939) was a Spanish poet of the Generation of '98, born in Seville and raised in Madrid, where he studied at the Institución Libre de Enseñanza. He spent time in Paris in 1899 and again in 1902, where he met Rubén Darío, and published his first book, Soledades, in 1903; an expanded Soledades. Galerías. Otros poemas followed in 1907.

From 1907 he taught French at the secondary school in Soria, where he married Leonor Izquierdo in 1909; her death in 1912 shaped much of his later verse. His Campos de Castilla (1912, enlarged 1917) made him the poet of the Castilian landscape and of Spain's reckoning with itself. He died in exile at Collioure, in southern France, in February 1939, shortly after the end of the Spanish Civil War. The text here is the 1917 Poesías completas, as digitised by Project Gutenberg.
