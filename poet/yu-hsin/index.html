Yü Hsin

513–581

Yü Hsin (Yu Xin, 庾信, courtesy name Zishan 子山, 513–581) was born at Xinye in Nanyang, the son of the poet Yu Jianwu, and grew up among the court poets of Liang. He served the Liang crown prince Xiao Gang and was a leading writer of the ornate palace style, with Xu Ling.

Sent on an embassy to Western Wei in 554, he was kept in the north when Liang fell, and served the Western Wei and Northern Zhou courts for the rest of his life, honoured but never released. The poems of his exile, among them the twenty-seven "Poems Modelled on Singing My Cares" and the "Lament for the South" (Ai Jiangnan fu), turn the ornate southern style to homesickness and the memory of a lost kingdom. The poems here are the Chinese originals, from Wikisource transcriptions of the Siku quanshu text of his collected works and of Yutai xinyong.
