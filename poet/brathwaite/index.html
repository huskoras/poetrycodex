Metaphysical & Cavalier

Richard Brathwaite

1588?–1673

Richard Brathwaite (also spelled Brathwait, c. 1588-1673) was an English poet and prose writer from Westmorland, in the north of England. He studied at Oxford and Cambridge, spent most of his life in the north, and wrote a long run of conduct books, moral treatises and verse, among them The English Gentleman (1630).

The poems here come from two early volumes. A Strappado for the Divell (1615) mixes satirical epigrams, verse addresses to the townsfolk of Kendal, and the Ovidian tale of Pyramus and Thisbe. Natures Embassie (1621) gathers satires, funeral elegies, pastoral eclogues and the bird poems that give the book its name. Poems too badly damaged in the digital transcription of the printed text have been left out.
