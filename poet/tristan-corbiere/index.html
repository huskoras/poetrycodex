French

Tristan Corbière

1845–1875

Tristan Corbière (born Édouard-Joachim Corbière, 1845–1875) was a French poet born at Coat-Congar, near Morlaix in Brittany, the son of the sea captain and novelist Édouard Corbière, author of Le Négrier (1832). Chronically ill from his teens, probably with tuberculosis, he spent much of his short life in Brittany, at Roscoff and Morlaix, with intervals in Paris and a stay in Italy.

His only book, Les Amours jaunes, appeared in Paris at his own expense in 1873 and passed almost unnoticed. It mixes Parisian bohemia, Breton sailors and shore, love poems and mocking self-portraits in a deliberately broken, slangy, ironic verse. He died in Morlaix on 1 March 1875, aged twenty-nine. Paul Verlaine included him in Les Poètes maudits (1884), and later French poets, notably Jules Laforgue, took up his manner. These poems are the French text of the 1873 edition, from Wikisource.
