Jan Kasprowicz

1860–1926

Jan Kasprowicz (1860–1926) was a Polish poet born at Szymborze in Kujawy, the son of a peasant family. He studied in Wrocław, Leipzig and Lwów, was imprisoned in 1887 for socialist activity, and became one of the leading voices of Young Poland (Młoda Polska). From 1909 he held the chair of comparative literature at the University of Lwów, and he also translated Shakespeare and the Greek tragedians into Polish.

His early verse spoke for the rural poor; Krzak dzikiej róży (1898) and Z chałupy turn to the Tatra mountains and to peasant life, while the great free-verse Hymny of 1921–22 (Dies irae, Salome, Moja pieśń wieczorna and others) wrestle with sin, God and judgement. He died at Poronin in 1926. This selection takes the verse books and cycles digitised by Wolne Lektury from the editions of 1898, 1905 and 1922, with five poems from Boy-Żeleński's 1947 anthology Młoda Polska.
