French

François Villon

1431–after 1463

François Villon (born in Paris in 1431) took his master's degree at the University of Paris in 1452 and then lived by his wits on the margins of the city. In 1455 he killed a priest in a street quarrel and fled; he was later imprisoned at Meung-sur-Loire and released in 1461, and in 1462–63 he was condemned to be hanged in Paris and had the sentence commuted to banishment. After January 1463 nothing certain is known of him.

His reputation rests on two mock wills: the Lesser Testament (Le Lais), written at Christmas 1456, and the Greater Testament (Le Testament), begun about 1461, into which he set ballads such as the Ballad of Old-Time Ladies with its refrain about last year's snow, together with the Epitaph in Ballad Form, usually known as the Ballad of the Hanged. These texts are John Payne's English verse translation of 1878, as reprinted by Thomas Bird Mosher of Portland, Maine, in 1909; the Payne volume leaves out a few ballads, which the book itself marks as omitted.
