Italian

Ugo Foscolo

1778–1827

Ugo Foscolo (1778–1827), baptised Niccolò, was born on the Venetian-ruled Ionian island of Zakynthos to an Italian father and a Greek mother, and was educated in Dalmatia and Padua. Disillusioned when Napoleon ceded Venice to Austria at Campoformio in 1797, he wrote the epistolary novel Ultime lettere di Jacopo Ortis (1802) and served as a soldier in the Napoleonic armies in Italy and on the Channel coast.

His twelve sonnets and two odes appeared as Poesie in 1803, and the blank-verse carme Dei Sepolcri (1807), a meditation on tombs, memory and the civic power of poetry, made him a national poet of the Risorgimento. Refusing to swear allegiance to the Austrians restored to Lombardy, he left Italy in 1815 and settled in England in 1816, where he lived by writing and teaching. He died in 1827 at Turnham Green, near London; his remains were carried to Santa Croce in Florence in 1871.
