Johann Wolfgang von Goethe

1749–1832

Johann Wolfgang von Goethe (1749–1832) was born in Frankfurt am Main and studied law before the success of his novel The Sorrows of Young Werther (1774) made him famous across Europe. In 1775 he moved to Weimar at the invitation of Duke Karl August, where he served as a minister of state and director of the court theatre, and from 1794 he was the close friend and collaborator of Friedrich Schiller.

Best known for the drama Faust, he wrote lyrics, ballads, odes, elegies and epigrams throughout his life, from the early songs and the Roman Elegies and Venetian Epigrams of his Italian years to the West-Eastern Divan (1819), inspired by the Persian poet Hafiz. The English versions here are from Edgar Alfred Bowring’s 1853 The Poems of Goethe, translated in the original metres; it is a selection from his shorter verse.
