Arabic & Persian

Hafiz

c. 1315–1390

Shams al-Din Muhammad Hafez of Shiraz (c. 1315–1390) is the most beloved lyric poet of Persia. He is remembered chiefly for his ghazals, short rhymed lyrics of love, wine and mystical longing, gathered after his death into the Divan. His pen-name, Hafiz, means one who has memorised the Qur'an. He lived in Shiraz, where his tomb remains a place of pilgrimage, and his verses are still consulted and recited across the Persian-speaking world.

The forty-three poems here are Gertrude Lowthian Bell's Poems from the Divan of Hafiz (London: Heinemann, 1897). They are free rhymed English versions rather than literal renderings, and Bell's own introduction and notes are left out.
