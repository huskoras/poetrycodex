French

Eustache Deschamps

c. 1340–c. 1404

Eustache Deschamps (c. 1340–c. 1404) was a French poet of the generation after Guillaume de Machaut, whom he honoured as his master. Born at Vertus in Champagne, he served the Valois court under Charles V and Charles VI as a soldier and royal official, and wrote some thousand and more ballades, together with rondeaux, virelais, lays and a verse treatise on the art of poetry, the Art de dictier (1392). His work is a lively chronicle of court life, the Hundred Years' War and the follies of his age.

The poems here are the first hundred ballades of the first volume of the Société des anciens textes français edition of his works (ed. the marquis de Queux de Saint-Hilaire, 1878), the section headed Balades de moralitez, numbered as in the edition. They treat covetousness, pride and flattery, the wars with England and Flanders, the crusade, the troubles of a court servant and the hope of peace. The spelling is that of the edition, and the titles are the editor's. A row of dots marks a verse the manuscript leaves incomplete.
