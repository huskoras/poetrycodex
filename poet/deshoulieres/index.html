Antoinette Deshoulières

1638–1694

Antoinette du Ligier de la Garde, Madame Deshoulières (1638–1694), was born in Paris and married the soldier Guillaume de Boisguérin de Deshoulières in 1651. She kept a salon in Paris, enjoyed the favour of Louis XIV and his court, and was among the best-known women poets of the Grand Siècle; learned academies outside France, among them the Ricovrati of Padua, admitted her to their number.

Her verse is best known for its pastoral idylls, in which sheep, flowers, brooks and birds become occasions for quiet moral reflection on ambition, love, time and death; "Les Moutons" is the most famous. She also wrote epistles to friends and patrons, stances, rondeaux, ballades, madrigals and songs. The poems here follow the 1824 Paris edition of her Poésies, with the 1824 spelling kept.
