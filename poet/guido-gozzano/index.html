Guido Gozzano

1883–1916

Guido Gozzano (Turin, 19 December 1883 – Turin, 9 August 1916) was an Italian poet, the best-known voice of the “crepuscolari”, the twilight poets who answered D’Annunzio’s grandeur with a plain, ironic, conversational verse. He studied law at the University of Turin but followed the literature lectures of Arturo Graf, and he wrote in the shadow of tuberculosis, which he contracted in his early twenties.

His first book, La via del rifugio, appeared in 1907, and his second, I colloqui, in 1911; it contains his most admired poems, among them La signorina Felicita ovvero la Felicità, L’amica di nonna Speranza and Totò Merùmeni. He travelled to India in 1912–13 in search of a cure, and he died in his native Turin at thirty-two.
