Shimazaki Tōson

1872–1943

島崎藤村 (Shimazaki Tōson, 1872–1943), born Shimazaki Haruki in the Kiso district of Nagano, was a poet and later a major novelist. A member of the circle around the journal Bungakukai, he taught in Sendai in 1896–97 and published Wakanashu (Young Herbs, 1897), the first collection of Japanese new-style poetry (shintaishi) and the book that made his name.

He followed it with Hitohabune (1898), Natsukusa (1898) and Rakubaishū (1901), the last written in Komoro in Nagano, then turned to prose with Hakai (The Broken Commandment, 1906). This file holds the whole of Wakanashu from the Aozora Bunko text, plus the poems of the other three books that the poet kept in his own 1927 selection, Tōson shishō, in the old kana and kanji of that printing.
