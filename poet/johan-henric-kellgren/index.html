Johan Henric Kellgren

1751–1795

Johan Henric Kellgren (1751–1795) was a Swedish poet, critic and journalist, the most prominent writer of the Gustavian age, the period of Gustav III's rule. Born in Västergötland, he studied at the Academy in Åbo (Turku) before making his career in Stockholm, where in 1778 he helped found the newspaper Stockholms Posten, which became the organ of the new taste.

He was a member of the circle around the king, served as his secretary, and was one of the founders of the Swedish Academy in 1786. His lyric verse moves between Anacreontic pleasure, as in the Bacchus and Eglé poem, and sharp satire against superstition and pedantry (Ljusets fiender, Mina löjen); the long poem Den nya skapelsen is one of his best-known love poems. Gustav III's operas Gustaf Wasa and Drottning Christina also carry his words. He died in Stockholm in 1795.
