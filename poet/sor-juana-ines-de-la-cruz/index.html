Sor Juana Inés de la Cruz

1648–1695

Sor Juana Inés de la Cruz (1648–1695) was a Mexican nun, scholar and the outstanding poet of the Spanish-American baroque. Born Juana Inés de Asbaje y Ramírez in the viceroyalty of New Spain, she became a lady-in-waiting at the viceregal court and in 1669 entered the convent of San Jerónimo in Mexico City, where she assembled a large library and wrote on poetry, music, theology and natural philosophy. Some sources give 1651 as her birth year; the 1648 date is the one most often accepted.

Her lyric verse ranges from love sonnets and the satirical redondillas “Hombres necios” to romances, liras and religious poems, and she also wrote plays and the long philosophical poem Primero sueño. She died in 1695 while nursing sisters during an epidemic. The poems here are taken from the Obras selectas edited by Juan León Mera (Quito, 1873; Project Gutenberg no. 74087), in Spanish, with the editor's essay and notes omitted.
