Romantic

Thomas Chatterton

1752–1770

Thomas Chatterton (1752–1770) was born in Bristol, the posthumous son of a schoolmaster and church official, and was writing remarkable verse before he was in his teens. He invented a fifteenth-century Bristol priest named Thomas Rowley and wrote a body of poems in an imitation medieval English, passing them off as Rowley's recovered manuscripts.

He went to London in 1770 hoping to live by his pen and died there in August of that year, aged seventeen. The question whether the Rowley poems were genuine was argued for decades, but they are now known to be his own. Wordsworth called him ‘the marvellous Boy,’ and Keats dedicated Endymion to his memory.
