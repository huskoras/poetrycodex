Adelbert von Chamisso

1781–1838

Adelbert von Chamisso (Louis Charles Adélaïde de Chamisso de Boncourt) was born in 1781 at the château of Boncourt in Champagne. His family fled the French Revolution and settled in Prussia, where he served as an officer and learned German, the language in which he came to write. He took part in the Russian exploring voyage of the Rurik (1815–1818) as naturalist, later became a curator at the Berlin botanical garden, and died in Berlin in 1838.

He is best known for the tale Peter Schlemihl (1814) and for his German poems: ballads, humorous and political pieces, and the song cycle Frauen-Liebe und Leben (1830), set to music by Robert Schumann. The seven poems here come from the Wikisource transcription of the 1869 Gedichte and other nineteenth-century printings of his work.
