Francisco de Quevedo

1580–1645

Francisco Gómez de Quevedo y Villegas (Madrid, 1580 – Villanueva de los Infantes, 1645) was a Spanish nobleman, courtier, politician and writer of the Golden Age. He studied at Alcalá and Valladolid, and became a leading figure of the conceptista style, with its compressed wit and wordplay, and a long-running literary rival of Luis de Góngora.

His verse ranges from the metaphysical gravity of sonnets such as 'Miré los muros de la patria mía' and 'Cerrar podrá mis ojos la postrera' to bitter satire and burlesque; his prose includes the picaresque novel La vida del Buscón and the Sueños. Much of his poetry was published only after his death, in El Parnaso español (1648) and Las tres musas últimas castellanas (1670). This selection is transcribed from Spanish Wikisource.
