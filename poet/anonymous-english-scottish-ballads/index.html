Anonymous (English and Scottish Ballads)

traditional; texts recorded c. 15th–18th century

The popular ballads of England and Scotland are anonymous narrative songs, passed on by voice for generations before collectors wrote them down, mostly between the 15th and 18th centuries, so each ballad often survives in several differing versions. They tell of elves and ghosts, outlaws, border raids, feuds and unhappy lovers in plain, repeated formulas and refrains.

This archive follows the selection of Arthur Quiller-Couch in The Oxford Book of Ballads (Clarendon Press, 1910), which gathers 176 ballads in seven books, from the supernatural and romantic pieces to the outlaw, historical and later broadside ballads. The editor keeps the Scots spellings of his sources.
