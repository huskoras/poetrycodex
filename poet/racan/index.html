Honorat de Bueil, seigneur de Racan

1589–1670

Honorat de Bueil, seigneur de Racan (1589–1670), was born in Touraine and served as a page at the court of Henri IV. He became the pupil and friend of François de Malherbe, whose strict principles of verse he absorbed, and he was among the first members of the Académie française. His pastoral play Les Bergeries (1625) was widely admired in his lifetime.

His best-known poem is the 'Stances sur la retraite', an evocation of country life in the manner of Horace's Beatus ille, which was long learned by heart in France. He also wrote odes, sonnets, epitaphs and, late in life, paraphrases of the Psalms. This selection is taken from the 1857 edition of his collected works prepared by Tenant de Latour, in the original seventeenth-century spelling.
