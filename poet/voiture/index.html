French

Vincent Voiture

1597–1648

Vincent Voiture (1597–1648), son of a wine merchant of Amiens, became the most celebrated wit of the Hôtel de Rambouillet, the Paris salon that set the tone of polite letters under Louis XIII. He served the household of Gaston d’Orléans, travelled on missions abroad, and was among the early members of the Académie française.

He wrote gallant letters, sonnets, rondeaux and chansons in a deliberately playful, polished manner. His sonnet to Uranie set off the famous quarrel between the “Uranistes” and the partisans of Benserade’s sonnet on Job, and his rondeaux were long held up as models of the form. His works were collected by his nephew Martin de Pinchesne in 1649; the selection here follows Crépet’s anthology Les Poëtes français (1861) and Ubicini’s edition (1855).
