Wen Tianxiang 文天祥 (Wen T’ien-hsiang)

1236–1283

Wen Tianxiang (文天祥, 1236–1283), courtesy name Songrui, was a Southern Song scholar-official from Luling in Jizhou (modern Jiangxi). He took first place in the imperial examination of 1256 and rose to be chief councillor as the Song fell to the Mongol armies.

Captured in 1278 and held in Khanbaliq (modern Beijing) for some four years, he refused every offer of service to the Yuan court and was executed in 1283. Poems written in captivity, among them Guo Lingding yang and Zheng qi ge, made him the best-loved emblem of loyalty in Chinese poetry. The texts here are taken from Chinese Wikisource (Wenshan xiansheng wenji and Zhinan houlu); the prose preface to Zheng qi ge is omitted.
