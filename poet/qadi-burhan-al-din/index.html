Turkish & Ottoman

Qadi Burhan al-Din

1345–1398

Ahmed Burhan al-Din (1345-1398) was a judge, scholar and soldier who rose to rule Sivas in central Anatolia as its sultan, holding the kingdom he had made for himself by the sword until his death in 1398. He wrote verse in Arabic, Persian and Turkish.

His Turkish divan, copied in 1395-96 and preserved in a unique manuscript in the British Museum, is the oldest surviving collection of literary lyric poetry in the Turkish of Anatolia. E. J. W. Gibb, in A History of Ottoman Poetry (1900), counts him the first Western Turkish love-poet, and the first to leave the mystic circle for ghazels, quatrains and tuyughs of love, wine and the beloved. The selection here is Gibb's English rendering of his specimens.

  5. Is Not Her Love the Soul Within This Lifeless Frame of Me
  6. O Fairest One! O Fairest One! O Fairest
  7. I Said: That I Thy Lip Shall Drain, May It Be
  8. What Hurt Were I Made Glad by Union with Thee
  9. Again for Yon Leyli Is My Heart Mejnun-Wode
  10. Said I: Thy Lip! Said She: How Sweet He Speaks
  11. Said She: Why Thine Eyen Weeping Fain Do I See
  12. Thy Lips for My Soul the Cure or Bane Do I See
  13. The Heart for Thine Eyes to Fragments Torn Must Be
  14. Life for Your Loved Give! Ye Who Lovers Be
  15. That Which God, or Yet Time Was, Hath Writ, Shall Be
  16. Thanks to God, 'Tis Now of Heroes Bold the Day
  17. That on Earth I Have No Share, Full Well I Know
  18. Beauties Like to Thee Within the World Are Few
  19. Pity 'Twere If Aught Hid in the Heart Remain
  20. All Our Works and Deeds Before the Truth Are Known
  21. Youthful Charmer Like to Thee I Ne'er Did See
  22. With That Dearling We've Made Merry All the Night
  23. Still the Lover's Heart Doth Burn and Burn Alway
