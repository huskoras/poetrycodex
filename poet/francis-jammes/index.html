French

Francis Jammes

1868–1938

Francis Jammes (1868–1938) was a French poet born at Tournay in the Hautes-Pyrénées and long resident at Orthez and Hasparren in the Béarn and the Basque country. He began writing in the late 1880s, was encouraged by Mallarmé and André Gide, and became known for plain, rural verse in which donkeys, kitchens, hedgerows and village Sundays are set down in a deliberately unassuming voice, often in irregular lines. De l’Angélus de l’aube à l’Angélus du soir (1898) and Le Deuil des primevères (1901) established him.

Around 1905 Jammes returned to Catholicism after a friendship with Paul Claudel, and his later work is increasingly devotional. His prayers, among them the well-known ‘Prière pour aller au Paradis avec les ânes’, join sincere piety to the same humble country imagery. The poems here are given in French from the Wikisource transcriptions of the Mercure de France editions of the two books.
