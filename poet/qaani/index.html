Qa'ani of Shiraz

1808–1854

Qa'ani of Shiraz (قاآنی شیرازی, Mirza Habibullah Shirazi, 1808–1854; born in 1223 and died in 1270 of the Hijri lunar calendar) was a Persian court poet of the Qajar period. Born in Shiraz, he studied there and in Mashhad, and came to Tehran, where a poem in praise of Fath-Ali Shah won him the title Mujtahid al-Shu'ara. He served Muhammad Shah and Naser al-Din Shah and their ministers, and is buried in the shrine of Shah Abdol-Azim at Rey.

He is the best-known master of the bazgasht, the nineteenth-century return to the manner of the great classical qasida writers such as Farrokhi, Manuchehri and Anvari. His Divan runs to some twenty thousand couplets, and he also wrote a prose work in the manner of Sa'di's Golestan, the Parishan. This selection gives the first fifty qasidas of his Divan in the order of Ganjoor, each complete: praise of the Prophet and the Imams, then of the shahs, princes and ministers of his day. Each bayt stands on one line, the two hemistichs separated by a wide space. The Persian text is public domain.
