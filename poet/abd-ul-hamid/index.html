Arabic & Persian

Abd-ul-Hamid

b. c. 1650–1675; d. c. 1732 (Raverty)

Abd-ul-Hamid was born in the Mohmand village of Mashu Khel near Peshawar and was brought up for the priesthood. Raverty could not fix his birth year but places it about the middle of the last half of the seventeenth century, and gives his death as about A.D. 1732 on village testimony.

Raverty calls him the cynical poet of the Afghans, compared with Sa'di in Persian, and says that Persian critics knew him as 'Hamid the Hair-splitter'. His odes, from the Diwan 'Dur-o-Marjan' ('Pearls and Corals'), mix moral satire and contempt for worldly vanity with Sufi doctrine.
