Thomas D'Urfey

1653–1723

Thomas D'Urfey (1653-1723) was an English songwriter, poet and playwright, born in Exeter of Huguenot descent. He came to London to study law but turned to the stage and wrote some thirty plays, among them Madam Fickle (1676) and The Comical History of Don Quixote (1694-96). He was a familiar figure at the courts of Charles II, James II, William III and Anne, and was known above all as a writer and singer of comic and bawdy songs.

New Poems, consisting of Satyrs, Elegies and Odes, together with a Choice Collection of the Newest Court Songs (1690), from which this selection is drawn, mixes verse satires, prologues and epilogues, epithalamia and elegies with songs, several of them set by Henry Purcell and John Blow. D'Urfey later gathered songs, his own and others', into Wit and Mirth: or Pills to Purge Melancholy (1698-1720), the best-known song collection of its age.
