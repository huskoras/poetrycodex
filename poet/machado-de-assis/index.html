Machado de Assis

1839–1908

Joaquim Maria Machado de Assis (1839–1908) was born in Rio de Janeiro, the son of a mulatto house-painter, and rose from poverty and almost no formal schooling to become the greatest figure of Brazilian letters. Working first as a typesetter and proof-reader, then as a civil servant, he wrote chronicles, short stories and the novels for which he is chiefly remembered, among them Memórias póstumas de Brás Cubas (1881) and Dom Casmurro (1899). He was the first president of the Brazilian Academy of Letters, founded in 1897.

He was a poet before he was a novelist. His verse appeared in four books, Crisálidas (1864), Falenas (1870), Americanas (1875) and Ocidentais (1880), gathered by the author himself in Poesias completas (Rio de Janeiro: H. Garnier, 1901). The texts here follow that 1901 edition, in its printed spelling, beginning with the philosophical poems of Occidentaes and going on through Americanas, Phalenas and Chrysalidas; his translations (such as O Corvo, after Poe) are left out.
