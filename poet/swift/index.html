Jonathan Swift

1667–1745

Jonathan Swift (1667–1745), Dean of St Patrick’s Cathedral in Dublin, is remembered above all for his prose satires, but his verse — plain-spoken, often savage, sometimes tender — was equally his own invention.

Poems like A Description of a City Shower and The Lady’s Dressing-Room turned unflinching, unglamorous observation into satire, while Cadenus and Vanessa and the Stella poems show a gentler, more private side of a famously guarded man.
