Vittoria Colonna

1490–1547

Vittoria Colonna (1490–1547), Marchioness of Pescara, was born into the great Roman family of the Colonna and was married as a girl to the soldier Ferrante d’Avalos. After his death in 1525, following wounds received at the battle of Pavia, she lived largely in retirement, at Ischia and in convents in Orvieto, Viterbo and Rome.

She is among the first women to publish a book of poems in Italian, and her Rime, which mourn her husband and later turn to religious themes, won the praise of Ariosto and Bembo. She was a close friend of Michelangelo, who addressed poems to her. The sonnets here are taken from the 1760 edition of her Rime, in its spelling and in its order.
