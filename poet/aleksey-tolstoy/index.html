Russian & Slavic

Aleksey Konstantinovich Tolstoy

1817–1875

Count Aleksey Konstantinovich Tolstoy (1817–1875) was a Russian poet, novelist and dramatist, born in St Petersburg into the high aristocracy and brought up largely in the Ukrainian countryside. He served at court and was close to the future Alexander II, yet he kept clear of the political camps of his day, a stance he states in his poem about being a guest in two camps and a fighter for neither.

He is best known for his lyrics, many of them set to music by Tchaikovsky, Rimsky-Korsakov and others, and for his historical ballads and byliny drawn on old Rus. His dramatic trilogy on Ivan the Terrible, Tsar Fyodor and Boris Godunov and his historical novel Prince Serebryany are not part of this selection. With his cousins the Zhemchuzhnikov brothers he also stood behind the invented poet Kozma Prutkov, whose verse is likewise left out here.

He died in 1875 at his estate of Krasny Rog, in the Bryansk region, from an overdose of morphine taken against severe headaches. These texts come from Russian Wikisource, one page per poem; several are in modern spelling, without ё.
