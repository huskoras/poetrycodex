Fernando Pessoa

1888–1935

Fernando Pessoa (1888–1935) was born in Lisbon and spent part of his childhood in Durban, South Africa, where he was schooled in English; he returned to Lisbon at seventeen and earned his living as a commercial translator and correspondent. He was a central figure of Portuguese modernism, taking part in the magazines Orpheu (1915), Athena (1924–25) and Presença, and he wrote under his own name and under a number of 'heteronyms', imagined authors with their own biographies and styles, chief among them Alberto Caeiro, Ricardo Reis and Álvaro de Campos.

Almost all of his work appeared in magazines or remained in manuscript at his death. Mensagem (1934), a book of forty-four poems on Portuguese history and destiny in three parts, was the only Portuguese-language book of verse he published in his lifetime. He also published English verse in Lisbon: 35 Sonnets (1918) and the pamphlets of English Poems (1921). This selection is limited to the poems he saw into print himself, namely Mensagem, the Athena printings, and his English books.
