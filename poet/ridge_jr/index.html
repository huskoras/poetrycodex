John Rollin Ridge

1827–1867

John Rollin Ridge (1827–1867), known among the Cherokee as Yellow Bird (Cheesquatalawny), was born in the Cherokee Nation in Georgia on 19 March 1827, the son of the Cherokee leader John Ridge and his wife Sarah Northrup, and the grandson of Major Ridge. After the removal of the Cherokees westward, his father was killed in 1839 in the bitter feud that divided the nation, and the family settled in Arkansas, where he was educated in part by missionaries and in New England schools.

He went to California about 1850 and worked there chiefly as a journalist and newspaper editor. His best-known work is the novel The Life and Adventures of Joaquin Murieta (1854), a romance of the California bandit, usually counted as the first novel by a Native American author. The verse he wrote, much of it in his youth, appeared in the California newspapers and magazines.

He died in Grass Valley, California, in October 1867. His widow collected his verse in Poems (San Francisco: Henry Payot & Co., 1868), published after his death; it includes the long poems “Mount Shasta” and “California” and the lyric “To a Star Seen at Twilight”.
