Luis de Góngora

1561–1627

Luis de Góngora y Argote (Córdoba, 1561 – 1627) was a Spanish priest and poet of the Golden Age, the leading figure of the style called culteranismo. He studied at Salamanca, took minor orders, and held a prebend at Córdoba cathedral before moving to Madrid in 1617 as a chaplain to Philip III.

His early ballads and letrillas, popular and satirical, were widely sung; the sonnets range from love and moral reflection to biting court satire, with "Mientras por competir con tu cabello" among the most famous poems in the language. From 1612–13 he wrote the dense, Latinate Fábula de Polifemo y Galatea and the Soledades, which provoked a long literary quarrel with Quevedo and others and shaped Spanish poetry for centuries.
