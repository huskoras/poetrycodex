Nakahara Chūya

1907–1937

中原中也 (Nakahara Chūya, 1907–1937) was born in Yamaguchi Prefecture. He moved to Kyoto and then, in 1925, to Tokyo, where he read the French Symbolists, translated Rimbaud and Verlaine, and wrote in both classical and colloquial Japanese verse forms. He died in Kamakura at the age of thirty.

His first book, 山羊の歌 (Yagi no uta, "Song of the Goat"), appeared in 1934. After the death of his young son Fumiya in 1936 he prepared a second collection, 在りし日の歌 (Arishi hi no uta, "Songs of Days Gone By"), which was published in 1938, after his own death. Both books are given here complete, in book order. The text is that of Aozora Bunko, with the reading aids (ruby) and editorial notes removed; the afterword to the second book is left out.
