John Gay

1685–1732

John Gay (1685–1732) was an English poet and dramatist, born in Barnstaple, Devon. A friend of Pope, Swift and Arbuthnot, he wrote Trivia, a mock-georgic on walking the streets of London, and The Beggar’s Opera (1728), a ballad opera that was a great popular success.

His Fables, published in two series in 1727 and 1738, are short verse stories in which animals speak and act as foils to human folly. They were widely read for generations and are the part of his verse that still has readers.
