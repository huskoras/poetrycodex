Philippe Desportes

1546–1606

Philippe Desportes (1546–1606) was born at Chartres and made his career at the court of the last Valois kings. He was secretary to Henri d'Anjou, the future Henri III, and followed him to Poland in 1573; in later life he held several rich abbeys, among them Tiron and Bonport, and he died at Bonport in 1606.

His Amours de Diane appeared in 1573, followed by the Amours d'Hippolyte and the Cléonice, and the three were gathered with his other pieces in the Premières œuvres. Writing after Ronsard, he drew freely on Petrarch and the Italian sonneteers and was for a generation the most admired love poet in France, until Malherbe and his followers turned against him. These poems follow the old-spelling text of the 1600 edition.
