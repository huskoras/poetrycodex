Majnun Layla (Qays ibn al-Mulawwah)

d. c. 688

Qays ibn al-Mulawwah, called Majnun Layla (“Layla’s madman”), is the best-known of the Udhri poets, the school of Bedouin love poets of the Hijaz and Najd in the early Umayyad period who made unfulfilled, chaste passion their single theme. Tradition places him in the tribe of Banu ‘Amir and says that he loved his cousin Layla from boyhood, that her father refused him the marriage, and that he wandered the desert in grief until he died, c. 688. His name became a byword for the lover driven out of his wits, and Persian, Turkish and Urdu poets from Nizami onward retold the story.

His existence and his authorship are disputed. Medieval Arab critics already doubted whether Majnun was a historical person, and the anecdotes about him, collected in the Kitab al-Aghani and elsewhere, belong to storytelling as much as to biography. The diwan that bears his name was assembled and enlarged by later transmitters, and many of its short pieces circulate with variant wording and rival attributions to other poets. The texts here are the pieces transcribed on Arabic Wikisource under his name; they keep that source’s spelling and vowel marks, and the attribution of each is traditional rather than certain.
