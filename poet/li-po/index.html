Li Po

701–762

Li Po (701–762) is the most famous name in Chinese literature. A wanderer and a lover of wine, he was for a time a favourite at the court of the Emperor Ming Huang, but left it for a life as a guest of governors and mandarins, and was later swept up in the rebellion of An Lu-shan and imprisoned. He appears in two of the books drawn on here.

The poems here are Arthur Waley’s versions in More Translations from the Chinese (1919); L. Cranmer-Byng’s versions in A Lute of Jade (1909), given with the poet’s name as spelled in those books.
