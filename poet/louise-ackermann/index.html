Louise Ackermann

1813–1890

Louise-Victorine Ackermann, born Choquet, was born in Paris in 1813 and spent part of her youth studying in Berlin, where she read German literature and philosophy. In 1843 she married the scholar Paul Ackermann, who died three years later; she lived on as a widow, for most of her life at Nice, where she died in 1890.

She is remembered for philosophical verse of a severe, pessimistic cast, written in lucid classical stanzas and often close in temper to the Parnassian poets. Her Premières poésies (1871) are mostly lyrics and mythological pieces; her Poésies philosophiques (1874), with Prométhée, Pascal, Le Positivisme and La Guerre among them, set a godless nature and human defiance against inherited faith.

This selection follows the text of her Œuvres published by Lemerre (1885), as transcribed on French Wikisource.
