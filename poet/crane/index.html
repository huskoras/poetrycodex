American

Stephen Crane

1871–1900

Stephen Crane (1871–1900) was an American novelist, short-story writer and poet, born in Newark, New Jersey. He is best remembered for the Civil War novel The Red Badge of Courage (1895) and for stories such as "The Open Boat", and worked as a journalist and war correspondent.

The Black Riders and Other Lines, his first book of verse, appeared in 1895. Its short, untitled, numbered pieces in unrhymed free verse, often parables addressing God, institutions and the self, were unusual for their time and are often compared with Emily Dickinson's. Crane died of tuberculosis in 1900, aged twenty-eight.
