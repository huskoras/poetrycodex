Romantic

Thomas Campbell

1777–1844

Thomas Campbell (1777–1844) was a Scottish poet, born in Glasgow, the youngest of eleven children. He made his name at twenty-one with The Pleasures of Hope (1799), a didactic poem on hope, liberty and the wrongs of Poland and Africa, and followed it with the Pennsylvania tale Gertrude of Wyoming (1809).

He is best remembered for his patriotic and martial lyrics — ‘Ye Mariners of England’, ‘The Battle of the Baltic’, ‘Hohenlinden’ — and for the ballads ‘Lord Ullin’s Daughter’ and ‘Lochiel’s Warning’. He was also an early promoter of University College London and served as Lord Rector of Glasgow University; he died at Boulogne and is buried in Westminster Abbey.
