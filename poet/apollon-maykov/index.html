Russian & Slavic

Apollon Maykov

1821–1897

Аполлон Николаевич Майков (1821–1897) was a Russian poet, born in Moscow into the family of the painter Nikolai Maykov; his brothers Valerian and Leonid became a critic and a literary scholar. He studied at the University of St Petersburg, published his first book of verse in 1842, and travelled in Italy and Western Europe in the years that followed. From 1852 he served in the censorship committee for foreign books, and he lived in St Petersburg until his death in 1897.

Maykov is the Russian poet of antiquity and of the pictorial: Greek and Roman scenes, Italian landscapes, the polished short lyric of mood and nature, set beside historical ballads and poems on faith and history. His two-volume Стихотворения of 1858, published at the expense of Count Kushelev-Bezborodko, gathered the work of his first period, arranged in cycles such as На волѣ (In the Open), Фантазіи and Дома.

This selection takes his own poems from the pre-reform-spelling pages of Russian Wikisource, mostly from the 1858 Стихотворения, with three poems from earlier and later printings (1835, 1860 and 1913). The old orthography (ѣ, і, ъ) is kept as printed. His translations, including those of Heine and of modern Greek songs, are left out.
