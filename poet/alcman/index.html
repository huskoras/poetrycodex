Ancient Greek & Roman

Alcman

fl. late 7th century BC

Alcman was a choral poet of Sparta in the later seventh century BC, and the earliest of the Greek lyric poets whose work survives in any quantity. Ancient tradition made him a native of Lydian Sardis who came to Sparta as a slave and was freed, though some critics held him a Spartan born. His songs were written for choirs of girls and young men to sing at Spartan festivals, and the Alexandrian scholars gathered them into six books.

His best-known work is the Partheneion or Maiden-Song, a processional hymn for a chorus of girls in honour of Orthia, with its praise of the choir-leaders Agido and Hagesichora. It was lost until a first-century papyrus was found at Saqqara and acquired by the Louvre in 1855. Apart from that long fragment his poetry survives in short quotations preserved by grammarians, scholiasts and writers such as Athenaeus.

The translations here are by J. M. Edmonds, from his Lyra Graeca, Volume I (Loeb Classical Library, 1922), which prints the surviving Poems and Fragments in his own order and numbering, with restorations in square brackets. The Partheneion is given as one poem and the shorter pieces under his fragment numbers. Fragments that survive only as single words, names or grammarians' glosses are not included.
