Cai Yan (Cai Wenji)

c. 177–after 206

Cai Yan (蔡琰, courtesy name Wenji, earlier Zhaoji) was the daughter of the scholar and calligrapher Cai Yong, born in the Eastern Han dynasty around 177. In the chaos that followed the fall of Dong Zhuo she was carried off by Xiongnu raiders, lived for some twelve years among the Southern Xiongnu, where she bore two sons, and was ransomed by Cao Cao, who had been a friend of her father, and returned to the Han lands. She then married Dong Si, and the biography of "Dong Si's wife" in the Hou Han shu records her recitation of her father's lost books from memory.

The two "Poems of Grief and Indignation" (悲憤詩), one in five-character lines and one in sao style, are preserved in the Hou Han shu (juan 84) and give her own account of the capture, the exile and the parting from her children. The "Eighteen Songs of a Nomad Flute" (胡笳十八拍), printed in the Chuci jizhu, retells the same story in eighteen stanzas; its attribution to Cai Yan is traditional and has long been disputed, and some scholars place its composition later. Texts here follow Chinese Wikisource, in traditional characters.
