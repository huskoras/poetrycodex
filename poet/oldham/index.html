John Oldham

1653–1683

John Oldham (1653–1683) was an English satirist, the son of a Nonconformist minister in Gloucestershire. After St Edmund Hall, Oxford, he worked as an usher (assistant schoolmaster) at a school in Croydon. His Satyrs upon the Jesuits (1681), written in the vein of Juvenal, made his name, and he followed it with imitations and paraphrases of Latin and French poets, among them Juvenal, Horace, Boileau, Catullus and Anacreon.

The 1683 volume from which these poems are taken, Poems, and Translations, is anonymous on its title page (‘by the author of the Satyrs upon the Jesuits’); the author is identified as John Oldham by the TCP/ESTC catalogue record. He died of smallpox at the age of thirty. Dryden, who admired his work, wrote the elegy ‘To the Memory of Mr. Oldham’, and the archive also holds Gould’s elegy on him.
