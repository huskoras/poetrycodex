Abu al-Qasim al-Shabbi

1909–1934

Abu al-Qasim al-Shabbi (1909–1934) was a Tunisian poet, born in the oasis town of Tozeur in the south of the country. He was educated at the Zaytuna University in Tunis and went on to study law, and he came to be counted among the foremost Romantic poets of the modern Arabic revival.

His poems, gathered after his death under the title Aghani al-Hayat (Songs of Life), are marked by a longing for beauty and freedom, by grief and doubt, and by a fierce, hopeful call to rise. He wrote in classical metres but with a lyrical immediacy that set him apart from the neoclassical poets of his day.

He died in Tunis in October 1934, at the age of twenty-five. His poem The Will to Live, whose closing verses are sung in Tunisia's national anthem, has been chanted in Arab protest movements ever since. The texts here are taken from the Arabic Wikisource transcription of Aghani al-Hayat.
