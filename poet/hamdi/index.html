Hamdi (Hamdullah Hamdi)

1449–1509

Hamdullah Hamdi (Hamdi, 'the Praiser') was the youngest of the twelve sons of the saint and physician Aq Shemseddin, a learned Sufi descended from Suhrawardi, who made his home at Güynük near Bolu and died there in 1459. Hamdi was born there in 1449, studied for the learned profession, and left it, after serving as a college principal at Bursa, to study mysticism and write poetry. He was a contemporary of Jami and is said to have corresponded with him.

He wrote a set of five mesnevis, among them a Leyla and Mejnun (finished in 1499 or 1500), a Tuhfet-ul-ʿUshshaq ('Gift to Lovers'), a Mevlid, and above all the Yusuf u Zelikha, the romance of Joseph and Potiphar's wife, which for generations was among the best-loved poems in Turkish. By tradition he lived in modest circumstances, copying out the Yusuf u Zelikha for sale; he died in March 1509 and was buried at Güynük beside his father. The pieces here are E. J. W. Gibb's English verse renderings (1902).

Poems (11)

  1. The Hunting of the Stranger Wolf by the Brethren of Joseph, and their slandering of the same
  2. The Bitter Wailing of Zelikha in her First Passion
  3. Joseph (on whom be Peace!) seeth his Father and Mother in a Vision and prayeth God to reunite him with them
  4. Mejnun speaketh to the Crow
  5. The Death of Mejnun
  6. Gabriel announceth his Mission to Muhammed
  7. The Merchant's Son seeth the Daughter of the Vezir
  8. Of the Complexion
  9. Of Laughing
  10. Of the Ear
  11. Of Movement
