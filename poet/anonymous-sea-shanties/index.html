Victorian

Anonymous (Sea Shanties)

traditional; collected 1914

Sea shanties, or chanteys, were the work-songs of the merchant sailing ships. A chanteyman sang the verse, usually improvising or drawing on stock lines, and the crew answered with the refrain as they hauled a rope or walked the capstan round, so that their efforts fell together. The tunes and words were passed from sailor to sailor and altered in the passing, and no author can be named for any of them.

The texts here are the words of the chanteys in Cecil J. Sharp's English Folk-Chanteys (1914), which he took down from old sailors living in retirement in Somerset, Cornwall, Bristol and London, most of them from John Short of Watchet. Sharp's book prints each refrain with the first verse and gives later verses only as their varying lines, and the texts follow it, with each refrain written out once. The second version of Leave Her Johnny, whose words repeat the first, is omitted.
