Victorian

Isaac Williams

1802–1865

Isaac Williams (1802–1865) was a Welsh-born clergyman and poet, a Fellow of Trinity College, Oxford, and a student and disciple of John Keble. A member of the Oxford Movement, he contributed verses to Lyra Apostolica and wrote Tract 80, “On Reserve in Communicating Religious Knowledge.”

His best-known verse is devotional. The Cathedral (1838) is a cycle of short poems in which the parts of a church — porch, cloisters, nave, transepts, choir, pillars and windows — stand for the Christian life and the Church of England. The Baptistery (1842), published anonymously “by the author of The Cathedral,” sets a long poem to each of twenty-three engraved “images” that follow the baptized soul’s way to eternal life.
