Charles-Hubert Millevoye

1782–1816

Charles-Hubert Millevoye (1782–1816) was a French poet, born at Abbeville in Picardy, who died in Paris at the age of thirty-three. He began publishing very young, won prizes from the Académie française and from the Académie des Jeux floraux of Toulouse, and wrote narrative and occasional verse as well as translations; he is remembered chiefly for his elegies.

His best-known poem, “La Chute des feuilles” (1811), is the elegy of a dying young man who watches the autumn leaves fall; it was long learned by heart in French schools. Writing on the threshold of Romanticism, Millevoye is often counted among the poets whose melancholy elegiac manner anticipated Lamartine. His Œuvres complètes were edited by Pongerville in 1837.
