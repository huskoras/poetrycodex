Russian & Slavic

Alexander Blok

1880–1921

Александр Александрович Блок (1880–1921), born in Saint Petersburg and grandson of the botanist Andrey Beketov, was the foremost poet of Russian Symbolism. His first book, Стихи о Прекрасной Даме (“Verses about the Beautiful Lady”, 1905), turned his love for Lyubov Mendeleeva, whom he married in 1903, into a mystical cult of the Eternal Feminine in the manner of Vladimir Solovyov.

The later verse grew darker and more urban: the cycles Город, Страшный мир (“The Terrible World”, 1909–1916), Ямбы and Родина are among the central works of the Silver Age, and “Ночь, улица, фонарь, аптека” is one of the best-known quatrains in the language. He welcomed the revolution of 1917; Двенадцать and Скифы, both of January 1918, were his last great poems, and he died in Petrograd on 7 August 1921.
