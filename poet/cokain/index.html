Metaphysical & Cavalier

Sir Aston Cokain

1608–1684

Sir Aston Cokain (also spelt Cokayne or Cockayne, 1608–1684) was a Derbyshire gentleman, Royalist and minor poet and dramatist, born at Ashbourne Hall. He was educated at Trinity College, Cambridge, and travelled in France and Italy before settling into the life of a country squire. He was created a baronet in 1642 and sided with the King in the Civil War, after which his estates were much reduced.

Cokain moved in literary circles and was a friend and admirer of Philip Massinger and John Fletcher, on whose shared grave in Southwark he wrote an epitaph, and a kinsman of Charles Cotton. His chief book, A Chain of Golden Poems (1658), gathers epigrams, love and funeral elegies, songs, eclogues, verse letters and a satire, together with two comedies, The Obstinate Lady and Trappolin Suppos'd a Prince. It is a lively record of Cavalier manners and of the Derbyshire gentry around him.
