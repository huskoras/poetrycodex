Ancient Greek & Roman

Gaius Valerius Flaccus

d. c. AD 90

Gaius Valerius Flaccus was a Roman epic poet of the Flavian age. Almost nothing is known of his life: he is not mentioned by Pliny or Tacitus, and the one ancient notice, in Quintilian’s survey of Latin authors written about AD 90, says that Rome had lately lost much in him. His name Setinus Balbus in some manuscripts suggests a family from Setia in Latium. He is usually thought to have begun his poem under Vespasian, whom he praises in the proem, and to have died before it was finished.

His only surviving work is the Argonautica, an epic in eight books on the voyage of Jason and the Argonauts to Colchis for the Golden Fleece. It follows the plot of Apollonius of Rhodes but is a free Latin reworking, in Virgil’s manner, and gives Medea and her love for Jason a large share of the later books. The poem breaks off in the middle of Book VIII, in the Argonauts’ flight from Colchis, and was little read until its rediscovery in the fifteenth century.

Major Works (2)

  1. The Argonautica — translated by H. G. Blomfield (1916)
  2. Argonautica — Epic poem, c. AD 70–90
