Purananuru Poets

c. 1st–3rd century CE

The Purananuru (புறநானூறு, “the four hundred poems of the outer sphere”) is one of the eight great anthologies of Sangam literature, the earliest surviving poetry in Tamil, and is dated to roughly the first to third centuries CE. Its 400 poems are puram poems: songs of war, kingship and patronage, of the grief of the battlefield, of charity and of hard-won wisdom, as against the akam poems of private love gathered in companion anthologies.

The poems are the work of well over a hundred named poets, among them Kapilar, Avvaiyar, Paranar and Kaniyan Pungundranar, and they address the Chera, Chola and Pandya kings and the lesser chieftains of the Tamil country. Among the best known is poem 192, whose opening, “yatum ure yavarum kelir”, declares every town one’s own and every person kin. Each record here gives the poet, the patron addressed and the classical tinai and turai categories in its note. The Tamil text is taken from Tamil Wikisource (CC BY-SA); the poems themselves are in the public domain. Numbers missing from the transcription are not supplied.
