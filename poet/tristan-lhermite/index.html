French

Tristan L’Hermite

1601–1655

François Tristan L’Hermite (1601–1655), who styled himself Tristan, was a French poet and dramatist of the reign of Louis XIII. Born in the Marche, he spent a restless youth as a page and courtier before finding patrons in the households of Gaston d’Orléans and the duc de Guise, and in 1649 he was elected to the Académie française.

His verse, gathered in Les Plaintes d’Acante (1633), Les Amours de Tristan (1638), La Lyre (1641) and Les Vers héroïques (1648), joins the Baroque taste for metamorphosis, mirrors and shifting light to an intimate, melancholy tone. ‘Le Promenoir des deux amants’, an ode of a lovers’ walk beside a grotto, is the best known of his poems; he also wrote tragedies, among them La Mariane (1636) and La Mort de Sénèque (1645).
