Théodore de Banville

1823–1891

Théodore Faullain de Banville (Moulins, 14 March 1823 – Paris, 13 March 1891) was a French poet, critic and dramatist. He came to Paris as a boy and made his name with Les Cariatides (1842) and Les Stalactites (1846), verse of great metrical brilliance written under the influence of Victor Hugo and the Pléiade.

The Odes funambulesques (1857), comic and fantastic odes in the manner of the Paris stage and carnival, and Les Exilés (1867) confirmed his reputation as a virtuoso of rhyme. His Petit traité de poésie française (1872) became a handbook for the Parnassians and later poets; he also revived the ballade and other fixed forms. This selection gives poems from Les Stalactites and the Odes funambulesques, from the Wikisource transcription of the Lemerre editions.
