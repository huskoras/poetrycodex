Abu Nuwas

c. 756–c. 814

Abu Nuwas (أبو نواس), whose full name was al-Hasan ibn Hani' al-Hakami, was born around 756 in the region of Ahwaz in south-western Iran, grew up in Basra and Kufa, and made his career in Baghdad, where he became a favourite poet at the court of the Abbasid caliphs Harun al-Rashid and al-Amin. He died in Baghdad around 814. He is the most celebrated Arabic poet of wine, who turned the old desert ode, with its halting at the abandoned campsite, into a joke against itself and set the tavern in its place.

These poems are taken from the Arabic text transcribed on Wikisource and are given in the original, with the order of the old divans: first the wine poems (khamriyyat), then love poems (ghazal), then a few poems of repentance and mortality (zuhdiyyat). Poems that turned on coarse or obscene matter, which form a large part of his reputation and of the transmitted collection, and his satires have been left out.

  152. في الحبِّ رَوْعاتٌ وتعذيبُ (Fī al-ḥubb rawʻāt wa-taʻdhīb)
  153. لَمّا جَفَاني الحبِيبُ، وامتَنَعَتْ (Lammā jafānī al-ḥabīb)
  154. لله طَيفٌ سرَى فأرّقَني (Lillāh ṭayf sará fa-arraqanī)
  155. ما أقْبَحَ الهجرَ بالمحبّ، ومَا (Mā aqbaḥ al-hajr bi-al-muḥibb)
  156. ما لي وللعاذِلاتِ (Mā lī wa-lil-ʻādhilāt)
  157. مُتَيّمٌ القَلبِ، مُعَنّاهُ (Mutayyam al-qalb muʻannāhu)
  158. وقائلةٍ لي: كيْفَ كنْتَ تُريدُ (Wa-qāʼilah lī kayfa kunta turīd)
  159. وقَصْرِيّةٍ أبْصَرْتُها، فهويتُها (Wa-qaṣriyyah abṣartuhā fa-hawaytuhā)
  160. يا قلبُ يا خائنَ الحبيبِ (Yā qalb yā khāʼin al-ḥabīb)
  161. عَيْني! ألومُكِ لا ألو (ʻAynī alūmuki lā alū)
  162. أيا رُبّ وَجهٍ، في التّرابِ، عَتيقِ (Ayā rubba wajh fī al-turāb ʻatīq)
  163. سبحانَ علاَّمِ الغيوبِ (Subḥān ʻallām al-ghuyūb)
  164. الموتُ منّا قريبٌ (Al-mawt minnā qarīb)
  165. دَبّ فيَّ الفَناءُ سُفْلاً وعُلْوَا (Dabba fiyya al-fanāʼ)
  166. أيّةُ نارٍ قدَحَ القادِحُ (Ayyat nār qadaḥa al-qādiḥ)
