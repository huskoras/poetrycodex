Russian & Slavic

Vladimir Solovyov

1853–1900

Vladimir Sergeyevich Solovyov (1853–1900) was the leading Russian religious philosopher of his century: a Moscow professor's son, a lecturer, critic and essayist, and the thinker who gave Russian thought its doctrine of Sophia, Divine Wisdom, and of the unity of all things. His verse is slight in bulk, written in bursts from the 1870s to 1900, and almost all of it turns on one vision, the Eternal Feminine glimpsed in Moscow, London and the Egyptian desert, which he set down in the autobiographical poem Three Meetings (Три свидания, 1898).

The poems took on a second life after his death. Aleksandr Blok and Andrei Bely, and with them the younger Symbolists, read his lyrics as scripture: the Beautiful Lady of Blok's early verse and much of the Symbolists' language of dawn, azure and the world-soul descend from him. He also wrote comic verse and parodies, a vein not included here.
