Italian

Gabriele D'Annunzio

1863–1938

Gabriele D'Annunzio (1863–1938) was born at Pescara in the Abruzzo and educated at the Cicognini school in Prato. His first poems, Primo vere, appeared in 1879 when he was sixteen. He moved to Rome in 1881, wrote poetry, stories and journalism, and with the novel Il piacere (1889) became the leading figure of Italian decadence and aestheticism. He later wrote plays and novels, served in the First World War as a soldier and airman, led the occupation of Fiume in 1919–1920, and spent his last years at the Vittoriale degli Italiani at Gardone Riviera, where he died on 1 March 1938.

Alcyone, published in 1903 as the third book of his Laudi del cielo del mare della terra e degli eroi, is a poetic diary of a summer on the coast of Tuscany, between the Arno, the Versilia pinewoods and the Apuan Alps. Its poems follow the season from the first heat to the first autumn migration, in the company of the figure Ermione, and mingle the landscape with Greek myth. It contains his best-known lyrics, among them La sera fiesolana and La pioggia nel pineto, whose verse imitates the sound of rain on leaves, and it is generally regarded as the high point of his poetry.
