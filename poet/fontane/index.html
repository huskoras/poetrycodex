Theodor Fontane

1819–1898

Theodor Fontane (30 December 1819, Neuruppin – 20 September 1898, Berlin) trained as a pharmacist before turning to writing. He worked as a journalist, spent several years in England as a correspondent, and published the Wanderungen durch die Mark Brandenburg (1862–82).

His Gedichte first appeared in 1851, and his ballads on English, Scottish and Prussian history, among them Archibald Douglas and John Maynard, made him a leading German balladeer. From the 1870s he turned chiefly to the novel (Effi Briest, 1895; Der Stechlin, 1898).
