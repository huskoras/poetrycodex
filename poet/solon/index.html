Solon

c. 638 – c. 558 BC

Solon (c. 638 – c. 558 BC) was an Athenian statesman, lawgiver and poet. Elected archon around 594 BC, he was given power to settle the quarrel between the rich and the poor of Attica. His reforms cancelled debts and ended enslavement for debt, and he revised the laws of Athens. Later Greeks counted him among the Seven Sages.

Solon wrote verse in elegiac couplets, iambic trimeters and trochaic tetrameters, and used it to argue his political case before the Athenian people: for the recovery of Salamis, for good order (Eunomia) against greed and civil strife, and in defence of his own moderation. His longest surviving poem, the Elegy to the Muses, reflects on wealth, justice and the gods. Only fragments survive, preserved by later authors such as Plutarch and Aristotle. They are given here in Greek, as edited by Hiller and Crusius in the Anthologia Lyrica (Teubner, 1904), under the edition's own fragment numbers. Fragments of a single line, and a few whose text could not be read with confidence from the scans, are left out.
