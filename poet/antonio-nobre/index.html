António Nobre

1867–1900

António Nobre was born in Porto in 1867 and died in 1900, aged thirty-two, of tuberculosis. He studied law at Coimbra and then at the Sorbonne in Paris, where he lived in the Latin Quarter, and where he wrote most of the poems of his only book published in his lifetime, Só (Paris, 1892).

Só is a book of homesickness, childhood and illness, written in a colloquial, diminutive Portuguese full of Porto and Minho landscapes, old servants, bells and the sea. It was received at first with some bafflement at home, but Nobre came to be regarded as a central figure of Portuguese Saudosismo and an influence on Pessoa and later poets. His posthumous Despedidas appeared in 1902.
