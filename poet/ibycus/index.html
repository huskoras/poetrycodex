Ibycus

fl. c. 550 BC

Ibycus was a Greek lyric poet from Rhegium, the Greek colony at the toe of Italy. Ancient writers say he came from a good family there and might have become tyrant of his city, but chose instead to leave Italy and live as a poet. His dates are uncertain; he flourished around the middle of the sixth century BC.

He settled at Samos, where the tyrant Polycrates welcomed poets to his court, and he is said to have dedicated a book of verse to him. His songs, collected in antiquity in seven books, told of heroes and the Trojan story, but he was remembered above all for his poems of love, in which desire strikes like a storm and an ageing lover trembles like an old racehorse yoked once more to the chariot.

Only fragments survive, quoted by later writers and scholiasts, together with a damaged papyrus found at Oxyrhynchus. A famous legend tells that Ibycus was set upon by robbers near Corinth and, as he died, called on a flock of passing cranes as witnesses. When the cranes later appeared over the theatre, one of the murderers cried out, and so betrayed himself. Schiller made the story into a ballad, 'The Cranes of Ibycus'.
