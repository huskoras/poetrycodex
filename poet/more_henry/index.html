Metaphysical & Cavalier

Henry More

1614–1687

Henry More (1614–1687) was an English poet and philosopher, the most prolific of the Cambridge Platonists. Born at Grantham in Lincolnshire, he was educated at Eton and entered Christ's College, Cambridge, in 1631, where he remained as a Fellow for the rest of his life.

His poetry belongs to the first years of his Fellowship. Psychodia Platonica, or A Platonicall Song of the Soul (1642, much enlarged in 1647 as Philosophical Poems) sets out his Platonist doctrines of the soul's life, immortality and pre-existence in Spenserian stanzas, with a deliberate mix of allegory, dialogue and argument. Democritus Platonissans (1646) is a poetical essay on the infinity of worlds, and a small group of shorter poems and the Divine Hymns complete the verse.

The poems are given here as printed in 1647, in the old spelling, from the edition by Alexander B. Grosart (1878).
