French

Rémy Belleau

1528–1577

Rémy Belleau (c. 1528–1577) was a French poet of the Pléiade, born at Nogent-le-Rotrou in the Perche. Early in life he entered the household of René de Lorraine, marquis d'Elbeuf, and sailed with him on the Naples expedition of 1557; he later had charge of the education of the marquis's son Charles. He died in Paris in March 1577 and was buried in the church of the Grands-Augustins; Ronsard, his friend and the master of the Pléiade, wrote the epitaph that named him 'the painter of nature'.

His first book of note, the Petites inventions (1556), joined a French rendering of the Odes of Anacreon to short descriptive poems on small things (the butterfly, the oyster, the snail, the cherry). La Bergerie (1565) mixes prose and verse in a pastoral frame, and its song of April ('Avril') is the best-known French spring lyric of its century. The pieces here are given in the spelling of Marty-Laveaux's edition of 1878, which keeps the printers' u for v, i for j and 'Auril' for April; one sonnet is given in the modernised spelling of The Oxford Book of French Verse (1907).
