Vincenzo Monti

1754–1828

Vincenzo Monti (1754–1828) was born at Alfonsine, near Ravenna, and made his name in Rome in the 1770s and 1780s as a poet of the Arcadian Academy, with occasional odes, sonnets and blank-verse pieces on classical and mythological subjects. His Bassvilliana (In morte di Ugo Bassville, 1793), a poem in terza rima on the murder of a French envoy in Rome, brought him fame across Italy and shows his debt to Dante.

After the French invasion of Italy he moved to Milan and wrote for the Cisalpine Republic and the later Napoleonic regime, as in the poem on Lorenzo Mascheroni and the political odes, and he is also remembered for his verse translation of the Iliad. The poems here follow the order of the 1891 school edition of his Poesie, as transcribed on Italian Wikisource, in the original Italian; the editor's notes, variant readings and line numbers are left out.
