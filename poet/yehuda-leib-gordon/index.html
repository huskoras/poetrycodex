Judah Leib Gordon

1830–1892

Judah Leib Gordon (יהודה ליב גורדון, 1830–1892), known by the initials Yalag, was born in Vilna and became the leading Hebrew poet of the Haskalah, the Jewish Enlightenment in Russia. He worked as a teacher and later as secretary of the Jewish community of St Petersburg, and wrote satires, verse fables and long biblical and historical narrative poems.

His best-known poem, "Hakitsah ammi" (Awake, My People, 1866), urged Russian Jews to join the modern world, and the long narrative "Kotso shel yod" (1876) turned a dispute over Jewish law into a protest on the position of women. The poems here are in Hebrew with their vowel points, taken from the Dvir edition of his collected writings (Tel Aviv, 1959) as digitised by the volunteers of Project Ben-Yehuda (benyehuda.org), whose public-domain dump supplies the text.
