Indian

Surdas

c. 1478–c. 1583

Surdas (सूरदास) is the great blind bhakti poet of Krishna in Braj, the dialect of the region around Mathura and Vrindavan. Tradition dates him c. 1478 to c. 1583 and counts him among the devotees of the Vallabha school; the dates and most of the life are matters of tradition rather than record, and the poems that carry his signature ("Sur" or "Surdas") were gathered into the large collection called the Sur Sagar.

This selection is the Bhramargit-sar, the "essence of the Bee Songs", chosen from that collection and edited by the critic Ramchandra Shukla (1926). In the episode Krishna, now in Mathura, sends his friend Uddhava to console the cowherd women of Braj with a message of impersonal, ascetic knowledge; the gopis answer with wit, grief and reproach, turning their anger on a passing black bee (bhramar). Shukla's selection is the standard canon of these poems. Each is headed in the manuscripts by a raga, which is given in its note.
