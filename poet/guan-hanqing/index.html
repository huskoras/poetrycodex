Guan Hanqing

c. 1241 – c. 1320

Guan Hanqing (關漢卿) was a playwright of the Yuan dynasty, usually placed in Dadu (modern Beijing) and counted, with Ma Zhiyuan, Zheng Guangzu and Bai Pu, among the four great masters of Yuan drama. Few facts of his life are certain; the Register of Ghosts (錄鬼簿, c. 1330) lists him among the earliest of the zaju dramatists, and he is credited with some sixty plays, of which about eighteen survive.

His best-known play, The Injustice to Dou E (感天動地竇娥冤), tells of a wrongly executed young widow whose innocence is proved by wonders from heaven. He also wrote many songs (sanqu) in a lively, colloquial voice, and his work set a standard for Yuan drama that later playwrights took as a model.
