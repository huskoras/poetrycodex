al-Buhturi

821–897

al-Buhturi (أبو عبادة الوليد بن عبيد البحتري, 821–897) was an Abbasid poet born in the Syrian town of Manbij, of the Arab tribe of Tayyi. As a young man he went to Homs and met the poet Abu Tammam, who encouraged him and whom he is traditionally counted as a pupil of. He later made his career at Baghdad and Samarra, where he wrote praise poems for the caliph al-Mutawakkil, for the vizier al-Fath ibn Khaqan and for a long line of Abbasid officials and patrons, and he returned to Syria in his last years.

He is remembered for the smooth, musical, finely wrought style that critics set against the more intricate manner of Abu Tammam. His best-known poem is the Siniyya, in which he looks on the ruined palace of the Persian kings at Ctesiphon and weighs the fall of earthly power. The texts here are transcriptions from Arabic Wikisource, with the vocalisation of the source: the 120 longest poems of his diwan, each of three or more lines, chosen from the Wikisource author category.
