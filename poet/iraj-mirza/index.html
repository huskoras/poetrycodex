Arabic & Persian

Iraj Mirza

1874–1926

Iraj Mirza (ایرج میرزا, 1874–1926, with the pen-name Jalal al-Mamalik) was a Persian poet of the Qajar court and the Constitutional era, born at Tabriz into a family of the Qajar princes. He served as a court poet and later as an official in provincial administration, and he is remembered for a plain, speakable verse that brought colloquial Persian into the masnavi and the qit'a, with fables, satire and tender poems on mothers, children and teachers.

This selection comes from his Divan as given on Ganjoor: qasidas, ghazals, masnavis and qit'as ranked by the recitations and linked songs recorded there, with ties kept in Ganjoor's own order. The long Arif-nama and pieces of obscene or abusive content are left out. Each bayt stands on one line, the two hemistichs separated by a wide space. The Persian text is public domain.
