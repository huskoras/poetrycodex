American

Edwin Arlington Robinson

1869–1935

Edwin Arlington Robinson was born on 22 December 1869 at Head Tide, Maine, and grew up in the nearby town of Gardiner, which he later transformed into the 'Tilbury Town' of his verse. He attended Harvard as a special student from 1891 to 1893, then returned to Maine and later settled in New York, where he lived for years in poverty and worked briefly in the Custom House.

His early books, The Torrent and the Night Before (1896) and The Children of the Night (1897), are filled with compact character studies and sonnets, ballades and villanelles in a plain, ironic idiom. The Man Against the Sky (1916) established his reputation, and he went on to win three Pulitzer Prizes for poetry. He died in New York on 6 April 1935. The texts below come from the Project Gutenberg editions of The Children of the Night and The Man Against the Sky.
