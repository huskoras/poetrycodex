Kabir

c. 1440–1518

Kabir was a Hindi-language mystic poet and weaver of Benares (Varanasi), traditionally dated c. 1440 to 1518. Born, by tradition, into a Muslim weaving family, he is said to have received initiation from the Hindu teacher Ramananda, and he rejected the claims of both Hindu and Muslim orthodoxy in favour of direct devotion to a single, formless God.

His short songs and couplets were transmitted orally and later collected in the Bijak, the Adi Granth of the Sikhs and other anthologies. The English Songs of Kabir (1915) were rendered by Rabindranath Tagore with the help of Evelyn Underhill, who wrote the introduction; this edition gives the hundred songs of that volume.

The Gurmukhi originals of his hymns and couplets as preserved in the Guru Granth Sahib of the Sikhs (the Adi Granth, 1604) are now held here too, in the Sant Bhasha of the Bhagats written in Gurmukhi script.
