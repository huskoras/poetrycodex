Arabic & Persian

Hafiz Ibrahim

1872 – 1932

حافظ إبراهيم (Ḥāfiẓ Ibrāhīm, 1872–1932) was born on a Nile boat near Asyut in Upper Egypt, was orphaned young, and grew up in Cairo in straitened circumstances. After a short military career in the Egyptian army, which took him to the Sudan, he was pensioned off, and later worked in the Egyptian National Library (Dar al-Kutub). Known as "the Poet of the Nile" and "the people's poet", he stood beside Ahmad Shawqi, the court poet, as one of the two leading voices of the Arabic neoclassical revival, though his verse is closer to everyday Egyptian life, poverty, patriotism and social reform. He died in Cairo in 1932.

The poems given here are taken from his Diwan in the Arabic transcription of Arabic Wikisource, which is credited as the source of the text: odes on Egypt, the Arabic language and education, elegies, occasional pieces, and lighter verse on wine, love and friendship. Each bayt (verse line of two hemistichs) stands on one line. His Arabic version of Les Misérables is prose and is not included.
