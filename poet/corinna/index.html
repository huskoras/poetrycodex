Corinna

5th century BC (date disputed)

Corinna of Tanagra, a town of Boeotia near Thebes, was a lyric poet who wrote in the local Boeotian speech rather than the literary Doric or Aeolic of her rivals. Ancient tradition names her father as Acheloodorus and her mother as Hippocrateia, and calls her a pupil of the poetess Myrtis. Her work was gathered in five books in antiquity, which included narrative poems in the manner of the 'old-wives' tales' she claims to sing for the white-robed maidens of her native city, together with inscriptions and lyric nomes.

What survives is almost entirely local legend: the birth of Boeotus, the Seven against Thebes, the huntsman Orion and his daughters the Shuttle-Maidens, the daughters of Minyas, the marriages of the daughters of the river Asopus, and the contest between the mountains Helicon and Cithaeron, preserved on a papyrus now in Berlin. The rest is scraps quoted by ancient grammarians interested in her Boeotian dialect. Pausanias tells of a monument to her at Tanagra and a painting showing her binding on the headband won in a contest at Thebes.

A well-known tradition, reported by Plutarch, Aelian and Pausanias, makes her the rival of Pindar, whom she is said to have defeated in competition at Thebes (five times, according to Aelian), and whose lavish use of myth she is said to have mocked. Modern scholars dispute this: the papyrus texts point to a date well after Pindar, perhaps the third century BC, while others still place her in the early fifth. The extracts below are from J. M. Edmonds's Loeb edition of Lyra Graeca (vol. III, 1927), whose brackets and dots mark gaps in the papyrus.
