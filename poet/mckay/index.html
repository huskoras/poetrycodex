American

Claude McKay

1889–1948

Claude McKay (1889–1948) was born in Sunny Ville, Clarendon Parish, Jamaica, and published two volumes of dialect verse there before emigrating to the United States in 1912. He studied briefly at Tuskegee Institute and Kansas State, then settled in New York, where he became a leading figure in the literature of the Harlem Renaissance.

Harlem Shadows (1922), his first book of poems in standard English, gathered sonnets and lyrics of racial protest, urban life and homesickness for Jamaica, among them "If We Must Die" and "America". It carries an introduction by Max Eastman. McKay also wrote novels, including Home to Harlem (1928), and died in Chicago in 1948.
