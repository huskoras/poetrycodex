Edna St. Vincent Millay

1892–1950

Edna St. Vincent Millay (1892–1950) was an American poet and playwright, born in Rockland, Maine. She became famous at nineteen with "Renascence", published in 1912 and collected in Renascence and Other Poems (1917). She studied at Vassar College and afterwards lived in Greenwich Village, New York, where her lyrics and sonnets of love, loss and independence made her a leading voice of the 1920s.

Her collection The Harp-Weaver and Other Poems (1923) won the Pulitzer Prize for Poetry in 1923, the first awarded to a woman for poetry. This edition takes her poems from the London volume Poems (Martin Secker, 1923) and adds two poems from Renascence and Other Poems that Secker omitted. She died at her home at Steepletop, near Austerlitz, New York, in 1950.
