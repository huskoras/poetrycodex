Bernard Freeman Trotter

1890–1917

Bernard Freeman Trotter (1890–1917) was a Canadian poet and soldier, born in Toronto on 16 June 1890, the son of the Rev. Thomas Trotter, a professor at McMaster University who later became President of Acadia University at Wolfville, Nova Scotia, where the family moved when Bernard was five. Frail health took him to California for three years, at Montecito and Santa Barbara, where he wrote many of his early poems; from 1910 he studied at McMaster University in Toronto and edited its University Monthly.

He went to England in 1916 and served as a Second Lieutenant in the 11th Leicesters. He was killed in action in France on 7 May 1917, at the age of twenty-six. A Canadian Twilight and Other Poems of War and of Peace, chosen by others from the many poems he left, appeared in Toronto in 1917 with an introduction by W. S. W. McLay; it contains “The Poplars” and his last poem, “Ici Repose”, written in France in April 1917.
