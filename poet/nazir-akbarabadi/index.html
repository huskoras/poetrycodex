Nazir Akbarabadi

1735–1830

Nazir Akbarabadi (c. 1735–1830), born Wali Muhammad in Delhi and long resident in Agra (Akbarabad), was an Urdu poet who spent his life as a schoolteacher, tutoring the children of local families. He is remembered as the great poet of the common people of the city: the hawker, the kite-flyer, the beggar, the grain-seller and the festival crowd.

Besides ghazals, he wrote a large body of nazms, poems on a single theme, among them Admi Nama, Banjara Nama, the seasonal poems on the rains and the winter, and poems on Holi, Diwali, Eid and the figure of Guru Nanak. They are written in plain speech and cross the lines between Hindu and Muslim festival life. His work was long undervalued by tazkira writers, and his reputation rose only in the twentieth century.
