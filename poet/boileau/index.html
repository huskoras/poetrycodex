French

Nicolas Boileau

1636-1711

Nicolas Boileau (1636–1711), also known as Boileau-Despréaux, was born in Paris, the son of a clerk at the Parlement. He studied law and theology before turning to literature, and from the 1660s he circulated verse satires that attacked bad poets, bad taste and the follies of Parisian life, naming Chapelain, Cotin and others.

He was a friend of Molière, Racine and La Fontaine, and in 1677 he and Racine were appointed royal historiographers to Louis XIV. His mock-heroic Le Lutrin appeared in 1674, and in the same year L’Art poétique codified the rules of French classical taste in four cantos of alexandrines. He entered the Académie française in 1684. His Art poétique was translated into English by Sir William Soame and John Dryden in 1683 and widely imitated in England.
