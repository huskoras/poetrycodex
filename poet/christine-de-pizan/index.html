Christine de Pizan

1364–c. 1430

Christine de Pizan (1364–c. 1430) was born in Venice and brought up in Paris, where her father, the Bolognese physician and astrologer Tommaso da Pizzano, served Charles V. She married the royal notary Étienne du Castel in 1380 and was widowed about ten years later, left with three children and her mother to support. She turned to writing for her living, and became one of the first women in Europe to earn her living by her pen.

Her first poems were the lyrics of her widowhood, among them the Cent balades, with their refrains of solitude and loss, followed by virelais, rondeaux and the playful Jeux à vendre. She went on to the Epistre au dieu d'Amours, the Livre de la cité des dames, the Livre des trois vertus and a long series of didactic and political works, and wrote the Ditié de Jehanne d'Arc in 1429, the last of her works that can be dated. She is thought to have died in the early 1430s, probably in the abbey of Poissy.
