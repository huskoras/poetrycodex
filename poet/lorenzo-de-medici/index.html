Lorenzo de' Medici

1449–1492

Lorenzo de' Medici (1449–1492), called Lorenzo the Magnificent, was the ruling citizen of Florence from 1469 until his death and the most important patron of Renaissance culture in Italy. He sponsored artists such as Botticelli and Michelangelo and gathered scholars and poets around him, among them Angelo Poliziano and Marsilio Ficino.

He was also a poet of real range. His Canzoniere and the prose Comento on some of his sonnets belong to the Tuscan lyric tradition of Petrarch and Dante, while his canti carnascialeschi (carnival songs), written for the festivities of Florence, and his canzoni a ballo (dance songs) draw on popular verse. Best known of them is the Trionfo di Bacco e Arianna, with its refrain on the shortness of youth. The Nencia da Barberino, an octave poem in the voice of a country lover praising his Nencia, is traditionally attributed to him.

This selection follows the Simioni edition (Laterza, 1913–14) as transcribed on Italian Wikisource: the Trionfo di Bacco e Arianna, ten further carnival songs, the first eighteen canzoni a ballo and the Nencia da Barberino. The Canzoniere sonnets, the spiritual lauds and the prose works are not included.
