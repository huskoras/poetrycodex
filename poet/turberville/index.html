Tudor & Elizabethan

George Turberville

c. 1540–c. 1610

George Turberville (c. 1540 – c. 1610) was an Elizabethan poet and translator, born in Dorset and educated at Winchester College and at Oxford before he entered the Inns of Court in London. In 1568 he went to Russia as secretary to Thomas Randolph, Elizabeth's ambassador to Ivan the Terrible, and wrote verse letters home about the country and its customs, which Richard Hakluyt later printed.

His Epitaphes, Epigrams, Songs and Sonets (1567) mixes love complaints, epigrams and funeral verses, and gathers a loosely told love story of Tymetes and Pyndara. He also translated Ovid's Heroides (1567), the eclogues of Mantuan (1567) and Italian tales, and wrote two popular manuals on falconry and hunting (1575). This edition is the 1567 text, with the spelling kept as printed.
