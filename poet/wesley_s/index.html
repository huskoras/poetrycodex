Samuel Wesley

1662–1735

Samuel Wesley (1662–1735) was an English clergyman and poet, born in Dorset and educated at Oxford. He became rector of Epworth in Lincolnshire in 1696 and held the living until his death. He is best remembered as the father of John Wesley, the founder of Methodism, and of the hymn-writer Charles Wesley.

Before his long career in the Church he published Maggots, or Poems on Several Subjects (1685), a book of comic verse written in his youth. Its mock-heroic and burlesque poems on trifling subjects, a hog's grunting, a cheese, a tobacco pipe, a louse, are the playful side of a man later known for pious and learned writing.
