Romanian

Octavian Goga

1881–1938

Octavian Goga (1881–1938) was born in Rășinari, near Sibiu, in Transylvania, then part of the Kingdom of Hungary, the son of an Orthodox priest. He studied in Sibiu, Budapest and Berlin, and wrote in a Romanian rooted in the speech, the church and the village life of the Transylvanian countryside.

His first book, Poezii (Budapest, 1905), made him the poet of Transylvanian suffering. Its plain, songlike verses give voice to peasants, village teachers and priests, to a people denied its land and language, and to the lament of the Olt valley. It was crowned by the Romanian Academy, and Goga went on to publish Ne cheamă pământul (1909) and Din umbra zidurilor (1913). In later life he entered Romanian politics, serving briefly as prime minister in 1937–38.
