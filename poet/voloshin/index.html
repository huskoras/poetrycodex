Russian & Slavic

Maximilian Voloshin

1877–1932

Максимилиан Александрович Волошин (1877–1932), born in Kiev, was a Russian poet, critic and watercolour painter. He studied in Moscow and spent long periods in Paris, where he absorbed French Symbolist and Parnassian poetry and later translated Heredia, Verhaeren and others into Russian. From the early 1900s he wrote for the Symbolist journals, and his Paris and Crimean poems appeared in the collections Стихотворения 1900–1910 and Selva Oscura.

From 1903 he spent much of each year at Koktebel on the Crimean coast, where his house became a gathering place for poets and painters. He remained in Crimea through the First World War and the Civil War, and the poems of Неопалимая купина (“The Burning Bush”) and the cycle Путями Каина (“The Ways of Cain”) record the revolution and its terror. He died at Koktebel in 1932. This selection draws on texts held by Russian Wikisource.
