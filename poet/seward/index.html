Romantic

Anna Seward

1742–1809

Anna Seward (1742–1809), born at Eyam in Derbyshire and raised in Lichfield, where her father was a canon of the cathedral, was known in her own day as the “Swan of Lichfield”. She was educated at home on progressive lines and became a prominent figure in the literary society of the Midlands, publishing an elegy on Captain Cook, a monody on Major André and the poetical novel Louisa (1784).

Her Original Sonnets on Various Subjects, and Odes Paraphrased from Horace appeared in 1799. The hundred sonnets, many in the Petrarchan and Miltonic manner and some of them nature poems or elegies, helped to revive the English sonnet in the years before Wordsworth, and the Horatian paraphrases freely expand their originals. Her letters were published posthumously in 1811.
