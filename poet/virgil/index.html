Virgil

70–19 BC

Publius Vergilius Maro, known in English as Virgil, was born near Mantua in 70 BC and became the greatest poet of Augustan Rome. His Eclogues and the Georgics, a poem on farming and the countryside, made his name; his patron Maecenas brought him into the circle around the emperor Augustus.

The Aeneid, left unfinished at his death in 19 BC, tells how the Trojan prince Aeneas escapes the burning of Troy and carries his gods and his people to Italy, where his descendants will found Rome. In twelve books it answers Homer in Latin and became the model for the Western epic. This archive presents it in John Dryden’s 1697 verse translation, in heroic couplets.
