French

Ponce-Denis Écouchard-Lebrun

1729–1807

Ponce-Denis Écouchard-Lebrun (1729–1807), known as Lebrun-Pindare, was born in Paris and raised in the household of the prince de Conti. He made his name as a lyric poet with odes in the manner of Pindar, among them his ode to Buffon, and he was admired in his day as the greatest French odist, though later critics found his Pindaric manner more strained than inspired.

He welcomed the Revolution and wrote patriotic odes for it, the best known being the ode on the sinking of the warship Le Vengeur in 1794, and he later sang the praises of Napoleon. He was equally famous for his sharp epigrams, and a member of the Institut. The texts here are the Oxford Book of French Verse (1926 edition) and French Wikisource's transcription of the 1811 Œuvres edited by Ginguené.
