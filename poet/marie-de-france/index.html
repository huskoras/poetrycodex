Marie de France

fl. c. 1160–1215

Marie de France was a poet who lived in the second half of the twelfth century and wrote in Anglo-Norman French, probably in England. Nothing certain is known of her life beyond the name she gives in her own verse: “Marie ai nun, si sui de France.” She is the earliest known woman poet to write in French, and her short narrative poems, called lays, drew on Breton tales of love, loss and enchantment.

The twelve lays given here are those preserved in the London manuscript, British Library Harley 978. They are read in the English prose of Eugene Mason (Everyman’s Library, 1911), which renders her octosyllabic couplets as continuous prose, preceded by her dedicatory prologue.
