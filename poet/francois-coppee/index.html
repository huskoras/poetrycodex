French

François Coppée

1842–1908

François Coppée (1842–1908) was born in Paris, the son of a minor clerk in the War Ministry, and spent his working life as a clerk himself before becoming librarian of the Senate and archivist of the Comédie-Française. He published Le Reliquaire in 1866 and joined the Parnassian circle around Leconte de Lisle and Lemerre, but his one-act verse play Le Passant (1869), famously performed by Sarah Bernhardt, made his name with the general public.

His best-loved poetry turns from Parnassian marble to the streets of Paris: Les Intimités, Les Humbles (1872) and Promenades et Intérieurs (1872) give the clerks, shopkeepers, nurses and suburban strollers of the city a plain, tender and sometimes ironic voice, which earned him the title of poet of the humble. He entered the Académie française in 1884, succeeding Victor de Laprade, and remained among the most widely read French poets of his generation.
