Valery Bryusov (Валерий Брюсов)

1873–1924

Валерий Яковлевич Брюсов (1873–1924), born in Moscow into a merchant family and educated in history and philology at Moscow University, was the organiser and chief theorist of Russian Symbolism. He made his name in the 1890s with the anthologies Русские символисты and with the collections Me eum esse (1897) and Tertia Vigilia (1900), co-founded the publishing house Скорпион (the Scorpion) in 1899, and edited the journal Весы (The Scales) from 1904 to 1909. He was also a prolific translator, above all of Verlaine and Verhaeren, and the author of the novel The Fiery Angel.

Urbi et orbi (1903), dedicated to Konstantin Balmont, gathered his poems of 1900–1903 in cycles of reveries, songs, ballads, elegies, sonnets, city pictures and odes; its title, “to the city and the world”, is the papal blessing. Στέφανος (“The Wreath”, 1906) followed it. This selection takes every poem of Urbi et orbi in book order, in the old spelling of the Scorpion edition, and then the opening poems of Στέφανος, from the Wikisource transcriptions of the printed scans (CC BY-SA); the prefatory essay of Urbi et orbi is left out. Bryusov joined the Communist Party in 1920 and died in Moscow on 9 October 1924.
