Théophile de Viau

1590–1626

Théophile de Viau (1590–1626) was a French poet and dramatist, born at Clairac in the Agenais into a Huguenot family. He made his name at the court of Louis XIII in the years around 1620 with odes, stances, elegies and sonnets, as well as the tragedy Pyrame et Thisbé, and wrote in an openly sensuous, naturalist and free-thinking vein against the austere reforms of Malherbe.

In 1623 he was accused of impiety and of authorship of poems in the Parnasse satyrique; he was condemned to be burned in effigy, then arrested, tried and imprisoned, and in 1625 was banished from France. He died in Paris in 1626. This selection follows the Œuvres complètes edited by Charles Alleaume (Paris: Jannet, 1856), with the spelling of that edition.
