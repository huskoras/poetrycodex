Fyodor Sologub (Фёдор Сологуб)

1863–1927

Fyodor Sologub was the pen name of Fyodor Kuzmich Teternikov, born in St Petersburg in 1863 to a poor family; his father was a tailor and his mother a domestic servant. He trained as a teacher, worked in provincial schools for about a decade, and moved to the capital in the early 1890s, where he joined the circle of the Russian Symbolists and wrote for their journals.

He became one of the central poets and prose writers of the Russian Silver Age, known for the dark novel The Petty Demon and for lyrics that turn on the fascination of death, the closed self, and an invented consoling world, as in his poems of the star Mair. His verse books appeared from 1896 through the early 1920s, and he died in Leningrad in 1927. This selection is taken only from the books printed in his lifetime.
