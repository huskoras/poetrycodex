Russian & Slavic

Mikhail Kuzmin

1872–1936

Mikhail Alekseyevich Kuzmin (1872–1936) was a Russian poet, composer, novelist and translator, born in Yaroslavl and educated in St Petersburg, where he studied at the Conservatory and became a central figure of the city's literary and artistic circles in the first decades of the twentieth century. He was close to the Symbolists and the World of Art group, and his lucid, ornamental, deliberately light manner (the 'beautiful clarity' he advocated) stood apart from the mystical register of much Symbolist verse, anticipating Acmeist taste.

His first book of verse, Сети (“Nets”, 1908), contains the cycle Любовь этого лета (“This Summer's Love”, 1906) and the Александрийские песни (“Alexandrian Songs”, 1905–08), short free-verse lyrics in the voice of Hellenistic Egypt. Осенние озёра (“Autumn Lakes”, 1912) and Глиняные голубки (“Clay Doves”, 1914) followed, then Параболы (“Parabolas”, 1923), and the cycle Форель разбивает лёд (“The Trout Breaks the Ice”, 1929), his last book of verse. He wrote the novella Крылья (“Wings”, 1906), translated Apuleius's Golden Ass, and died in Leningrad in 1936.
