Maurice Rollinat

1846–1903

Maurice Rollinat was born in Châteauroux, in the Indre, in 1846, the son of a republican lawyer, François Rollinat, who was a friend of George Sand; she took an interest in the young man’s first verses. He came to Paris in 1868, and his first book, Dans les brandes (1877), drew on the heathland and peasant life of his native Berry.

In Paris he sang and played his own poems at the Hydropathes and the Chat Noir, and with Les Névroses (1883), a book of morbid, nervous and macabre pieces in the line of Baudelaire and Poe, he became a leading name among the Decadents. Later books included L’Abîme (1886). He spent his last years in the Creuse and died in 1903.
