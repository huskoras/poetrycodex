Gonçalves Crespo

1846–1883

António Cândido Gonçalves Crespo was born in Rio de Janeiro in 1846, the son of a Portuguese merchant and an enslaved Black woman. He was taken to Portugal as a child, studied law at Coimbra, and later practised as a lawyer and sat as a deputy in the Portuguese parliament.

He became the leading Portuguese poet of the Parnassian school, with Miniaturas (1871) and Nocturnos (1882), the latter dedicated to his wife, the writer Maria Amália Vaz de Carvalho. He died in Lisbon in 1883, aged thirty-six.
