Morris Rosenfeld

1862–1923

Morris Rosenfeld (1862–1923) was born in a small town in Russian Lithuania and wrote in Yiddish. He worked as a tailor in Warsaw and then in London, before emigrating in 1886 to New York, where he spent years as a presser in the garment sweatshops of the Lower East Side.

His songs of the factory, the clock and the machine, together with poems on exile, the ghetto and the Jewish festivals, made him the best-loved voice of the immigrant working class, and he became known as the "poet of the sweatshop". The poems here are the English versions of his Yiddish originals by Rose Pastor Stokes and Helena Frank, published in Boston in 1914.
