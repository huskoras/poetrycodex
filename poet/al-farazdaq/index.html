al-Farazdaq

c. 641–c. 730

al-Farazdaq (الفرزدق), whose given name was Hammam ibn Ghalib, was a poet of the Arab tribe of Tamim, born around 641 and active mostly in Basra under the Umayyad caliphs. He died around 730. With Jarir and al-Akhtal he formed the great trio of Umayyad poets, and his long poetic feud with Jarir, carried on in answering poems (the naqa'id), was one of the famous literary entertainments of the age. He wrote boasting, praise and abuse in a grand, hard, closely worked old Arabic style, and later scholars valued his verse as a storehouse of the pure language of the tribes.

These poems are taken from the Arabic text transcribed on Wikisource and are given in the original, one verse (bayt) to a line with its two halves apart. The selection is the longer poems of the collection, each of three or more verses, beginning with the celebrated ode in praise of Ali ibn al-Husayn Zayn al-Abidin, a descendant of the Prophet, which he is said to have recited at Mecca during the pilgrimage. Each poem is named by its first half-line, with a plain Latin transliteration.

  113. أرَى ابنَ سُلَيْمٍ يَعصِمُ الله دِينَهُ (arā ibana sulaym yaʻaṣimu al-lah dīnahu)
  114. أقُولُ لحَرْفٍ قَدْ تَخَوّنَ نيَّهَا (aqūlu laḥarf qad takhawwana nayyahā)
  115. ألِكْني، وَقَدْ تأتي الرّسالَةُ مَن نَأى (aliknī waqad taʼatī al-rasāla man naʼā)
  116. ألَمْ يَكُ جَهْلاً بَعْدَ سِتّينَ حِجّةً (alam yaku jahlā baʻda sittīna ḥijja)
  117. تَزَوّدَ مِنْهَا نَظْرَةً لمْ تَدَعْ لَهُ (tazawwada minhā naẓra lam tadaʻ lahu)
  118. تَقُولُ كُلَيْبٌ حينَ مَثّتْ سِبَالُهَا (taqūlu kulayb ḥīna maththat sibāluhā)
  119. وَجَدْنَا الأبْرَشَ الكَلْبيَّ تَنمي (wajadnā al-ʼabrasha al-kalbayya tanamī)
  120. أمّا قُرَيشٌ أبَا حَفصٍ فَقدْ رُزِئَتْ (ammā quraysh abā ḥafaṣ faqad ruziʼat)
