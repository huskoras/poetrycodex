William Crowe

1745–1829

William Crowe (1745–1829) was an English clergyman and scholar, for many years Public Orator of the University of Oxford. His blank-verse poem Lewesdon Hill, first published in 1788 and dedicated to the Bishop of St Asaph, describes a May-morning climb to the top of a hill in western Dorset and the prospect of land and sea from its summit; it was admired by Wordsworth and Coleridge.

The texts here are from Lewesdon Hill, with Other Poems, the corrected and much enlarged edition (London, John Murray, 1827). Besides the title poem the volume gathers odes spoken at Oxford installations, songs, epigrams, epitaphs, translations from Lucretius and the Psalms, and verses in Latin and Greek. The author’s notes, the advertisement and the dedication are omitted.
