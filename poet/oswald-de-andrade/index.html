Oswald de Andrade

1890–1954

Oswald de Andrade (José Oswald de Souza Andrade, 1890–1954) was a Brazilian poet, novelist, playwright and cultural critic, born and raised in São Paulo. After a youthful journey to Europe he returned to become, with Mário de Andrade and others, a leading voice of Brazilian Modernism, and he took a prominent part in the Semana de Arte Moderna held in São Paulo in February 1922.

In 1924 he published the Manifesto da Poesia Pau-Brasil, calling for a poetry of the country's own materials, history and speech, and in 1925 he issued the collection Pau Brasil, illustrated by Tarsila do Amaral. Its short, spare poems, many of them reworked from the chronicles of early colonial writers, mix Brazilian history, landscape and everyday modern life. In 1928 he followed it with the Manifesto Antropófago. He died in São Paulo.
