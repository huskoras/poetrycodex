Giovan Battista Marino

1569–1625

Giovan Battista Marino (Naples, 1569 – Naples, 1625) was the leading Italian poet of the early seventeenth century and the founder of the style called Marinism, which prized ingenuity, ornament and the surprising conceit. He spent years at the courts of Naples, Rome, Ravenna and Turin, and from 1615 to 1623 lived in Paris under the patronage of Marie de’ Medici, where his long mythological poem Adone appeared in 1623.

This selection is drawn from his shorter verse as edited by Benedetto Croce in Poesie varie (Bari: Laterza, 1913): the love sonnets, the madrigals and the love canzoni of its first sections, in the order of the edition, under the titles printed there. The long Adone is not included.
