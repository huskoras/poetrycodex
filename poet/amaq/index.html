'Am'aq of Bukhara

d. c. 1148

'Am'aq of Bukhara (عمعق بخاری, Shihab al-Din 'Am'aq, born about 440 AH / 1048 in Bukhara; Ganjoor gives his death as 542 AH, c. 1148) was a Persian court poet of Transoxiana, where he served the Qarakhanid (Al-i Afrasiyab) rulers and carried the title Amir al-Shu'ara, 'prince of poets'. Panegyrics on Shams al-Mulk Nasr and other princes of the house fill his Divan, and he is remembered above all for his spring qasidas, rich in simile and in the brocades, jewels and pictured silks of the garden.

The long qasida on spring that opens 'Rise, paradisal beauty, bring the cup of wine' stands first here, with its Persian original from Ganjoor (qasida no. 8) and the ten verses that Reynold A. Nicholson put into English in 1922. After it come the other qasidas of the Divan as Ganjoor prints them (one coarse invective omitted) and a selection of the shorter pieces. Each bayt stands on one line, the two hemistichs separated by a wide space. The Persian text is public domain.
