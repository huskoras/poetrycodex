Hedd Wyn (Ellis Humphrey Evans)

1887–1917

Hedd Wyn was the bardic name of Ellis Humphrey Evans (1887-1917), a shepherd and farmer's son from the Ysgwrn farm at Trawsfynydd in Meirionnydd. He had little formal schooling but learned the strict-metre cynghanedd from his father, won his first eisteddfod chair at eighteen, and took his bardic name Hedd Wyn, 'blessed peace', from the mist on the mountainside. He wrote in Welsh, in strict and free metres alike, of the hills, the flocks, the dead of his village and the war.

Conscripted into the Royal Welch Fusiliers in 1917, he wrote his awdl 'Yr Arwr' (The Hero) in the army and posted it from France under the pen-name Fleur-de-lis. He was killed at Pilckem Ridge, near Ypres, on 31 July 1917. At the National Eisteddfod at Birkenhead that September the chair was awarded to him, and the empty chair was draped in black, the 'Black Chair'. His only book, Cerddi'r Bugail ('The Shepherd's Poems'), was edited by J. J. Williams and published in 1918.
