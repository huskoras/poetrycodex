American

Countee Cullen

1903–1946

Countee Cullen (1903–1946) was an American poet, born in 1903 and raised in New York City, where he attended New York University and Harvard and became one of the best-known poets of the Harlem Renaissance. He wrote in traditional forms, the sonnet, the quatrain and the ballad, often turning them to questions of race, faith and mortality.

Color (1925) was his first book, and includes "Yet Do I Marvel", "Heritage" and "Incident" along with a run of short epitaphs. The Black Christ & Other Poems (1929) followed, written in part with the aid of a Guggenheim Fellowship; its title poem is a long narrative of a lynching and a resurrection. He later wrote further verse, a novel and children's books, and died in New York in 1946.
