Andrei Bely (Андрей Белый)

1880–1934

Boris Nikolayevich Bugayev (1880–1934), who wrote as Andrei Bely, was born in Moscow, the son of the mathematician Nikolai Bugayev, and studied natural sciences at Moscow University. He became one of the leading figures of the second generation of Russian Symbolism, a poet, novelist and critic who also wrote pioneering studies of the rhythm of Russian verse.

His first verse book, Золото в лазури (“Gold in Azure”, 1904), is a sunlit, mystical and ironic collection of sunsets, centaurs and cosmic visions. Пепел (“Ashes”, 1909), dedicated to the memory of Nekrasov, turned to the poverty and despair of the Russian countryside and the city after 1905, and Урна (“The Urn”, 1909) is a book of philosophical and elegiac lyrics. His best-known novel is Петербург (1913–14); the long autobiographical poem Первое свидание (“The First Encounter”, 1921) recalls his Moscow youth and the figure of Vladimir Solovyov. He died in Moscow on 8 January 1934.
