al-Khansa

c. 575 – c. 646

al-Khansa (الخنساء), whose given name was Tumadir bint 'Amr, belonged to the tribe of Sulaym and lived from about 575 to about 646; the dates are traditional and uncertain. She is the best known woman poet of the Arabic tradition, and a mukhadram, one who lived through both the Jahiliyya and the coming of Islam. Her fame rests on her elegies (marathi) for her brothers Sakhr and Mu'awiya, killed in the tribal wars before Islam; Sakhr's death, above all, is the grief she returns to again and again.

Tradition says that she came to Medina with a delegation of her tribe, accepted Islam, and recited her poetry before the Prophet. The pieces here are the poems of her diwan that run to three or more verses, in the Arabic Wikisource transcriptions, with the vowel marks of that source. Several elegies on the same theme exist in more than one version; each poem appears once, in its fuller, vocalised form.
