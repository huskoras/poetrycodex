German

Wilhelm Busch

1832-1908

Wilhelm Busch (1832-1908) was a German humorist, draughtsman and poet, born in Wiedensahl in Lower Saxony. After studying at the art academies of Düsseldorf, Antwerp and Munich, he became famous for his illustrated verse tales, above all Max und Moritz (1865), whose comic rhymes and caricatured drawings made him a forerunner of the modern comic strip.

Kritik des Herzens (1874) is his first collection of lyric verse without pictures: short, wry poems on love, vanity, hypocrisy, ageing and the follies of the respectable world, written in plain song-like stanzas. Its sceptical, self-mocking humour sets Busch beside Heine among the satirists of German verse.
