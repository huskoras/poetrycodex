Dutch & Flemish

Hendrik Tollens

1780–1856

Hendrik Tollens (1780–1856) was born in Rotterdam, where he worked as a paint and varnish manufacturer and merchant before giving his time wholly to literature. He became one of the best-loved Dutch poets of the first half of the nineteenth century, admired for simple, sentimental verse about home, family and country.

His song “Wien Neêrlands bloed” (1815) served as the Dutch national anthem until 1932. His verse tale of the Dutch seamen who wintered on Novaya Zemlya in 1596–97, the “Tafereel van de overwintering der Hollanders op Nova Zembla” (1819), is among his best-known longer works; it is given here in Daniel van Pelt’s English version of 1884.
