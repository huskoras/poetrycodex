Ancient Greek & Roman

Homer

fl. c. 8th century BC

Homer is the name tradition gives to the poet, or poets, credited with the Iliad and the Odyssey, the two foundational epics of ancient Greek literature. Nothing reliable is known of his life; ancient sources disagree even on his birthplace, and many scholars believe the poems as we have them are the culmination of centuries of oral epic tradition rather than the work of one man.

Whether historical individual or convenient legend, "Homer" is credited with shaping the Trojan War and the wanderings of Odysseus into the verse that became the bedrock of Greek and later Western literature and education.

This archive presents multiple English translations of the Iliad and the Odyssey side by side, so that the different choices each translator made — prose versus verse, plain diction versus ornate — can be compared directly.
