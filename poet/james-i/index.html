King James I of Scotland

1394–1437

James I (1394–1437) was the son of Robert III of Scotland. Sent to France for safety as a boy, he was captured at sea by English ships in 1406 and held in England for eighteen years. In captivity he received a thorough education, and in 1424 he married Joan Beaufort, a kinswoman of the English royal house, and returned to Scotland to be crowned at Scone. He was murdered at Perth in February 1437.

The Kingis Quair (‘The King’s Book’) is traditionally attributed to him and is usually dated from his years in England or soon after. It survives in a single manuscript, the Bodleian Library’s MS Arch. Selden B.24. Its Chaucerian rhyme-royal stanzas and dream-vision form made James the first of the Scottish poets who looked back to Chaucer, and some of the poem’s lines are read as a record of his own love for Joan Beaufort. Whether the whole poem is his has been debated.

  1. The Kingis Quair — Dream-vision poem (Middle Scots), c. 1424
