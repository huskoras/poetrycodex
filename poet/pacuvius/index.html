Marcus Pacuvius

c. 220 – c. 130 BC

Marcus Pacuvius was born about 220 BC at Brundisium in southern Italy, the son of a sister of the poet Ennius. He was a painter as well as a poet, and he settled in Rome, where he wrote tragedies on Greek mythological themes, among them Antiopa, Chryses, Hermiona, Iliona, Medus, Niptra, Periboea and Teucer, as well as a play on a Roman subject, Paulus. Cicero names him the finest of the Roman tragic poets.

Only fragments of his plays survive, as quotations in Cicero, Varro, Nonius, Gellius and other writers. Later in life he retired to Tarentum, where he is said to have died around 130 BC. Here his fragments are given as Garrod printed them in the Oxford Book of Latin Verse (1912), together with the epitaph he composed for himself, which Gellius preserved.
