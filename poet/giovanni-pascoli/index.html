Italian

Giovanni Pascoli

1855–1912

Giovanni Pascoli (1855–1912) was born at San Mauro di Romagna, in the same Romagna countryside that fills his verse. His father was murdered in 1867, and a run of family deaths followed; the loss of that household became the hidden subject of much of his poetry. He studied at Bologna under Giosuè Carducci, taught Latin and Greek, and in 1905 succeeded Carducci in the chair of Italian literature at Bologna. From 1895 he lived at Castelvecchio di Barga in Tuscany with his sister Maria, and he died at Bologna on 6 April 1912.

Myricae, first published in 1891 and enlarged in successive editions up to its final form in 1903, gathers short, exact poems of fields, birds, weather and the dead, in which small things are made to carry grief and wonder. The Canti di Castelvecchio (1903, second edition 1907) continue the vein from his Tuscan house, with longer songs of the household, the seasons and the night. Pascoli's attention to sound and to the humble detail, and his picture of the poet as a child who sees the world fresh, made him a founder of modern Italian poetry.
