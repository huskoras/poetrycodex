Guillaume de Machaut

c. 1300–1377

Guillaume de Machaut (c. 1300–1377), born in the Champagne region and long associated with Reims, was the dominant French poet and composer of the fourteenth century. He served as secretary to John of Luxembourg, King of Bohemia, and later to other royal and noble patrons, and ended his life as a canon of Reims cathedral.

He is remembered above all for his dits, such as the Remede de Fortune and the Voir Dit, for his polyphonic Mass, and for a large body of lyric forms (ballades, rondeaux, virelais, lais and chants royaux) that set the pattern for French courtly song for generations. The poems here follow V. Chichmaref's complete edition of the lyrics (1909), in the original Middle French spelling.
