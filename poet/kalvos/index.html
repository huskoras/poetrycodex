Modern Greek

Andreas Kalvos

1792–1869

Andreas Kalvos (1792–1869) was born on the island of Zakynthos, then under Venetian and later British rule, and educated in Italy, where he came under the influence of Ugo Foscolo. He later lived in Florence, Geneva, Paris and Corfu, and spent his last years in England, where he taught and died in 1869.

His fame rests on twenty odes on the Greek War of Independence. Modelled on Pindar and the neoclassical ode, in strophes of five short lines, they are written in an archaising, highly wrought language of his own. Ten appeared in Geneva in 1824 as Η Λύρα, and ten more in Paris in 1826 as Λυρικά.

Largely overlooked in his lifetime, Kalvos was rediscovered in the later nineteenth century and is now counted, with Dionysios Solomos, among the founders of modern Greek poetry.
