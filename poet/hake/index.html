Victorian

Thomas Gordon Hake

1809–1895

Thomas Gordon Hake (1809–1895) was an English physician and poet. His verse appeared over more than fifty years, from The Piromides (1839) through Madeline, Parables and Tales and The New Day (1890), and was admired by Dante Gabriel Rossetti, who reviewed his work and drew his portrait.

He wrote long, solemn narrative poems in short stanzas, often about dream, sleep and the passage of the soul. The poems here are taken from the 1894 selection published by Elkin Mathews and John Lane, with a prefatory note by Alice Meynell.
