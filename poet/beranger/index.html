Pierre-Jean de Béranger

1780–1857

Pierre-Jean de Béranger (1780–1857) was a French songwriter, born in Paris and raised largely by his grandparents and an aunt at Péronne. After an apprenticeship in a printing house and years of poverty as a young writer, he turned to the chanson and found a patron in the Institut and then a post in the University administration.

His satirical and patriotic songs, notably under the Restoration, made him the most popular French songwriter of his day. They brought prosecutions and brief imprisonments in 1821 and 1828, and fixed in the popular mind the Napoleonic legend and the cause of liberty. He refused honours and office after 1830 and died in Paris, receiving a state funeral.
