Guido Guinizelli

c. 1230–1276

Guido Guinizelli (c. 1230–1276) was a Bolognese jurist and judge, a member of a family of Bolognese notables, who wrote lyric poetry in the Italian vernacular in the generation after the Sicilian School.

He is regarded as the founder of the dolce stil novo, the 'sweet new style' of love poetry, chiefly through his canzone 'Al cor gentil rempaira sempre amore', which joins love to nobility of heart rather than of birth. In the Purgatorio (Canto XXVI) Dante calls him his 'padre' and the father of those who write sweet and gracious rhymes of love.

A supporter of the Ghibelline party at Bologna, he was sent into exile in 1274 when the Guelfs gained the upper hand, and he is thought to have died at Monselice in 1276. Some twenty poems are attributed to him.

  2. Al cor gentil rempaira sempre amore
  3. Ch'eo cor avesse, mi potea laudare

  5. Con gran disio pensando lungamente
  6. Dolente, lasso, già non m'asecuro

  10. Fra l'altre pene maggio credo sia
  11. Gentil donzella, di pregio nomata
  12. In quanto la natura
  13. Io voglio del ver la mia donna laudare
  14. Lamentomi di mia disaventura
  15. Lo fin pregi' avanzato

  18. Madonna, il fino amor ched eo vo porto
  19. Omo ch'è saggio non corre leggero
  20. Vedut' ho la lucente stella diana
