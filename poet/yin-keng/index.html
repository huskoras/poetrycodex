Yin Keng

c. 511–c. 563

Yin Keng (c. 511–c. 563), courtesy name Zijian, came from Wuwei and was famed from boyhood for his five-character verse, said to have recited poems and rhapsodies by heart at five. He served the Liang as a military official under the Prince of Xiangdong and, under the Chen, held posts in the household of the Prince of Shixing and rose to be governor of Jinling and an attendant gentleman of the court.

He is remembered as a master of the landscape poem and the poem of parting, in the close, balanced couplets of the Liang and Chen court style. The Tang poet Du Fu praised him, naming him beside He Xun as a model for poets who care for finished lines. The five poems given here are taken from juan 109 of Feng Weine's Gushi ji, as transcribed in the Siku quanshu.

Poems (5)

  1. 渡青草湖 (Du Qingcao hu)
  2. 廣陵岸送北使 (Guangling an song bei shi)
  3. 江津送劉光祿不及 (Jiangjin song Liu Guanglu bu ji)
  4. 和傅郎歲暮還湘州 (He Fu lang suimu huan Xiangzhou)
  5. 開善寺 (Kaishan si)
