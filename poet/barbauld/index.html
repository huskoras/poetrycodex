Anna Laetitia Barbauld

1743–1825

Anna Laetitia Barbauld (1743–1825), née Aikin, was born in Leicestershire, the daughter of a Dissenting minister and schoolmaster, and grew up at Warrington Academy. Her Poems (1773) went through several editions within a year, and she went on to write hymns, children’s books and essays and to teach at a school she ran with her husband.

Her later work includes the long political poem Eighteen Hundred and Eleven (1812), which looks forward to the decline of Britain and the rise of America. She was a friend of Joseph Priestley and a figure in the Dissenting and literary circles of her day.
