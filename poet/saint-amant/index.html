Marc-Antoine Girard de Saint-Amant

1594–1661

Marc-Antoine Girard, sieur de Saint-Amant (1594–1661), was born at Quimper in Brittany into a Protestant family and spent his life as a courtier-poet, soldier of fortune and traveller, with journeys that took him to Italy, Spain, England and Poland. He belonged to the libertine circle around Théophile de Viau and to the first generation of the Académie française, and he was a friend of Faret and Boisrobert.

He is the great poet of French Baroque wit and sensation: La Solitude, a hymn to wild places and melancholy, stands beside his sonnets on the seasons, the mock-heroic and burlesque caprices, and a run of tavern songs and odes to wine, melon and cheese. These poems come from Charles-Louis Livet’s edition of his complete works (Paris: P. Jannet, 1855); the long epic Moïse sauvé and the editor’s notice and notes are left out.
