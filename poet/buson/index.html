Yosa Buson

1716–1784

与謝蕪村 (Yosa Buson, 1716–1784) was born in Settsu Province, near present-day Osaka, and died in Kyoto. He left for Edo as a young man to study haikai, travelled in the northern provinces, and settled in Kyoto around 1751. He was at once a leading painter of the literati school and, after Bashō, the most admired master of the hokku, the opening verse that later ages called haiku. His hokku are known for their pictorial clarity and their borrowings from Chinese poetry and the Heian court.

These hokku are those quoted by Masaoka Shiki in his critical study 俳人蕪村 (1897–99), which restored Buson's reputation after a century of neglect, and by Hagiwara Sakutarō in 郷愁の詩人 与謝蕪村 (1936). They are taken from the Aozora Bunko texts of those two books and are a selection, not a complete edition; verses the two critics attribute to other poets are left out, and the spelling is as the critics printed it.
