Herman Melville

1819–1891

Herman Melville (1819–1891) was an American novelist, short-story writer and poet, born in New York City. He is best known for the sea novel Moby-Dick (1851), which was little appreciated in his lifetime.

Battle-Pieces and Aspects of the War (1866) was his first book of verse, a sequence of poems on the American Civil War, from John Brown to the surrender at Appomattox and its aftermath. Melville turned chiefly to poetry in his later years and worked for many years as a customs inspector in New York.
