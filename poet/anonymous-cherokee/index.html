Anonymous (Cherokee)

traditional; manuscripts recorded 1887–1888

These are the sacred formulas of the Cherokee shamans: spoken prayers and incantations for healing, hunting, love, war and the ball play. They were written down by the shamans themselves, for their own use, in the syllabary that Sequoyah invented in 1821. The manuscripts passed to the Bureau of Ethnology, which acquired them on the Cherokee reservation in North Carolina in 1887 and 1888. The best-known source among them is the notebook of the shaman Aʼyunini (Swimmer).

They are known to us through the ethnologist James Mooney (1861–1921), who selected, transliterated and translated them for the Bureau's Seventh Annual Report (1891). Here each formula is given in Mooney's English, followed by the Cherokee as he printed it in roman letters; his explanations and notes on each formula are left out.
