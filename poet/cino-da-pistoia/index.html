Cino da Pistoia

1270–1336/37

Cino da Pistoia (Guittoncino de’ Sighibuldi, c. 1270–1336/37) was a jurist and a poet, and in both capacities one of the best-known Tuscans of his generation. Born in Pistoia, he studied law at Bologna, took his doctorate there, and held chairs at Siena, Perugia, Florence and Naples; his Lectura on the first nine books of the Code of Justinian (1314) made him a leading authority of his day, and his pupils are said to have included Bartolus of Sassoferrato. Caught in the factional strife of Pistoia, he lived for some years in exile.

As a poet he belongs to the dolce stil novo, the ‘sweet new style’ of love poetry. Dante, who was his friend and exchanged sonnets with him, singles him out in De vulgari eloquentia as the poet of love, as Bertran de Born is of arms and Giraut de Bornelh of rectitude; Petrarch later honoured him in his Triumph of Love and in a sonnet on his death. His Rime tell of his love for a lady he calls Selvaggia, whose death he laments, and also include verses addressed to Dante, to Cecco d’Ascoli and others, and a pair of poems on the death of the Emperor Henry VII. The Italian texts given here follow Le Rime di M. Cino da Pistoia e d’altri del secolo XIV, with a preface by Giosuè Carducci (1862), in the order of its selection; the poems that edition prints by other fourteenth-century poets, and Carducci’s own preface, are not included.
