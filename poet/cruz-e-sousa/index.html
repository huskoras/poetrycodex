Cruz e Sousa

1861–1898

João da Cruz e Sousa was born in 1861 in Desterro (now Florianópolis), in the Brazilian state of Santa Catarina, the son of parents who had been enslaved and freed. His patrons, the family of his parents' former owner, paid for his schooling, and he grew up reading widely in literature and languages. He worked as a journalist and clerk, and racial prejudice kept him from a public post, a wound that runs through his verse.

He is counted the greatest Brazilian Symbolist. Broquéis (1893), together with the prose of Missal, brought the movement to Brazil. He died of tuberculosis in 1898 at Sítio, Minas Gerais, and his later poems, Faróis (1900) and Últimos sonetos (1905), were published after his death. His poetry is known for its musical language, its imagery of whiteness, and its themes of suffering and transcendence.
