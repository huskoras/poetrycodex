Cielo d'Alcamo (Ciullo)

fl. early 13th century

Cielo d'Alcamo, also called Ciullo, was a Sicilian poet of the early thirteenth century, a jongleur whose name is known only from the single poem attributed to him. Nothing certain is recorded of his life; the poem's references and its southern dialect place it at the Sicilian court milieu of the Duecento.

His 'Rosa fresca aulentissima' is a 'contrasto', a dialogue in which a lover pleads with a lady who first refuses him with spirit and wit, then yields. It is the most famous early Italian poem in dialogue form, and one of the oldest surviving works in the Italian vernacular. Dante Gabriel Rossetti's English version, 'Lover and Lady', is held in the Early Italian Poets collection.
