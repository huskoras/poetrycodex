Auguste Barbier

1805–1882

Henri-Auguste Barbier was born in Paris on 28 April 1805 and died in Nice on 13 February 1882. He was a poet, and also a short-story writer, memoirist, librettist, art critic and translator; with Léon de Wailly he wrote the libretto of Berlioz’s opera Benvenuto Cellini. He was elected to the Académie française in 1869.

He made his name with the Iambes of 1831, satires on the Revolution of July 1830 and the regime that followed it, written in alternating long and short lines after the manner of André Chénier. “La Curée” (the quarry thrown to the hounds after the hunt) and “L’Idole”, on Napoleon, are the best known. Il Pianto (1833), poems of Italy and its artists, and Lazare (1837), a harsh picture of poverty in industrial England, were gathered with the Iambes in the collected edition of 1841, from which these poems are taken.
