French

Aloysius Bertrand

1807–1841

Aloysius Bertrand (Louis Jacques Napoléon Bertrand) was born on 20 April 1807 at Ceva in Piedmont and grew up in Dijon, the Burgundian city whose old streets, towers and bells haunt his book. He went to Paris, wrote for journals and moved among the Romantic circle there, but his poems found no publisher in his lifetime. He died in Paris on 29 April 1841, at the Necker hospital.

His one book, Gaspard de la nuit, was published after his death, in 1842, with a notice by Sainte-Beuve. Its short prose pieces, cast in paragraphs of a few lines and presented as the papers of a mysterious painter-poet, are generally regarded as the first collection of prose poems in French, and so as the ancestor of Baudelaire’s Petits poèmes en prose and of later French prose poetry.
