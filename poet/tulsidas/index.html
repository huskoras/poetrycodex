Indian

Tulsidas

c. 1532–1623

Tulsidas (तुलसीदास) is traditionally dated c. 1532 to 1623, though the dates and most of the life rest on tradition rather than record. He is the great devotional poet of Rama in the Hindi-speaking north, best known for the Ramcharitmanas, his retelling of the Ramayana in Awadhi, and for a body of shorter devotional works in Awadhi and Braj.

The Kavitavali is his collection of kavitta, savaiya, ghanakshari and chhappay verses, arranged by the seven kandas of the Ramayana story, with a long closing movement of petitions to Rama and of lament for the Kali age. Its idiom is the literary Braj-Awadhi of his day. This selection is taken from the Hindi Wikisource transcription of the commentary edition of Champaram Mishra (Indian Press, Allahabad, first edition, V.S. 1990, i.e. 1933): only the verse text is used, never the modern prose gloss. The verse numbers follow that edition's continuous numbering; verses whose pages are still unproofread on Wikisource, or whose text could not be rejoined cleanly, are left out.
