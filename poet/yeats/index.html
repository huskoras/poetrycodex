W. B. Yeats

1865–1939

William Butler Yeats (1865–1939) was an Irish poet and dramatist, born in Dublin, who led the Irish literary revival and co-founded the Abbey Theatre. He received the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1923 and served as a senator of the Irish Free State.

His poetry moved from the Celtic Twilight lyrics of The Rose and The Wind Among the Reeds, through the harder manner of Responsibilities and The Wild Swans at Coole, to the visionary poems of Michael Robartes and the Dancer and The Tower. The poems here are drawn from his books published up to 1928.
