Russian & Slavic

Yakov Polonsky

1819–1898

Яков Петрович Полонский (1819–1898) was a Russian lyric poet, born in Ryazan. He studied law at Moscow University and published his first book of verse, Гаммы, in 1844. In the later 1840s he lived in Tiflis, where he served in the Transcaucasian administration, and the Caucasus and Georgia, with their roads, songs and nights, fill much of his early poetry.

Polonsky wrote musical, speech-like lyrics of landscape, love, memory and the open road, many of them set as romances; Песня цыганки («Мой костёр в тумане светит»), Колокольчик and Зимний путь are among the best known. He was a friend of Fet, Tyutchev and Turgenev, and also wrote narrative poems, plays and prose. This selection takes the shorter lyrics of the first two volumes of his Полное собрание стихотворений (St Petersburg: A. F. Marks, 1896), in the order of the edition and in the pre-reform spelling as printed there. The long narrative poems, the dramatic pieces and the translations are left out. He died in St Petersburg in 1898.
