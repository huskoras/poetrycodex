Emile Verhaeren

1855–1916

Emile Verhaeren was born in 1855 at Sint-Amands, near Antwerp, and grew up on the banks of the Scheldt amid the Flemish plains. He studied at Ghent and Louvain, was called to the Bar at Brussels in 1881, and soon gave up law for literature, publishing his first volume of poems in 1883 and writing for the review L'Art Moderne. He was a friend of Maeterlinck and a leading figure of the Belgian literary revival known as Young Belgium.

His work moves from the vigorous Flemish scenes of Les Flamandes through the anguished books of the 1880s to the visionary village and city poems of Les Villages illusoires and Les Villes tentaculaires, and to the tender love-songs of Les Heures claires, written for his wife. A master of free verse, he died in 1916. The English versions gathered here are by Alma Strettell (1915), Jethro Bithell (1911) and F. S. Flint (1916), with one poem by Osman Edwards (1896).
