Francesco Berni

1497/98–1535

Francesco Berni was born at Lamporecchio in Tuscany in 1497 or 1498 and was brought up in Florence. He spent his working life as a secretary in the households of churchmen: first in Rome under Cardinal Bibbiena and Bishop Giberti, later in Verona, and finally in Florence in the service of Cardinal Ippolito de' Medici and Duke Alessandro de' Medici. He died in Florence in 1535.

Berni gave his name to the 'bernesque' style, a burlesque manner that praises trifles such as eels, peaches, a needle or a chamber pot, and turns the language of high poetry to satire and mock-encomium. His capitoli and sonnets, written in a racy Tuscan, were collected as the Rime and widely imitated; he also recast Boiardo's Orlando innamorato in a smoother Tuscan. His sonnet on his lady, a parody of Petrarchan praise, is the best known of his poems.
