Antara ibn Shaddad

6th century

Antara ibn Shaddad (عنترة بن شداد), of the tribe of 'Abs, was the son of an Abyssinian slave mother and a noble father, and tradition remembers him as the black warrior-poet of pre-Islamic Arabia who won his freedom by courage in battle. His love for his cousin 'Abla is the theme of his verse. The dates of his life are uncertain, and the later popular romance of his adventures, the Sirat 'Antar, is folk tradition, not his own work.

His ode is one of the Mu'allaqat, the long odes of the age of the Jahiliyya, and it begins with the famous question "Hal ghadara al-shu'ara'" (Have the poets left any patch to be sewn?). It is followed here by the shorter pieces of his diwan that run to three or more verses. The texts are from the Arabic Wikisource transcriptions and keep the vowel marks of that source; several pieces in the diwan are of doubtful attribution.
