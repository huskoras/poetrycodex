Yüan Chi

AD 210–263

Yüan Chi (AD 210–263) is represented in this archive by one poem, “Regret,” in which a man who learned fencing in his youth looks back on his wasted years.

The poems here are Arthur Waley’s version in A Hundred and Seventy Chinese Poems (1918), given with the poet’s name as spelled in those books.

The file now also holds Chinese originals of the Yonghuai poems (詠懷詩), the cycle of eighty-two five-character poems for which Ruan Ji (阮籍, 210–263) is best known: seventeen chosen for the Wen xuan, with poem 61, the original of Waley’s “Regret.”

With the later additions the whole cycle is now held: all eighty-two Yonghuai poems in the Chinese, from Wikisource, apart from the verse "Song of the Great Man" (大人先生歌), which survives only inside Ruan Ji's prose Biography of Master Great Man.
