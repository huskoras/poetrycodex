Jean Moréas

1856-1910

Jean Moréas (born Ioannis Papadiamantopoulos, Athens, 1856 – Paris, 1910) was a Greek who came to Paris as a young man and made himself a French poet. He frequented the Symbolist circle, and on 18 September 1886 published in Le Figaro the manifesto that gave Symbolism its name. His first books, Les Syrtes and Les Cantilènes, belong to that movement.

By 1891 he had broken with it and founded the École romane with Charles Maurras and Raymond de la Tailhède, turning toward the clarity and measure of Greek, Latin and French classical models. Les Stances, his late masterpiece, appeared in six books between 1899 and 1901, with a collected edition in 1905; its quatrains and short stanzas, in the manner of Ronsard and Malherbe, mourn time and exile and return often to the Attic landscape of his childhood.
