Tudor & Elizabethan

Francis Davison

c. 1575–c. 1619

Francis Davison (born about 1575; died in or before 1619) was the eldest son of William Davison, Queen Elizabeth's Secretary of State, who was driven from office after the execution of Mary Queen of Scots. He was admitted to Gray's Inn in 1593, wrote speeches for the Gray's Inn masque of 1594, and in May 1595 set out on his travels abroad.

In 1602 he published A Poetical Rhapsody, an anthology of his own poems, his brother Walter's, those of friends he names only 'Anomos', and pieces by Sidney, Campion, Raleigh, Donne and others; a second edition followed in 1608, a third in 1611, and a fourth in 1621, after his death. This page gathers the poems that A. H. Bullen's edition (1890–91) credits to Francis himself: the eclogues, sonnets, odes, madrigals, epigrams and elegies that carry his name.
