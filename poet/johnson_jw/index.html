American

James Weldon Johnson

1871–1938

James Weldon Johnson (1871–1938) was born in Jacksonville, Florida, and became a poet, lyricist, novelist, diplomat and civil-rights leader. He graduated from Atlanta University, studied at Columbia, was admitted to the Florida bar, and served as U.S. consul in Venezuela and Nicaragua. With his brother J. Rosamond Johnson he wrote songs for the stage, including “Lift Every Voice and Sing.”

Fifty Years & Other Poems (1917), his first book of verse, marked the fiftieth anniversary of emancipation and gathered formal lyrics, sonnets and a closing group of dialect poems. He later became a leading secretary of the NAACP and a figure of the Harlem Renaissance, publishing God’s Trombones (1927) and editing The Book of American Negro Poetry.
