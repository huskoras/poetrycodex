Saul Tchernichovsky

1875–1943

Saul Tchernichovsky (שאול טשרניחובסקי, 1875–1943) was born in Mikhaylovka, in the Crimean steppe of the Russian Empire, and was raised in a Russian-speaking Jewish farming community. He trained as a physician in Heidelberg and Lausanne, practised in Russia, and wrote Hebrew poetry throughout, among the foremost Hebrew poets of his generation. He settled in Palestine in 1931 and died in Jerusalem in 1943.

His verse is known for its pagan and nature-loving strain (the sonnet to the statue of Apollo of 1899 is the famous early example), for long idylls of Jewish life in the Russian countryside, ballads, sonnets and lyrics. He also translated Homer, the Kalevala and much else into Hebrew; none of those translations is included here. The poems given here are drawn from the Dvir edition of his collected poems (Tel Aviv), in the texts prepared by the volunteers of Project Ben-Yehuda.
