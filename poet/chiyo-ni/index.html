Fukuda Chiyo-ni (Kaga no Chiyo)

1703–1775

Fukuda Chiyo-ni (1703–1775), often called Kaga no Chiyo, was a haikai poet of Kaga province, on the Japan Sea coast. One of the best-known women poets of the Edo period, she became a Buddhist nun in 1754, and is remembered above all for her morning-glory verse, in which a well-bucket entangled by the vines is left undisturbed.

These are the hokku that Basil Hall Chamberlain attributes to her in Bashō and the Japanese Poetical Epigram (1902), in romaji with his English renderings where he gives verse. For the morning-glory verse and two others he offers only a literal gloss or a prose paraphrase, which is not reproduced here.
