Taneda Santōka 種田山頭火

1882–1940

種田山頭火 (Taneda Santōka, 1882–1940) was born Taneda Shōichi in Yamaguchi Prefecture. After a failed business and a collapsed family, he was ordained as a Zen monk in 1925 and from 1926 wandered Japan on foot as a mendicant, begging for alms and writing haiku. He died in Matsuyama in 1940.

He belonged to the free-verse school of haiku, which gave up the fixed 5-7-5 measure and the seasonal word, and he is counted with Ozaki Hōsai as its chief poet. 草木塔 (Sōmokutō, 'The Grass-and-Tree Pagoda'), the collection he compiled in 1940, gathers his haiku in book order; this selection takes the opening sections, with the famous 夕立が洗つていつた茄子をもぐ.
