Romanian

George Coșbuc

1866–1918

George Coșbuc (1866–1918) was born in Hordou, a village in the Năsăud region of Transylvania, then part of the Austro-Hungarian Empire. He studied at Năsăud and at the University of Cluj, wrote for the Transylvanian press, and moved to Bucharest in 1889, where he worked as a journalist, editor and translator and became a figure of the Romanian literary world. He died in Bucharest in 1918.

His first volume, Balade și idile (Ballads and Idylls, 1893), brought him wide fame for its verse drawn from village life, folk tale and legend, praised for its rhythmic energy and its closeness to the speech of Romanian peasants. It was followed by Fire de tort (1896), which includes the poem 'Noi vrem pământ!' ('We want land!'), and by later collections. He also translated Virgil's Aeneid, Dante and Kalidasa into Romanian. Several of his poems are staple reading in Romanian schools.
