Restoration & Augustan

William Shenstone

1714–1763

William Shenstone (1714–1763) was an English poet and landscape gardener, born at Halesowen in Shropshire (now the West Midlands) and educated at Pembroke College, Oxford. He is remembered for The School-Mistress (1742), a gently comic imitation of Spenser, and for his Elegies, Odes, Songs and Ballads, which were widely admired in the middle of the eighteenth century.

From 1745 he lived on his family estate, The Leasowes, near Halesowen, which he laid out as a ferme ornee, a working farm shaped into a landscape of walks, cascades and inscriptions. Visitors came from across Britain to see it, and it helped shape the English taste for the picturesque garden. His Works in Verse and Prose appeared after his death, and the poems here follow the two-volume Poetical Works of 1778.
