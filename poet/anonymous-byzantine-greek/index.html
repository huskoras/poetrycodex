Anonymous (Byzantine Greek)

10th–12th c. (Trebizond MS, 16th c.)

Digenes Akrites ("the two-blood border lord") is the best-known Byzantine verse romance, built on the career of a frontier warrior of the Euphrates and Cappadocia who is the son of an Arab emir and a Greek general's daughter. Its author is unknown; the story took shape between the tenth and twelfth centuries, and it survives in several quite different medieval versions written in the fifteen-syllable political verse.

The version given here is the one from Trebizond, a sixteenth-century manuscript that Constantine Sathas brought to light and that he and Émile Legrand published for the first time in 1875 as Les exploits de Digénis Akritas. Its ten books are incomplete at both ends: the first book and the opening of the second are lost, and the tenth breaks off in mid-sentence. The text is in the half-learned, half-vernacular Greek of the period and is printed here in the editors' spelling.
