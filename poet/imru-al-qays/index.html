Imru' al-Qays

6th century

Imru' al-Qays (امرؤ القيس), son of Hujr, of the Kinda tribe, lived in the sixth century, and tradition calls him the first and greatest of the poets of pre-Islamic Arabia. Arab critics hold that he set the pattern of the classical ode: the halt at the abandoned camp, the memory of the beloved, the journey, the hunt and the storm. The dates traditionally given for his birth and death are uncertain.

His opening ode is one of the Mu'allaqat, the long odes of the age of the Jahiliyya, and it begins with the famous words "Qifa nabki" (Halt, let us weep). Here it is followed by the shorter pieces of his diwan that run to three or more verses. The texts are from the Arabic Wikisource transcriptions and keep the vowel marks of that source.
