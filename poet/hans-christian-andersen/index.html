Hans Christian Andersen

1805–1875

Hans Christian Andersen (1805–1875) was born in Odense, the son of a poor shoemaker, and went to Copenhagen at fourteen to seek a career in the theatre. Helped by patrons, notably Jonas Collin, he was sent to grammar school and then to the University of Copenhagen, and he made his name with prose, plays and travel books as well as verse. He is known throughout the world for his fairy tales, begun in 1835, but he first appeared in print as a poet.

The poems here are in Danish, in the spelling of the first printings. They come from his first book, Digte (1830), which he published at his own expense, and from the Samlede Digte of 1833, in which many of the same pieces were reprinted and revised; where a poem had already appeared in the earlier book, only the first version is given. The 1830 volume mixes humorous and fantastic pieces with sentimental lyrics, ballads and folk-tale fancies, several of them opening with epigraphs from German writers.
