Vladimir Mayakovsky

1893–1930

Vladimir Mayakovsky (Владимир Владимирович Маяковский, 1893–1930) was born in Baghdati, in Georgia, then part of the Russian Empire, and moved to Moscow as a teenager. At the Moscow School of Painting, Sculpture and Architecture he fell in with the Futurist painter and poet David Burliuk, and in 1912 he put his name to the manifesto A Slap in the Face of Public Taste. His early poems, such as 'Nate!' and 'Poslushayte!', combine street speech, shock and an unexpected tenderness, and his long poem A Cloud in Trousers (1915) made his name.

After 1917 Mayakovsky put his verse at the service of the Revolution, writing agitational poems and designing posters and captions for the ROSTA windows, and in the 1920s he led the avant-garde group LEF (the Left Front of the Arts). He printed his lines in the stepped 'ladder' form that marks his page, and travelled widely, visiting Paris and, in 1925, the United States, which prompted 'Brooklyn Bridge'. Increasingly at odds with literary officialdom, he shot himself in Moscow on 14 April 1930.
