Guillaume Apollinaire

1880–1918

Guillaume Apollinaire (born Wilhelm Albert Włodzimierz Apolinary Kostrowicki, in Rome on 26 August 1880) was the son of a Polish mother and a father who never acknowledged him. He grew up in Monaco and on the Riviera, settled in Paris around 1900, and made himself a central figure of the avant-garde as poet, art critic and friend of Picasso, Braque and Marie Laurencin. He served in the French army in the First World War, was wounded in the head in March 1916, and died in Paris on 9 November 1918, two days before the Armistice.

His poems of 1898 to 1912 were gathered in Alcools (1913), which dispenses with punctuation; Le Bestiaire ou Cortège d'Orphée (1911), a set of short animal poems, first appeared with woodcuts by Raoul Dufy. Calligrammes (1918) joins war poems and love poems to typographic experiments in which the lines are arranged as pictures. This selection takes the whole of Alcools, the thirty poems of Le Bestiaire, and the poems of Calligrammes that read as ordinary lines of verse; the figured calligrams are left out.
