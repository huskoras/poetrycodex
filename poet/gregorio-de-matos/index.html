Gregório de Matos

1636-1696

Gregório de Matos Guerra (Salvador, Bahia, 1636 - Recife, 1696) studied at Coimbra and served as a magistrate in Portugal before returning to Brazil, where he became the leading poet of the colonial Baroque. His savage satires on the governors, clergy, merchants and mixed society of Bahia earned him the nickname 'Boca do Inferno' (Mouth of Hell) and, in 1694, deportation to Angola.

He also wrote refined love lyrics and devotional sonnets of repentance in the manner of Góngora and Quevedo. None of his poems was printed in his lifetime; they survive in manuscript copies, first collected in the Academia Brasileira edition of the Obras (1923-1933).
