Ibn Zaydun

1003–1071

Ibn Zaydun (Abu al-Walid Ahmad ibn Abd Allah ibn Zaydun al-Makhzumi, 1003–1071) was born in Córdoba, in the last years of the Umayyad caliphate, into a family of standing, and became the leading Arabic poet and court secretary of eleventh-century al-Andalus. He rose to be a vizier of Ibn Jahwar, who ruled Córdoba after the caliphate fell, but was imprisoned on a charge of treason, escaped, and spent years away from his city before entering the service of the Abbadid rulers of Seville, al-Mu'tadid and then al-Mu'tamid. He died in Seville in 1071.

He is remembered above all for his love for Wallada bint al-Mustakfi, the poet-princess of Córdoba, to whom much of his love poetry is addressed. His long nun-rhymed ode of separation, 'Aḍḥā al-tanā'ī badīlan 'an tadānīnā', is among the best-loved poems of Andalusian Arabic literature. The texts here are transcriptions of his diwan from Arabic Wikisource, with the vocalisation of the source; the pieces of three or more lines are given.
