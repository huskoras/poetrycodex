Franciszek Karpiński

1741–1825

Franciszek Karpiński (1741–1825) was born in Galicia, studied in Lwów, and after some years in the households of the Czartoryski family and at the court of King Stanisław August he settled as a leaseholder on the land, where he lived out his last decades in retirement. He is the Polish “poet of the heart”: his idylls and love songs (Laura i Filon, the Justyna lyrics) bring the voice of the village and a plain, tender feeling into the verse of the Enlightenment, and he wrote a few elegies on the fall of the old Commonwealth, of which the Żale Sarmaty over the tomb of Sigismund Augustus is the best known.

His Pieśni nabożne, printed by the Basilian press at Supraśl in 1792, gave Poland songs that are still sung: the Christmas carol “Bóg się rodzi” (Song of the Nativity), the morning hymn “Kiedy ranne wstają zorze” and the evening hymn “Wszystkie nasze dzienne sprawy”. The Pieśni nabożne are given here in the spelling of the 1792 print (the long s normalised to s), a selection from the whole book; the secular poems come from Baumfeld's anthology Klejnoty poezji staropolskiej (1919), in modernised spelling, and two fables from Ejsmond's Antologia bajki polskiej (1915).
