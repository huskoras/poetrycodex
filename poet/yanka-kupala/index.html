Yanka Kupala

1882-1942

Yanka Kupala (born Ivan Lutsevich, 1882-1942) is a classic of Belarusian literature and a leading figure of the Belarusian national revival of the early twentieth century. He was born at Vyazynka, near Maladzechna in the Minsk region, and became known from 1905 as a poet of the newspaper Nasha Niva. His first book, Zhaleika ("The Reed Pipe", 1908), was followed by Huslyar (1910), Shliakham zhyccia ("Along the Road of Life", 1913) and Spadchyna ("Inheritance", 1922); a collected works began to appear in 1925.

Kupala wrote of the peasant's hard lot, of the homeland and its buried past, of song and the poet's calling, and of the dream of an awakened people, in lyrics, ballads and narrative poems such as Kurhan, Mahila lva and Yana i ya. He died in Moscow in 1942. This selection gives his poems in Belarusian from Belarusian Wikisource, in the classical (tarashkevitsa-type) spelling of the lifetime editions as transcribed there, kept unmodernised; his plays, translations and journalism are left out.
