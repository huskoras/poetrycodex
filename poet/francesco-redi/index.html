Francesco Redi

1626–1697

Francesco Redi (1626–1697) was born in Arezzo and became one of the leading physicians and naturalists of the Medici court in Florence, serving the Grand Dukes Ferdinando II and Cosimo III as court physician. His Experiments on the Generation of Insects (1668) tested, by controlled comparison, the belief that maggots arise spontaneously from decaying flesh, and he also wrote on vipers, parasites and the nature of poisons. A member of the Accademia del Cimento, he was also a respected philologist, and the Accademia della Crusca, whose dictionary he helped to compile, counted him among its most active members.

Alongside his scientific work Redi was a gifted poet in Tuscan. His dithyramb Bacco in Toscana (1685) is a comic, learned and exuberant celebration of Tuscan wines, in which Bacchus, seated with Ariadne on the hills above Florence, sips and judges the vintages of the grand duchy and condemns coffee, tea, chocolate and water. Its irregular, rhymed lines of varying length made it one of the most admired and imitated poems of the Italian Baroque. It is paired here with Leigh Hunt's verse translation, Bacchus in Tuscany (1825), which turns the dithyramb into rollicking English.
