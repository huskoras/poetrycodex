Christian Fürchtegott Gellert

1715–1769

Christian Fürchtegott Gellert (1715–1769) was born in Hainichen in Saxony and became a professor of poetry, rhetoric and morals at the University of Leipzig. He was among the most widely read German authors of the mid-eighteenth century, admired for the clarity and gentleness of his style, and he also wrote comedies, a novel and a celebrated body of letters.

His Fabeln und Erzählungen (1746–48) turned the verse fable into a polished vehicle of everyday moral observation, in light, easily memorised couplets and irregular rhymed lines, and they made him famous across Germany. His Geistliche Oden und Lieder (1757), many of them set to music by C. P. E. Bach, remained in the hymnals long after, and Beethoven later set several of them.
