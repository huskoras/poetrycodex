Korean

Han Yongun

1879–1944

Han Yongun (한용운 韓龍雲, 1879–1944), who wrote under the pen name Manhae (만해 萬海, 'Sea of Ten Thousand'), was a Korean Buddhist monk, reformer and poet, born in Hongseong in South Chungcheong Province. He took Buddhist orders in 1905 and became one of the leading voices for the renewal of Korean Buddhism.

He was one of the thirty-three signatories of the Korean Declaration of Independence in 1919 and was imprisoned for his part in the March First Movement. His one volume of verse, 님의 침묵 (Nim-ui chimmuk, 'The Silence of the Beloved'), appeared in 1926; its poems address an absent 'nim', at once lover, nation and the Buddha. He died in Seoul in 1944, shortly before Korea's liberation.
