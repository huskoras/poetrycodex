Giacomo da Lentini

fl. 1210–1260

Giacomo da Lentini (fl. 1210–1260), called 'il Notaro', was a notary at the court of the Emperor Frederick II in Sicily and the leading poet of the Sicilian School, the first body of lyric poetry in an Italian vernacular. Dante's Purgatorio (XXIV) names him in connection with the 'sweet new style' and calls him 'the Notary'. He is credited with inventing the sonnet, a form that he seems to have shaped from the Sicilian stanza and that later passed to Tuscan poets and on to Petrarch and the whole European tradition.

The texts given here are Giacomo's Italian originals, in the Wikisource transcription of Roberto Antonelli's edition (Bulzoni, 1979): canzoni, then sonnets, in the editor's numbering, including his share of the tenzoni (poetic debates) with the Abate di Tivoli and with Iacopo Mostacci and Pier della Vigna. The sonnets of his correspondents are not included; the three poems of doubtful attribution that close the edition are marked as such. Two canzoni that survive only as fragments of a couple of lines are omitted. Dante Gabriel Rossetti's English versions of seven of these poems are in the Rossetti collection.
