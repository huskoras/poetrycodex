T’ao Ch’ien

AD 365–427

T’ao Ch’ien (AD 365–427) gave up official life to farm his own land, and his poems of retirement, wine, poverty and the pleasure of a small house and garden are among the best loved in Chinese. Waley devoted a whole chapter of his 1918 anthology to him.

The poems here are Arthur Waley’s versions in A Hundred and Seventy Chinese Poems (1918), given with the poet’s name as spelled in those books.
