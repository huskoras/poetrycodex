William Lisle Bowles

1762–1850

William Lisle Bowles (1762–1850) was an English clergyman and poet, rector of Bremhill in Wiltshire. His Fourteen Sonnets (1789), written during journeys through England, Scotland and the Rhineland, were warmly admired by the young Coleridge and helped to revive the sonnet as a form of personal reflection.

His longer poems include The Spirit of Discovery (1804), The Missionary (1813) and several narrative and devotional poems. In the 1820s he took part in a famous controversy with Byron and others about the poetry of Pope. The texts here are from Gilfillan's edition (Edinburgh, Nichol, 1855), volumes I and II; translations, the editor's memoir and notes are omitted.
