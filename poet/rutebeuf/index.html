Rutebeuf

fl. 1245–1285

Rutebeuf (fl. 1245–1285) was a Parisian trouvère of the thirteenth century, one of the first French poets to write openly in his own voice. Nothing is known of his life beyond what his poems tell, and even his name may be a professional nickname.

He is remembered for the poems of misfortune, in which he speaks of poverty, a bad marriage, failing sight and the loss of patrons, and for his satires on the mendicant orders, his defence of the University of Paris, his laments for dead lords and his songs urging crusade, besides poems to the Virgin. This selection follows the spelling of Achille Jubinal's 1839 edition; the fabliaux, the prose-and-verse monologue and the Miracle de Théophile are not included.
