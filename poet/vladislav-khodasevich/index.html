Russian & Slavic

Vladislav Khodasevich

1886–1939

Vladislav Felitsianovich Khodasevich (1886–1939) was born in Moscow into a family of Polish descent on his father's side. He began as a poet close to the Symbolists and published his first book, Molodost' (Youth), in 1908, followed by Schastlivyi domik (1914), Putyom zerna (1920), Tyazhelaya lira (1922) and Evropeiskaya noch' (1927).

His verse is exact, classical in metre and often dark in tone: it keeps the forms of Pushkin and Baratynsky while speaking of exile, the divided self and the small details of modern life. After the Revolution he worked in Moscow and Petrograd, and left Soviet Russia in 1922 with the writer Nina Berberova.

He lived in Berlin, Italy and from 1925 in Paris, where he wrote criticism and a life of Derzhavin and the memoir Necropolis. He died in Paris on 14 June 1939, aged fifty-three.
