Anonymous (Italian popular poetry)

13th–16th century

Anonymous Italian popular verse of the fourteenth to sixteenth centuries survives in the ballate, strambotti and rispetti that singers and scribes passed from hand to hand, and in the cradle-songs and pastoral songs of the countryside. Its voices are usually those of women and country people, the young wife shut up in a convent or married to an old man, the girl who watches the nightingale, the labourer in the field, the mother rocking her child, and its subject is almost always love, set in a few repeated lines that a refrain can carry.

This selection takes the eleven anonymous popular poems that G. L. Lucas prints in The Oxford Book of Italian Verse (1910), where they stand under the heading Ignoto, 'unknown', with a century and sometimes a city (the first is marked Venice, fourteenth century). Each is given here in Lucas's text and spelling, titled by its first line, and without his numbers, dates or notes.

Poems (11)

  1. Deh! lassa mi, tapina sagurata
  2. Era tutta soletta
  3. Piacessi a Dio che e' non fosse mai nata!
  4. Cantando un giorno in voce umile e lieve
  5. Mamma, il bel lusignolo
  6. La mi tenne la staffa
  7. Che bella vita ha al mondo un villanello
  8. Deh! lasso, quanto dolorosamente
  9. Figlio, dormi, dormi, figlio
  10. Se io potessi far, fanciulla bella
  11. La pastorella si leva per tempo
