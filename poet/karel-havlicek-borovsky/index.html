Karel Havlíček Borovský

1821–1856

Karel Havlíček Borovský (1821–1856), born in Borová (now Havlíčkova Borová), was the foremost Czech journalist and satirist of the National Revival. He studied in Prague, spent a year in Russia as a tutor and teacher in 1843–44, and in 1848 founded the newspaper Národní noviny, which he edited until it was suppressed.

The Austrian authorities banished him in 1851 to Brixen in the Tyrol, where he wrote the Tyrolské elegie, the mock-heroic Křest svatého Vladimíra and the folktale satire Král Lávra. Allowed home in 1855, he died of tuberculosis at Prague in 1856; his epigrams are among the sharpest in Czech verse.
