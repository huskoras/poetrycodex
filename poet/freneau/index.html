American

Philip Freneau

1752–1832

Philip Freneau (1752–1832) was an American poet and journalist, born in New York City. He studied at Princeton alongside James Madison and became known as the “Poet of the American Revolution” for his satires on the British and his patriotic verse.

He later edited the National Gazette in Philadelphia. His nature lyrics, such as “The Wild Honey Suckle” (1786), are often seen as early signs of Romanticism in American poetry.
