Dutch & Flemish

Jan Luyken

1649–1712

Jan Luyken (1649–1712) was an Amsterdam etcher, draughtsman and poet, one of the finest book illustrators of the Dutch Golden Age. His first book of verse, the love songs of Duytse lier (1671), was a worldly success; soon afterwards he turned to a strict, inward religious life and gave up the secular songs of his youth.

From the late 1670s he joined his two arts in the emblem book: each poem faces an engraved picture and a short meditation. Jezus en de ziel (1678) was the first of these, a dialogue of the soul and Christ in three parts, and it was followed by further emblem books on daily life and the trades. He engraved many Bible and martyr illustrations, and he died in Amsterdam in 1712.

The poems here are taken from the 1685 printing of Jezus en de ziel, in the old Dutch spelling of the book: the opening Voor-sangh, the thirty-nine emblem poems of the three parts, and the closing Toesang and Sang. The prose meditations, scripture mottoes and the verses set inside the meditations are left out.
