French

Paul Éluard

1895–1952

Paul Éluard, born Eugène Émile Paul Grindel at Saint-Denis on 14 December 1895, took his pen name from his grandmother’s family name. After a youth interrupted by tuberculosis and service in the First World War, he joined the Dada circle in Paris and then, with André Breton and Louis Aragon, became one of the central figures of Surrealism. Capitale de la douleur (Gallimard, 1926) gathered his poems of those years, and the collection included “L’Amoureuse”, among the best-loved French love poems of the century.

In 1942, under the German Occupation, he joined the Resistance, and his poem “Liberté” became one of its best-known texts. He remained a prominent poet and a member of the Communist Party until the end of his life, and he died at Charenton-le-Pont on 18 November 1952.
