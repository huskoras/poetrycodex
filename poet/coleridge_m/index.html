Victorian

Mary E. Coleridge

1861–1907

Mary Elizabeth Coleridge (1861–1907) was born in London into the family of Samuel Taylor Coleridge, to whom she was related as a great-great-niece. She wrote novels, essays and reviews, but her poems, which she published only anonymously or under the pseudonym Anodos ("Wanderer", taken from George MacDonald's Phantastes), are what last. They first appeared in Fancy's Following (1896, a private-press edition of 125 copies) and Fancy's Guerdon (1897).

She wished her verse to stay unsigned during her lifetime, fearing to tarnish a famous name. After her death Henry Newbolt gathered the seventy-seven pieces already printed, together with the rest of her uncollected work, in Poems (1908). The book shows her in the short lyric: terse, musical and intimate, with an eye for the strange and the uncanny. Robert Bridges, who knew her, was among her early admirers.
