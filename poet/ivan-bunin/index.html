Ivan Bunin

1870–1953

Ivan Bunin (1870–1953) was a Russian poet and prose writer, born in Voronezh into an impoverished gentry family. He left Russia after the Revolution and from 1920 lived in exile in France, mostly at Grasse. In 1933 he became the first Russian writer to win the Nobel Prize in Literature.

His verse keeps to the classical Russian lyric line of Pushkin and Fet, clear in diction and exact in its images of the countryside, the seasons and memory. He received the Pushkin Prize of the Academy of Sciences in 1903, for his poems and his translation of Longfellow's Hiawatha, and again in 1909. Here three poems are given as printed in the Wikisource text of the 2014 Pushkin House edition.
