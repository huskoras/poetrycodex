Diodorus Siculus

c. 90 – c. 30 BC

Diodorus Siculus, “Diodorus of Sicily”, was a Greek historian born at Agyrium (modern Agira) in Sicily, who wrote in the first century BC. By his own account he travelled in Egypt around 60–56 BC and spent many years in Rome gathering his materials, and he says the whole undertaking took him thirty years. Almost nothing else is known of his life.

His Bibliotheca historica, or Historical Library, set out to tell the history of the known world in forty books, from the mythic ages of Egypt, Asia and Greece down to Caesar's Gallic wars. Books I–V and XI–XX survive complete; the rest, including the lost Books VI–X, are known only from fragments and excerpts. Diodorus is a compiler rather than an original researcher, but he preserved much from historians whose own works are lost.

George Booth's English translation first appeared in 1700 and was reprinted in two volumes in London in 1814. The archive carries it as two works: volume 1 (Books I–V and XI–XIV) and volume 2 (Books XV–XX and the fragments of the lost books).

Major Works (2)

  1. The Historical Library, Volume I: Books I–V and XI–XIV — translated by George Booth (1700; 1814 reprint)
  2. The Historical Library, Volume II: Books XV–XX and Fragments — translated by George Booth (1700; 1814 reprint)
