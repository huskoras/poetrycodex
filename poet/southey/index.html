Robert Southey

1774–1843

Robert Southey (1774–1843) was an English poet and man of letters, Poet Laureate from 1813 until his death, and one of the “Lake Poets” alongside his friends Wordsworth and Coleridge (whose sister-in-law he married).

He was, in his own day, best known for his grim and often macabre ballads of guilt, folklore and the supernatural, and for prose-poem “Eclogues” that gave a plain, compassionate voice to the rural poor — work that has aged rather better than the long epics on which he staked his reputation.
