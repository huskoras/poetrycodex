Baha al-Din Zuhayr

1186–1258

Baha al-Din Zuhayr (Abu'l-Fadl Zuhayr ibn Muhammad al-Muhallabi, 1186 to 1258) was an Arabic poet of Ayyubid Egypt. Born at or near Mecca, he made his career in the service of the Ayyubid sultan al-Malik al-Salih Najm al-Din Ayyub, whose confidential secretary and trusted minister he became, and he lived in Cairo. After his master's death in 1249 he withdrew into private life. He died in Cairo in November 1258, in an epidemic, and was buried near the tomb of the Imam al-Shafi'i.

His diwan, arranged by himself in alphabetical order of the rhyme letters, was edited and translated by Edward Henry Palmer, Lord Almoner's Professor of Arabic at Cambridge: the Arabic text appeared in 1876 and the metrical English translation, with notes and introduction, as The Poetical Works of Beha-ed-Din Zoheir of Egypt, vol. II (Cambridge University Press, 1877). The pieces given here are Palmer's English versions, taken under his own English headings in the order of the book; they are free rhymed renderings, light and playful in tone, of epigrams, love poems, letters in verse and poems of praise to his patrons.
