Karoline von Günderrode

1780–1806

Karoline von Günderrode (1780–1806) was a German Romantic poet. After her father's early death she entered the Cecilienstift, a Protestant convent for unmarried noblewomen (a Stift) in Frankfurt am Main, where she lived as a canoness (Stiftsdame). She read widely in history, philosophy and the literature of the East, and her poems and verse plays draw on classical, Indian and Persian material as well as on the lyric of her own day.

She published under the pseudonym 'Tian': Gedichte und Phantasien (1804) and Poetische Fragmente (1805). She was a close friend of Bettina Brentano (later von Arnim), who later made her the subject of her book Die Günderode (1840), and she moved in the circle of the Frankfurt and Heidelberg Romantics. Her late poems were gathered in the Melete cycle and her other unprinted verse was collected after her death.

Günderrode died by suicide on the bank of the Rhine at Winkel in 1806, aged twenty-six. The poems here follow the TextGrid Digitale Bibliothek text of her Gesammelte Werke, edited by Leopold Hirschberg (Berlin-Wilmersdorf, 1920–1922), and begin with the sonnet 'Der Kuß im Traume'; the German spellings of that edition are kept.
