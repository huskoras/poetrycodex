Ancient Greek & Roman

Sappho

c. 630 – c. 570 BC

Sappho was a Greek lyric poet from the island of Lesbos, active around the turn of the 7th to 6th century BC. Ancient critics ranked her among the very greatest poets; Plato is said to have called her the "Tenth Muse."

Almost none of her work survives complete. What remains — quotations in later grammarians, and a few papyrus scraps recovered from Egypt — comes down to us as fragments, some no more than a phrase, prized for their directness of feeling and technical mastery of the Aeolic dialect and sapphic metre.

The fragments gathered here follow Henry Thornton Wharton’s 1885 literal prose translation, the edition that first made Sappho’s surviving words accessible to English readers in their entirety.
