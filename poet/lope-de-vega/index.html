Lope de Vega

1562–1635

Félix Lope de Vega y Carpio was born in Madrid in 1562 and died there in 1635. He was the most prolific dramatist of the Spanish Golden Age, credited with hundreds of surviving plays, and he also wrote lyric and epic verse, prose romances and pastoral fiction. His life was famously turbulent: he was exiled from Madrid in 1588 after a libel case, served in the Armada the same year, and was ordained a priest in 1614.

His lyric poetry is among the finest of the age. The sonnets gathered in the Rimas (1602) and the devotional Rimas sacras (1614) range from Petrarchan love complaint, with its Lucinda and Camila, through classical and mythological subjects to confession and prayer. The archive draws the Spanish texts from the Wikisource transcriptions of the public-domain editions, the first 99 of the many sonnets held there, beginning with those that Menéndez y Pelayo and Ford chose for their anthologies. Verse from the plays is not included.
