Russian & Slavic

Julian Tuwim

1894-1953

Julian Tuwim (1894–1953), born in Łódź, was one of the leading Polish poets of the interwar years and a founder of the Skamander group in 1916. He studied law and philology in Warsaw but left university to write, and his first book, Czyhanie na Boga (Lying in Wait for God, 1918), announced a vitalist, exuberant voice that celebrated the city, the body and ordinary things.

He went on to write cabaret texts, translations from Russian, French and German, and a famous body of verse for children, and he became one of the most widely read Polish poets. Of Jewish descent, he left Poland in 1939 and returned in 1946. This selection draws on his first book of 1918.
