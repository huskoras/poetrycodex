Matteo Maria Boiardo

1441–1494

Matteo Maria Boiardo (1441–1494) was a count of Scandiano, born into a noble family in the territory of Reggio Emilia and educated at the court of the Este in Ferrara, where he spent most of his life in the service of Duke Ercole I. He was governor of Reggio from 1478 and of Modena's hill country of the Frignano, and ended his life as captain of Reggio. He wrote Latin eclogues, a translation of Herodotus, and the Amorum libri tres, a sonnet cycle of 180 poems in the Petrarchan manner.

His masterpiece is the Orlando innamorato, a chivalric romance in ottava rima that joins the Carolingian matter of Charlemagne's paladins to the Arthurian theme of love. Its first two books appeared in 1483; a third was left unfinished at his death in 1494, in the ninth canto, as the French army entered Italy. Ariosto took up the story in the Orlando furioso.
