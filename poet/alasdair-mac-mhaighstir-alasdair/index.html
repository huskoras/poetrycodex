Alasdair mac Mhaighstir Alasdair

c.1698–c.1770

Alasdair mac Mhaighstir Alasdair (Alexander MacDonald) was born about 1698 at Dalilea in Moidart, the son of an Episcopalian minister. He became schoolmaster of Ardnamurchan, joined Prince Charles Edward Stuart in the Rising of 1745, and became the most fiery of the Jacobite poets in Gaelic. In 1751 he published Ais-eiridh na Sean Chànoin Albannaich, the first book of secular Gaelic verse printed in Scotland; he died about 1770.

His Birlinn Chlann Raghnaill, the voyage of a Clanranald galley from South Uist to Carrickfergus through a great storm, is generally reckoned the summit of Gaelic poetry, and his Moladh Mòraig and his nature songs are among its most admired pieces. The texts here are taken from John Mackenzie's Sar-Obair nam Bard Gaelach (Glasgow, 1841), in his spelling; his prose life, notes and glossary are left out.
