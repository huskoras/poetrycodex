Quintus Curtius Rufus

1st century AD

Quintus Curtius Rufus was a Roman historian whose life is almost entirely unknown. His date is disputed: scholars place him in the first century AD, most often under the emperor Claudius or under Vespasian, and the attempt to identify him with a senator named Curtius Rufus remains uncertain.

His only surviving work is the Historiae Alexandri Magni, a Latin history of Alexander the Great in ten books. The first two books are lost, and the text breaks off or begins mid-sentence at several points in Books V, VI and X. The surviving books follow the campaigns from Asia Minor to Alexander's death at Babylon and the division of his empire, in a rhetorical style that made the work popular from the Middle Ages onwards. John Digby's translation was printed in London in 1714.

Major Works (1)

  1. The History of the Wars of Alexander — translated by John Digby (1714)
