Dante Alighieri

1265–1321

Dante Alighieri was born in Florence in 1265 and became one of the city's political figures as well as its greatest poet. His early lyrics and the prose-and-verse Vita Nuova (c. 1294) celebrate Beatrice, the woman he loved from afar and who later became his guide in the Paradiso.

In 1302, on the losing side of Florence's factional struggles, he was sentenced to exile and never returned. During those years he wrote the Divine Comedy, a vision in rhymed tercets of the soul's journey through Hell, Purgatory and Heaven, composed in the Tuscan vernacular rather than Latin. He died in Ravenna in 1321, soon after finishing it.
