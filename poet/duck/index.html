Stephen Duck

1705–1756

Stephen Duck (1705–1756) was an English poet, born in Wiltshire and working as an agricultural labourer when he began to write verse. His poem The Thresher's Labour, which describes the farm worker's year of toil, became known beyond his village and brought him to the notice of Queen Caroline, who gave him a pension and a place at court.

He later took holy orders and served as a clergyman and as keeper of the Queen's library at Richmond. Poems on Several Occasions (1736), the collection drawn on here, gathers his verse, including The Thresher's Labour, longer narrative poems such as Avaro and Amanda, and imitations of Horace.
