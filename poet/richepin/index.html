Jean Richepin

1849–1926

Jean Richepin (1849–1926) was a French poet, novelist and dramatist, born in Médéa in Algeria. A graduate of the École normale supérieure, he made his name with La Chanson des gueux (1881), a book of poems in the voices of beggars, tramps, street children and vagabonds, written in a mixture of lyrical French and the slang of the streets.

The book brought him a month in prison for offending public morals, and a notable celebrity; its title poem, the "Ballade du Roi des Gueux", declares the poet king of the poor. Richepin went on to write further volumes of verse, among them Les Blasphèmes (1884), as well as novels and many plays, and was elected to the Académie française in 1908. The poems here are taken from the first edition of La Chanson des gueux (Maurice Dreyfous, 1881) and keep its slang and its dialect spellings.
