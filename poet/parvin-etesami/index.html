Parvin E'tesami

1907–1941

Parvin E'tesami (پروین اعتصامی) was born at Tabriz in 1907 and died at Tehran in 1941. Her father, Yusuf E'tesami, was a well-known writer and translator, and she was educated in Tehran, where she later worked as a librarian. She is regarded as the foremost Iranian woman poet of the twentieth century.

Her only collection, the Divan, appeared in 1935 and was enlarged in 1941. It is best known for its munazaras (debates) and tamsils (parables), short rhymed fables in which a tear, a thread, a sparrow or a thief argues a point of conscience in the manner of the classical Persian moralists. Her subjects are poverty, hypocrisy, orphans and the injustice of the powerful. The text here is her Persian, taken from Ganjoor: first the masnavis, parables and fragments, in Ganjoor order.
