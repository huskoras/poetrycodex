James Beattie

1735–1803

James Beattie (1735–1803) was a Scottish poet and moral philosopher, for many years professor of moral philosophy at Marischal College, Aberdeen. He was also known for his Essay on the Nature and Immutability of Truth (1770), written against Hume.

His best-known poem is The Minstrel (1771–74), in Spenserian stanzas, which traces the growth of a young poet, Edwin, among the hills and shepherds of the Scottish countryside. It was much admired by later eighteenth- and early nineteenth-century readers. The texts here are from Gilfillan's edition of Beattie, Blair and Falconer.
