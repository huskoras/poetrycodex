Erik Axel Karlfeldt

1864–1931

Erik Axel Karlfeldt (1864–1931) was a Swedish poet born Erik Axel Eriksson at Folkärna in Dalarna, the son of a farmer. He studied at Uppsala, took his name Karlfeldt in 1889, and made the peasant life, landscape and history of Dalarna and Västmanland the matter of his verse. His first book, Vildmarks- och kärleksvisor (1895), was followed by Fridolins visor och andra dikter (1898) and Fridolins lustgård (1901), with its Dalmålningar på rim.

He joined the Swedish Academy in 1904 and was its permanent secretary from 1912. He declined the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1918, and it was awarded to him posthumously in 1931, the year of his death in Stockholm. This selection takes the poems of the first three books in the order of the collected edition, Dikter (Bonniers, 1943), as transcribed on Swedish Wikisource, keeping the original spelling.
