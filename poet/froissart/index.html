Jean Froissart

c. 1337–c. 1405

Jean Froissart was born about 1337 at Valenciennes in Hainaut and trained as a clerk. Best known as the chronicler of the early Hundred Years’ War, he travelled widely and served patrons at the courts of Philippa of Hainault in England, of Gaston Phoebus at Orthez and of Wenceslas of Brabant. He seems to have died about 1405, probably at Chimay.

He was also a courtly poet in the tradition of Guillaume de Machaut, writing the narrative dits (among them L’Espinette amoureuse, L’Horloge amoureuse and Le Joli Buisson de Jonece), a long Arthurian romance, Méliador, and many short lyrics in the fixed forms: ballades, rondeaux, virelais and lays. The pieces gathered here are the short lyrics, mostly those that Froissart sets inside his dits, in the old spelling of Buchon’s edition of 1829.
