Korean

Jeong Cheol

1536–1593

Jeong Cheol (정철, 鄭澈, 1536–1593), known by his pen name Songgang (松江, "Pine River"), was a Joseon statesman and one of the great poets of the Korean language. He rose to high office at the court of King Seonjo, took a leading part in the Westerners (서인) faction, and spent several periods in exile and retirement; he died in 1593.

He is remembered above all as the master of the gasa, the long Korean verse form of the Joseon scholar-officials: Gwandong byeolgok (關東別曲), a journey through the Diamond Mountains and the east coast, Samiingok and Sokmiingok, the two "Songs of the Beautiful Lady" in which a woman's longing stands for a minister's loyalty to his king, and Seongsan byeolgok (星山別曲). His sijo, among them the sixteen Hunmin-ga (訓民歌) moral songs for the villagers of Gangwon Province, are gathered with the gasa in the Songgang gasa. This selection follows the Hwangju edition as transcribed on Wikisource, in Middle Korean with hanja and their hangul readings, as printed.
