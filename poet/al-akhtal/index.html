al-Akhtal

c. 640–c. 710

al-Akhtal (الأخطل, c. 640–c. 710), whose given name was Ghiyath ibn Ghawth, belonged to the Banu Taghlib and was a Christian. He became the leading poet of the Umayyad court at Damascus, where he praised the caliphs and their governors, and he is counted with Jarir and al-Farazdaq as one of the three great poets of his age.

He is remembered for his tribal satires, his part in the long poetic quarrels (naqa'id) with Jarir, and his wine poems. This selection gathers the longer poems collected in his diwan on Arabic Wikisource.
