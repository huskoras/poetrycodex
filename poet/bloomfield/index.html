Robert Bloomfield

1766–1823

Robert Bloomfield (1766–1823) was an English poet of labouring-class origins, born in Suffolk. He worked as a farm boy and then as a shoemaker in London, where he composed The Farmer's Boy in his head while he worked.

The Farmer's Boy (1800), a poem of rural life in four seasons, was introduced to the public by Capel Lofft and had a very large sale. Rural Tales, Ballads, and Songs followed in 1802 and May-Day with the Muses in 1822. He died in poverty at Shefford, Bedfordshire. The texts here are from early editions on Project Gutenberg; the prefaces and prose supplements are omitted.
