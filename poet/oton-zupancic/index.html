Oton Župančič

1878–1949

Oton Župančič (1878–1949) was born at Vinica in Bela krajina, in the south-east of Slovenia, and died in Ljubljana. With Ivan Cankar, Dragotin Kette and Josip Murn he is counted among the founders of Slovene modernism, and after the First World War he was often regarded as the greatest Slovene poet since Prešeren. He edited the review Ljubljanski zvon and also worked as a playwright and as a translator, notably of Shakespeare.

His first book, Čaša opojnosti (The Cup of Intoxication, 1899), was followed by Čez plan (Across the Plain, 1904), whose opening cycle mourns Josip Murn Aleksandrov, and by Samogovori (Soliloquies, 1908), which closes with the long free-verse poem Duma. This selection draws on those three books and adds Slap (The Waterfall), printed in the review Slovan in 1914. His children's verse and his translations are not included.
