American

George Moses Horton

c. 1798–c. 1883

George Moses Horton was born into slavery in North Carolina around 1798 and spent most of his life on the farm of his owner in Chatham County. Unable at first to write, he composed verses in his head and, on Sundays, walked to Chapel Hill, where students of the University of North Carolina paid him to compose love poems for them.

His first book, The Hope of Liberty (1829), is generally recognised as the first book of poetry by a Black author published in the South. Its poems were reissued in Philadelphia in 1837 as Poems by a Slave, the edition from which these pieces are taken. He remained enslaved until 1865, when Union forces arrived in North Carolina and he gained his freedom.
