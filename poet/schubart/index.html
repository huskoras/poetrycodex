Christian Friedrich Daniel Schubart

1739–1791

Christian Friedrich Daniel Schubart (1739–1791) was a Swabian poet, organist, composer and journalist, born at Obersontheim and active in Geislingen, Ludwigsburg, Augsburg and Ulm. His Deutsche Chronik, a newspaper he wrote almost single-handed from 1774, made him famous for its sharp comment on princes, priests and the politics of the day.

In 1777 Duke Karl Eugen of Württemberg had him seized and confined without trial in the fortress of Hohenasperg, where he stayed for ten years. In prison he wrote much of his best verse, among it “Die Gruft der Fürsten”, an indictment of tyrants, and the song “Die Forelle”, later set by Franz Schubert. Released in 1787, he resumed the Chronik and died at Stuttgart in 1791.
