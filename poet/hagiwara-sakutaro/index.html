Hagiwara Sakutarō

1886–1942

萩原朔太郎 (Hagiwara Sakutarō, 1886–1942) was born in Maebashi, Gunma Prefecture, and is regarded as the poet who gave modern Japanese free verse in the colloquial language its first fully mature voice. He wrote in the Taishō period, was a friend and fellow poet of Murō Saisei, and was encouraged by Kitahara Hakushū, who wrote a preface to his first book.

Here is his first collection, 月に吠える (Howling at the Moon), published in 1917, in the 55 poems that the Aozora file prints, two of them long: five sections (竹とその哀傷, 雲雀料理, 悲しい月夜, くさつた蛤, さびしい情慾), then 見知らぬ犬 and 長詩二篇. The text is that of Aozora Bunko, with the reading aids (ruby) and editorial marks removed; the prefaces, the notes and the essay by another writer printed in the same file are left out.
