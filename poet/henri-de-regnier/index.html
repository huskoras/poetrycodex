French

Henri de Régnier

1864–1936

Henri de Régnier (Honfleur, 1864 – Paris, 1936) was a leading French poet and novelist of the Symbolist generation. A regular at Mallarmé’s Tuesday gatherings in Paris, he made his name with the fluid, richly musical free verse and sonnets of Épisodes (1888–1891), Poèmes anciens et romanesques (1890) and Tel qu’en songe (1892), in which dream, memory and a half-legendary landscape of gardens, fountains and waters dominate. In 1895 he married Marie de Heredia, daughter of the Parnassian poet José-Maria de Heredia; he was elected to the Académie française in 1911.

From the later 1890s his verse turned toward a more classical manner, with regular stanzas and a lucid, Greek-flavoured pastoral: Les Jeux rustiques et divins (1897), Les Médailles d’argile (1900), La Cité des eaux (1902), La Sandale ailée (1906) and, late in life, Vestigia flammae (1921). He was also a prolific novelist and essayist (La Double Maîtresse, 1900). This selection takes the three poems of his that appear in the Oxford Book of French Verse and a choice from Épisodes, Tel qu’en songe, Les Jeux rustiques et divins and Les Médailles d’argile, in the original French.
