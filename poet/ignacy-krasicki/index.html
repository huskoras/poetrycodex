Ignacy Krasicki

1735–1801

Ignacy Krasicki (1735–1801) was a Polish bishop, satirist and fabulist, long called the “prince of Polish poets”. Born into a noble family at Dubiecko, he took holy orders and became a friend and adviser of King Stanisław August Poniatowski, at whose Warsaw table the writers of the Polish Enlightenment gathered. In 1766 he was made Prince-Bishop of Warmia, and in 1795 Archbishop of Gniezno; he died in Berlin.

His best-known verse belongs to the 1770s: the mock-heroic poems Myszeida (1775) and Monachomachia (1778), the twenty-two Satyry and the Bajki i przypowieści (both 1779), and the posthumous Bajki nowe (1802). The satires hold up the follies of the gentry (fashionable wives, drunkenness, gambling, court flattery) to dry, conversational ridicule in rhymed couplets, and the short fables, often only four to eight lines, end in a barbed moral. He also wrote the first Polish novel, and the 1774 Hymn do miłości ojczyzny, set to music, became an unofficial patriotic song.
