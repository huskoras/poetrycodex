Ishikawa Takuboku

1886–1912

石川啄木 (Ishikawa Takuboku, 1886–1912) was born in Iwate Prefecture in northern Japan and died of tuberculosis in Tokyo at the age of twenty-six. He wrote in the Meiji period and is remembered above all for his tanka, which he set down in three short lines instead of the single line of the older convention, in plain modern speech and in the voice of ordinary, poor and homesick city life.

Here are the two books of tanka he left. 一握の砂 (A Handful of Sand), published in December 1910, gathers 551 poems written from the autumn of 1908 on, in five sections; its dedication also remembers his infant son Shinichi, who died soon after birth. 悲しき玩具 (A Sad Toy), the collection of his last poems, appeared after his death in 1912. The text is that of Aozora Bunko, with the reading aids (ruby) removed.
