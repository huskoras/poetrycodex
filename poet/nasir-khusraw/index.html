Arabic & Persian

Nasir Khusraw

1004–c. 1088

Nasir Khusraw (ناصرخسرو قبادیانی, Nasir-i Khusraw Qubadiyani, 1004–c. 1088) was a Persian poet, traveller and Ismaili missionary, born at Qubadiyan near Balkh. He served as a secretary and revenue official in Khurasan until, in 1045, he set out on the pilgrimage that became seven years of travel through Syria, Arabia and Egypt, recorded in his prose Safarnama. In Cairo he was initiated into the Ismaili faith under the Fatimid caliph al-Mustansir and was sent home as its hujjat, or “proof”, in Khurasan.

Hounded there as a heretic, he fled and spent his last years in exile at Yumgan in the mountains of Badakhshan, where he wrote most of his Divan: religious and philosophical qasidas on reason, the inward sense of scripture, the vanity of the world and the wrongs of his exile. This selection takes the ten poems of the Divan that E. G. Browne quoted or translated in A Literary History of Persia, vol. II (1906), each given whole as Ganjoor prints it, with the page of Browne's book in the note. Each bayt stands on one line, the two hemistichs separated by a wide space. The Persian text is public domain.

  7. حاجیان آمدند با تعظیم (Qasida 147)
