Galician-Portuguese Cancioneiros

12th–14th century

The lyric of the Galician-Portuguese troubadours and jograis, composed from the late twelfth to the fourteenth century, survives chiefly in three great songbooks, the Cancioneiro da Ajuda, the Cancioneiro da Vaticana and the Colocci-Brancuti collection. Its commonest forms are the cantiga de amigo, a girl's song of longing for her absent lover, the cantiga de amor, in which the knight addresses his lady, and the cantiga de escarnho, the satirical song, with sea-shore barcarolas, dawn songs and dancing songs among them.

This file gathers the poets who are represented by only a few pieces in Aubrey F. G. Bell's Oxford Book of Portuguese Verse (1925): King Sancho I, Joan Zorro, Pero Meogo, Juian Bolseiro and others, taken in the order of the anthology. The note on each poem names its poet as Bell names him, with Bell's date, and the genre he gives it. Larger bodies of medieval song, such as those of King Dinis, Alfonso X and Martin Codax, have entries of their own.
