Hassan ibn Thabit

c. 563 – c. 674

Hassan ibn Thabit (حسان بن ثابت, c. 563 – c. 674) belonged to the Khazraj of Medina (Yathrib) and was already a court poet of the Ghassanid kings of Syria and the Lakhmids of al-Hira before the Prophet Muhammad came to the city. He accepted Islam and became known as the Poet of the Prophet (shaʻir al-Rasul), answering the satires of the Quraysh in verse. Traditional sources give him an exceptionally long life, of about a hundred and twenty years.

The selection here is from the Arabic Wikisource transcription of his diwan and gives the longer pieces first among the poems of three or more verses: first the elegies on the Prophet, among them the ode that begins "Bi-Taybata rasmun lil-Rasul wa-maʻhadu", then the longest pieces of the diwan, which include battle poems, polemics against the Quraysh and elegies for the fallen. The texts keep the vowel marks of that source.
