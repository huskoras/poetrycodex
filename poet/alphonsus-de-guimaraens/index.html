Alphonsus de Guimaraens

1870–1921

Alphonsus de Guimaraens (pseudonym of Afonso Henriques da Costa Guimarães, 1870–1921) was a Brazilian Symbolist poet. Born in Ouro Preto, he trained as a lawyer, became a judge, and spent the last decades of his life in Mariana, Minas Gerais, a quiet colonial town far from the literary centres of Rio de Janeiro and São Paulo.

His poetry is shaped by the early death of his beloved, a cousin who died shortly before their planned marriage; the dead woman, the church, the bell and the tower haunt his mystical, liturgical verse. Pastoral aos crentes do amor e da morte appeared posthumously in 1923, and its “Ysmalia”, the tale of a girl driven mad by the moon, is among the best-known Brazilian poems of the period.
