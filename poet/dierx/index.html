Léon Dierx

1838–1912

Léon Dierx (1838–1912) was a French Parnassian poet, born at Saint-Denis on the island of Réunion, a fellow islander of Leconte de Lisle, whom he admired. He settled in Paris, worked as a clerk in the Ministry of Public Instruction, and published Poèmes et Poésies (1864) and Les Lèvres closes (1867), followed by Les Paroles du vaincu (1871) and Les Amants (1879).

His verse is grave, impersonal and often bleak, with long narrative and visionary poems of death, memory and the silence of the gods. He was admired by Verlaine and Mallarmé, and on Mallarmé's death in 1898 he was elected "Prince des poètes"; he died in Paris in 1912. The texts here follow the Lemerre Œuvres complètes (vol. I) as transcribed by French Wikisource (CC BY-SA); the transcription of Poèmes et Poésies is partly unproofread.
