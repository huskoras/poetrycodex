German

Kurt Tucholsky

1890–1935

Kurt Tucholsky (Berlin, 9 January 1890 – Göteborg, 21 December 1935) was a German journalist, satirist and poet, one of the sharpest voices of the Weimar Republic. He wrote above all for Siegfried Jacobsohn's weekly Die Weltbühne (formerly Die Schaubühne), which he briefly edited after Jacobsohn's death in 1926, and he published under several pen names: Theobald Tiger for verse, Peter Panter for criticism and essays, Ignaz Wrobel for political polemic, and Kaspar Hauser.

His poems, the work of Theobald Tiger, are topical rhymes and cabaret songs that turn the ordinary speech of Berlin on militarism, nationalism, a partisan judiciary, unemployment and the small disappointments of love and marriage. They were gathered in books such as Fromme Gesänge (1919), Das Lächeln der Mona Lisa (1929) and Lerne lachen ohne zu weinen (1931).

Tucholsky lived in Paris from 1924 and in Sweden from 1930. His books were burned in Germany in May 1933 and he was deprived of German citizenship in 1934. He died in Göteborg in December 1935. The poems here are taken from the Weltbühne and the Arbeiter Illustrierte Zeitung, in the first printings of 1926 to 1930, from the transcriptions on German Wikisource.
