Tomás de Iriarte

1750–1791

Tomás de Iriarte (Puerto de la Cruz, Tenerife, 1750 – Madrid, 1791) was a Spanish Enlightenment poet, translator and playwright, born in the Canary Islands and brought up in Madrid under the eye of his uncle Juan de Iriarte, the royal librarian. He worked as a translator in the State Secretariat and as an archivist, and wrote the comedies El señorito mimado and La señorita malcriada, as well as La música, a didactic poem in five cantos.

His best-known work is the Fábulas literarias (Madrid, 1782): sixty-seven original fables in verse of many different metres, in which animals and things discuss authors, critics, translators and the follies of literary life. They were widely reprinted and translated, and they set him in a public quarrel with Samaniego, the other great Spanish fabulist of the age.
