French

Jean-Pierre Claris de Florian

1755–1794

Jean-Pierre Claris de Florian (1755–1794) was a French writer born at the Château de Florian in the Cévennes. He served as a page to the Duc de Penthièvre, a grandson of Louis XIV, and later as an officer in his household, and he wrote pastoral novels, comedies and verse. He is remembered above all for his fables, which were collected in five books and became a staple of French schooling.

His romance “Plaisir d’amour”, first printed in his novel Célestine, was set to music by Jean-Paul-Égide Martini and remains one of the best-known French songs. Florian was imprisoned during the Terror, was released after the fall of Robespierre, and died in 1794, aged thirty-eight. The fables in this selection follow the 1838 illustrated edition (Delloye); three of them also appear in The Oxford Book of French Verse (1907).
