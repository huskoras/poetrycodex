Matthew Prior

1664–1721

Matthew Prior (1664–1721) was the son of a joiner, educated at Westminster School and Cambridge, and made his career as a diplomat. He served as secretary to the embassy at The Hague and in Paris, and took part in the negotiations that led to the Treaty of Utrecht, and was imprisoned for a time after the Whig ministry came to power in 1715.

As a poet he was admired for his light, polished verse: songs and epigrams addressed to Cloe, tales and fables, and longer poems such as Alma and Solomon. His Poems on Several Occasions was published by subscription in 1718.
