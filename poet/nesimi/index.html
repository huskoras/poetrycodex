Turkish & Ottoman

Seyyid Nesîmî

c. 1369–1417

Seyyid İmâdeddin Nesîmî was a Hurufi mystic who wrote in Azerbaijani Turkish, as well as in Persian and Arabic. He was a disciple of Fazlullah of Astarabad, the founder of the Hurufi sect, which found God's self-revelation in the letters of the alphabet and in the human face.

He was executed at Aleppo, traditionally by flaying, around 1417, after the local scholars judged his verses blasphemous. His Turkish divan, with its ghazels and quatrains, makes him one of the founders of Turkish divan lyric. The poems here are E. J. W. Gibb's English renderings of his verse (1900).

Poems (14)

  1. Mesnevi: Stirred to its depths is the Circling Ocean
  2. Ghazel: Yea, every dulcet speech o' thine is e'en a pearl of lustrous ray
  3. Ghazel: Lo, thy beauty is the feast-tide, yea, the soul is victim there
  4. Ghazel: Thou whose Face is 'Aid from God!' O thou whose Hair is 'Victory near!'
  5. Ghazel: Since from yonder lunar Visage now the veil is cast aside
  6. Ghazel: 'I am The Truth!' I ceaseless cry, for Mansur-like Truth-helped I be!
  7. Quatrain: Thou for love of whose fair Face doth reel the Sphere
  8. Quatrain: Thou who lookest everywhere The Truth to see
  9. Quatrain: From The Truth I'm come; 'I am The Truth!' I cry
  10. Quatrain: Plunged have I amid the Sea that shore hath none
  11. Quatrain: Tired and weary of the wordly folk my heart
  12. Quatrain: Come and plunge thee deep beneath Love's ocean-tide
  13. Quatrain: Verily, The Truth in every thing I see
  14. Quatrain: God Most High as very son of man is seen
