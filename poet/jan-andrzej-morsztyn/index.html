Jan Andrzej Morsztyn

1621–1693

Jan Andrzej Morsztyn (1621–1693) was a Polish courtier, statesman and the leading poet of the Polish Baroque. He rose through royal service to the offices of referendary of the Crown and, from 1668, Grand Treasurer of the Crown, and his verse, much of it written in the 1640s and 1650s, circulated widely in manuscript among the nobility. After political quarrels that ended in a charge of treason he left Poland in 1683 for France, where he died in 1693.

His lyrics are the finest Polish examples of the conceited, witty manner of Marino and the Italian Baroque: sonnets, madrigals and epigrams on love, absence, jealousy and death, built on bold paradox and sharply turned endings. The poems here come from the first collected edition, Poezye oryginalne i tłomaczone (Warsaw: S. Lewental, 1883): the summer cycle Kanikuła albo psia gwiazda (1647) whole, and a selection from the two books of the Lutnia (The Lute, 1661), including its celebrated sonnets. His translations of Tasso, Corneille and others are not included.
