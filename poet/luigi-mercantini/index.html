Italian

Luigi Mercantini

1821–1872

Luigi Mercantini (1821–1872) was born at Ripatransone in the Marche and became one of the best-known patriotic poets of the Italian Risorgimento. He was also a teacher and student of literature, and his verse was written to be read aloud and sung: plain, ballad-like and aimed at a wide audience rather than a learned one.

His best-known poem, La spigolatrice di Sapri (1857), is spoken by a gleaner who watches the three hundred followers of Carlo Pisacane land and die in the failed Sapri expedition. His Inno di Garibaldi (1858), set to music by Alessio Olivieri, became the marching hymn of Garibaldi's volunteers. He died at Palermo in 1872. The five poems here are those printed in the 1861 edition of Giovanni Berchet's Poesie, where they were gathered as Italian patriotic verse.

Poems (5)

  1. La spigolatrice di Sapri
  2. Inno di Garibaldi
  3. Alla memoria di Daniele Manin
  4. Una madre veneziana al campo di San Martino
  5. Il buon capo d'anno del pellegrino italiano
