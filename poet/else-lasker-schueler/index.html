Else Lasker-Schüler

1869–1945

Else Lasker-Schüler (1869–1945) was born in Elberfeld, in the Rhineland, and wrote in German. She moved to Berlin around 1894 and became a central figure of the city's bohemian and Expressionist circles, publishing her first book of poems, Styx, in 1902 and presenting herself in her writing and drawings under invented personae such as Prince Yussuf of Thebes.

Her poetry, with its biblical and oriental imagery, its rapid shifts of mood and its intimate address to a beloved and to God, was gathered in several volumes, among them Der siebente Tag (1905), Meine Wunder (1911) and Hebräische Balladen (1913). Driven out of Germany by the Nazis in 1933, she went first to Zurich and then, in 1939, to Jerusalem, where she died in 1945.
