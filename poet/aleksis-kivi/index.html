Aleksis Kivi

1834–1872

Aleksis Kivi (born Alexis Stenvall, 1834–1872) was the son of a tailor in Nurmijärvi, in southern Finland, and is regarded as the national writer of Finland. He studied at the University of Helsinki without taking a degree and lived a poor and often precarious life as a writer. His verse play Kullervo (1864) won a state prize, and his novel Seitsemän veljestä (Seven Brothers, 1870) was the first significant novel in the Finnish language.

His poems, first printed in periodicals and in the Kanervala cycle of 1866, use a spoken, sometimes clipped Finnish and a free range of metres at a time when the written language was still being shaped. They were gathered after his death; this selection follows the Runot (1916) edition, with the cradle-song from Seven Brothers added. Kivi fell seriously ill in his last years and died at Tuusula at the age of thirty-eight.
