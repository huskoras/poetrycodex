Amir Mu'izzi

c. 1048–c. 1125

Amir Mu'izzi (امیر معزی, Muhammad ibn Abd al-Malik, c. 1048–c. 1125), of Nishapur, was the son of the court poet Burhani and followed him as amir al-shu'ara, poet-laureate, of the Seljuk sultan Malik-Shah. After Malik-Shah's death he went on in the service of Sanjar, the last great Seljuk ruler, and he is counted among the foremost Persian panegyrists of the age.

His Divan is made chiefly of qasidas in praise of his patrons, many of them opening with a love-prelude, and it also holds ghazals and quatrains. The Persian texts here are the first forty-nine qasidas of the Ganjoor edition together with the qasida beginning "O thou whose cheeks are the Pleiades" (no. 373), which R. A. Nicholson translated in 1922; his English version and a second short piece follow.
