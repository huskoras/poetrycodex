Mark Akenside

1721–1770

Mark Akenside (1721–1770) was born in Newcastle upon Tyne, the son of a butcher, and studied at Edinburgh and Leiden before practising as a physician in London. He was lame from a boyhood accident.

His blank-verse The Pleasures of Imagination (1744), a philosophical poem on the sources of aesthetic pleasure, made him famous when he was in his early twenties, and he later reworked it at length. His Odes and Hymn to the Naiads share its classical models and its enthusiasm for liberty.
