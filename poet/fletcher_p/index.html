Metaphysical & Cavalier

Phineas Fletcher

1582–1650

Phineas Fletcher (1582–1650), the elder son of Giles Fletcher the elder and brother of the poet Giles Fletcher the younger, was educated at Eton and King's College, Cambridge. He took holy orders, and from 1621 until his death he was rector of Hilgay in Norfolk. He is generally regarded as the leading Spenserian poet of his generation.

His chief work, The Purple Island, or The Isle of Man, an allegorical poem in twelve cantos on the human body and soul, was printed at Cambridge in 1633 together with Piscatorie Eclogs and Poeticall Miscellanies. Those two shorter collections are given here: seven eclogues in which fishermen take the place of shepherds, and a miscellany of occasional verses, epigrams, translations from Boethius, psalm paraphrases and hymns. The Purple Island itself is given in full as a work.
