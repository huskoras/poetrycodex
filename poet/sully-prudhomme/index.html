Sully Prudhomme

1839–1907

René François Armand Prudhomme, known as Sully Prudhomme, was born in Paris in 1839 and died at Châtenay in 1907. Trained first as an engineer and then in the law, he turned to poetry in his twenties and became one of the leading poets of the Parnassian school.

His first book, Stances et Poèmes (1865), contains the much-anthologised Le Vase brisé, and was followed by Les Épreuves, Les Solitudes and Les Vaines Tendresses. In 1901 he became the first winner of the Nobel Prize in Literature.
