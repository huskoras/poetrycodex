Ancient Greek & Roman

Parmenides

fl. c. 480 BC

Parmenides of Elea, a Greek colony on the southern coast of Italy, was an early philosopher who flourished around 480 BC. He set out his thought in a single poem in hexameter verse, traditionally known as On Nature (Περὶ φύσεως), which survives only in quotations preserved by later writers, above all Simplicius.

The poem opens with a proem in which a young man is carried in a chariot by the daughters of the Sun to the house of a goddess, who teaches him two ways: the Way of Truth, which argues that what is cannot have come into being or pass away, and the Way of Opinion, a cosmology of light and night offered as the beliefs of mortals. The Greek is given here as printed in Diels, Die Fragmente der Vorsokratiker (Berlin: Weidmann, 1903), with that edition's numbering of the fragments; its Latin fragment 18, the German translation, apparatus, testimonia and the doubtful and spurious fragments are left out, and the editor's brackets are removed.
