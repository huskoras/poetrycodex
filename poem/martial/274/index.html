Book XII, Epigram 17: In Lentinum

by Martial c. 40–c. 104

Translated by R. Fletcher (1656) · From Ex Otio Negotium (1656)

Social Commentaries

Lentinus why dost thou complain and groan
That all this while thine Ague is not gone?
Hee's carried in a Chair, and bath'd with thee,
Eates Mushrooms, Oysters, Sow's paps, and Brawn free,
Oft fox'd with Setine, oft with Falerne Wine,
Nor Caecube drinks without its Snow to joyn,
Lyes compass'd in with Rose-buds, black with sweets,
In a rich purple Bed, soft Down, fine Sheets.
When he doth live so well so brave with thee,
Wouldst have thy Ague to poor Dama flie?

Source Martial, Ex otio negotium. Or, Martiall his epigrams translated. With sundry poems and fancies, trans. R. Fletcher (London: Printed by T. Mabb, for William Shears, at the Bible in Bedford Street, Covent Garden, 1656). Digital text: EEBO-TCP A89611.
