Love

Thy Ruby Lips Unto the Sugar-Bale Have Wroughten Bale

by Qadi Burhan al-Din 1345–1398

Translated by E. J. W. Gibb (1900) · From A History of Ottoman Poetry, vol. I (1900)

Love

Thy ruby lips unto the sugar-bale have wroughten bale,
And made this parrot-heart of mine in melody to wail.

I flung my heart, ah, woe is me, upon her heart's pathway;
To save the vial cast against the stone, whate'er may vail?

She bent her eyebrow-bow and notched the arrow of her eye;
It seemeth then she would yon Turks unto this battle hail.

Her tresses and her locks do burn my soul like aloes-wood,
For this her rule with whatsoe'er she may to clutch avail.

O skinker, give into her hand the brimming bowl and see
The charm and seemliness she adds unto the wine vermeil.
