The Pedigree of the Owl

by Dafydd ap Gwilym c. 1315/20–c. 1350/70

Translated by Arthur James Johnes (1834) · From Translations into English Verse from the Poems of Davyth ap Gwilym (1834)

Mythology & Folklore

Creature of the world of gloom !
Owlet with the dusky plume !
With a song-like kitten's cries.
Less than bunch of down in size ;
Countess, delicate and slight,
Of the mountain rampart white !
May the shaft thy forehead tear ;
Huntress fierce in glen remote.
Face of frown and grief of note !

OWL.
Spare ! Oh, youth, for mercy spare !
Leave to me the woodlands — where
I am destined by my fate.
To endure the agony
Of sad penance — and the hate
Of all birds beneath the sky !
I must suffer in the night
Cold and mourning — in the light
Fear and wandering and flight !

POET.
Bird of wondrous sorrows, thou
With the countenance of age.
Wilt thou to the bard avow
What thy name and lineage ?

OWL.
By the men of noble race,
I am called " Unrivalled Face."
At the banquetings of yore,
I the name " Flower Aspect" bore ;
I was daughter of a chief.
Proudly through the land of Mon,
As the son of Meirchion known,
Rich in golden stores —

BARD.
Oh, grief!
Maiden, who art called " the Morn ;"
Who then wrought this fearful change ?

OWL.
Gwdion, son of Don, in scorn,
With his wand of magic sway,
Changed my beauty's proud array.
For the aspect you behold !
In revenge, because of old
Gronwy, Pevyr, Garanhir,
Of tall form and noble cheer,
Penllyn's lord — to me was dear.
