Love

That night in which our comrade is yon sugar-liplet bright

by Ahmed Pasha d. 1496

Translated by E. J. W. Gibb (1902) · From A History of Ottoman Poetry, vol. II (1902)

Love

That night in which our comrade is yon sugar-liplet bright, --
Grant thou the dawn no power to draw one breath, O Lord of Night.

Thy beauty's dawn is e'en a brilliancy-diffusing day
Enclosed on either side between thy darkling tresses' night.

O houri fairy-visaged, it is thou, an so there be
Embodied radiancy the which in soul for frame is dight.

O heart, behold her cheek and curl, nor take thy leave of us,
Nor start on journey, for the moon in Scorpio's alight.

The reason why I kiss the dust afore thy gate is this,
That gilded plate beside the door is surely meet and right.

O tear, go hide so soon as thou the loved one's face dost see;
'Tis seemly that the stars be lost when comes the sun in sight.

A kiss of yonder lip of thine sick-hearted Ahmed craves;
Is 't strange if fever make a man unfitting words recite?
