Living

Man, be ware and wise in dede

by Anonymous (Middle English) c. 13th–16th century

From Early English Lyrics (1907)

Living

Man, be ware and wise in dede ;
Asay thy frend or thou hast nede,

Under a forest that was so long
As I me rode with mekell drede,
I hard a bird singing a song,
‘Asay thy frend or thou hast nede.’

I there stode and hovéd still ;
To a tree I teid my stede.
Ever the bird sang full shill,
‘Asay thy frend or thou hast nede.’

Me thought it was a wonder noise ;
Alwey nere and nere I yede ;
And ever she song with loud vois,
‘Asay thy frend or thou hast nede.’

I beheld that bird full long ;
She bad me do as I thee rede,
Whether that thou do right or wrong,
Asay thy frend or thou hast nede.

The birdé sat upon a tree ;
With fethers gray than was her wede ;
She seid ‘And thou wilt do after me,
Asay thy frend or thou have nede.’

Of me I trow she was agast ;
She toke her flight in length and brede ;
And thus she sang whan she showed last,
‘Asay thy frend or thou have nede,’

Away full fast she gan her highe.
God graunt us well our lives to, lede !
For thus she sang, whan she gan flie,
‘Asay thy frend or thou have nede,’
