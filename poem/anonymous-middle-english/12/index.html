Maiden in the Mor Lay

by Anonymous (Middle English)

From Bodleian MS Rawlinson D. 913

Mythology & FolkloreNature

Maiden in the mor lay,
In the mor lay,
Seuenyst fulle, seuenist fulle,
Maiden in the mor lay,
In the mor lay,
Seuenistes fulle ant a day.

Welle was hire mete;
Wat was hire mete?
Þe primerole ant the,—
Þe primerole ant the,—
Welle was hire mete;
Wat was hire mete?—
The primerole ant the violet.

Welle was hire dryng;
Wat was hire dryng?
Þe chelde water of þe welle-spring.

Welle was hire bour;
Wat was hire bour?
Þe rede rose an te lilie flour.

✦ ✦ ✦

Codex Note

An incantatory song of a maiden who lay in the moor for seven nights, and what she ate and drank — primrose and violet, the cold water of the well-spring — written, like ‘Icham of Irlaunde’, on a flyleaf of a Bodleian manuscript. Text from Kenneth Sisam, Fourteenth Century Verse & Prose (Oxford, 1921).
