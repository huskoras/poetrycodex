Social Commentaries

I may seyn, and so moun mo

by Anonymous (Middle English) c. 13th–16th century

From Early English Lyrics (1907)

Social Commentaries

I may seyn, and so moun mo,
That in semenaunt goth gile.

Semenaunt is a wonder thing ;
It begileth bothe knight and king,
And maketh maidenes of love-longing.
I warne you of that gile.

Semenaunt is a sly peintour,
It florcheth and fadeth in many a flour,
And maketh wommen to lesen here bright colour
Upon a litel while.

In semenaunt be thingés three ;
Thought, speche, and previté,
And trewthé schuld the forté be ;
It is hens a thousand mile.

Trewthe is fer and semeth hende ;
Good and wikkit it hath in minde ;
It fareth as a candele ende
That brenneth fro half a mile.

Many man faire to me he speketh,
And he wiste him well bewreke,
He had well levere mine hed to-breke
Than help me over a stile.

God that deyed upon the cross—
Ferst he deyed, sithen he ros—
Have mercy and pité on us.
We leven here but a while.
