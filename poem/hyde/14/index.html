Teig and Mary (From the Irish)

by Douglas Hyde 1860–1949

Translated by Douglas Hyde (1893) · From Love Songs of Connacht (1893)

Love

Teig: Bright was the air, the hills were fair,
When first I saw thee, Mary.
Maurya: Not brighter they than thou, the day
Thou tookest Teig the “bairy.”

Teig: Thy eyes are bright as stars of night,
Each one God’s candle-bearer.
Maurya: There is no star of all that are,
But thou by far art fairer.

Teig: The setting sun shows black and dun,
And cold, beside thee, Mary.
Maurya: There is no sun of all that run
To which I could compare thee.

Teig: The fairy host might make their boast
Of thy sweet features, Mary.
Maurya: More fair they are than I, by far,
But thou more fair than fairy.

Teig: Top-knots of love all else above,
Lurk in thy tresses, Mary.
Maurya: Thou hast a smile which must beguile,
So gay it is, so airy.

Teig: Thy bright eyes spin a net so thin,
Thou took’st me in it, Mary.
Maurya: A love-spot thou hast on thy brow,
Of charms it is not chary.

Teig: Thy slave I’ll be; thou sees’t in me
Thy thrall and lover, Mary.
Maurya: No longer free, I yield to thee,
All shamefaced, all unwary.
