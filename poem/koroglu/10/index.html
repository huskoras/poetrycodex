How Beautiful Thy Head Looks

by Köroğlu 17th century (traditional)

Translated by Alexander Chodzko (1842) · From Specimens of the Popular Poetry of Persia (1842)

RelationshipsLove

How beautiful thy head looks with this plume; it is like the male crane's head. I shall keep it; I shall watch over it carefully. I sought thee in heaven, and I have found thee on earth; do not weep, my young crane. The arched line of thy eyebrows has been sketched by the pen of the Almighty. Thou art just come of age, thou art fifteen, O my youth. To all these ornaments one only is wanting, and it is that of chivalrous achievements. Thou shalt be the model of a Warrior. I shall cover thy head with a calotte of gold. O my young crane, weep no more.

This translation is printed as prose in its source edition.

✦ ✦ ✦

Codex Note

Kurroglou's song to a young man he has taken into his care, praising him and promising to make him a warrior.
