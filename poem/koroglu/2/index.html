I Am Called God in My House

by Köroğlu 17th century (traditional)

Translated by Alexander Chodzko (1842) · From Specimens of the Popular Poetry of Persia (1842)

History & PoliticsThe Mind

I am called God in my house. Yes; I am a God. I shall not bend my neck before a coward like thee. The pitcher has carried water long enough for thee; but that pitcher is broken at last.

✦ ✦ ✦

Codex Note

Roushan's answer to the prince's threats, in which he boasts of his own strength and refuses to bow to a coward.
