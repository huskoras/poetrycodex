Love

Ode LXIX

by Anacreon c. 582 – c. 485 BC

Translated by Thomas Moore (1800) · From Odes of Anacreon (1800)

Love

They wove the lotus band to deck
And fan with pensile wreath each neck;
And every guest, to shade his head,
Three little fragrant chaplets spread;
And one was of the Egyptian leaf,
The rest were roses, fair and brief:
While from a golden vase profound,
To all on flowery beds around,
A Hebe, of celestial shape,
Poured the rich droppings of the grape!

Source Anacreon, The Poetical Works of Thomas Moore, Collected by Himself, with Explanatory Notes, and a…, trans. Thomas Moore (London). Digital text: Project Gutenberg 8187. (details from catalogue records, not yet checked against the title page)
