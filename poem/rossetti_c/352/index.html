Love

Confluents

by Christina Rossetti 1830–1894

From Women Poets of the Victorian Era (ed. Elizabeth A. Sharp, 1890)

Love

As livers seek the sea,
Much more deep than they,
So my soul seeks thee,
Far away :
As running rivers moan
On their course alone,
So I moan,
Left alone.

As the delicate rose
To the sun's sweet strength
Doth herself unclose,
Breadth and length :
So spreads my heart to thee,
Unveiled utterly,
I to thee
Utterly.

As morning dew exhales
Sunwards pure and free,
So my spirit fails
After thee:
As dew leaves not a trace
On the green earth's face ;
I, no trace
On thy face.

Its goal the river knows,
Dewdrops find a way,
Sunlight cheers the rose
In her day :
Shall I, lone sorrow past,
Find thee at last ?
Sorrow past,
Thee at last?

Source Elizabeth A. Sharp, ed., Women Poets of the Victorian Era, edited, with an introduction and notes, by Mrs. William Sharp (Canterbury Poets) (London (and New York): Walter Scott, Limited, 1890). Digital text: Internet Archive womenpoetsofvict00sharrich.
