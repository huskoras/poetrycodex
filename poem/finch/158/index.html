Nature

The Bird

by Anne Finch, Countess of Winchilsea 1661–1720

From The Poems of Anne Countess of Winchilsea, ed. Myra Reynolds (1903)

Nature

Kind bird, thy praises I design,
Thy praises, like thy plumes, shou'd shine;
Thy praises, shou'd thy life outlive,
Cou'd I the fame I wish thee, give.
Thou, my domestick musick art,
And dearest trifle of my heart.
Soft, in thy notes, and in thy dresse,
Softer, then numbers can expresse,
Softer then love, softer then light
When just escaping from the night,
When first she rises, unaray'd,
And steals a passage through the shade.
Softer then air, or flying clouds
Which Phoebus glory, thinly shrouds,
Gay as the spring, gay as the flowers,
When lightly strew'd with pearly showers,
Ne'r to the woods shalt thou return,
Nor thy wild freedom, shalt thou mourn,
Thou, to my bosome shalt repair,
And find a safer shelter there;
There shalt thou watch, and shou'd I sleep,
My heart, thy charge, securely keep.
Love, who a stranger is to me,
Must by thy wings be kin to thee;
So painted o're, so seeming fair,
So soft, his first adresses are,
Thy guard, he n'er can passe, unseen;
Thou, surely thou hast often been,
Whilst yett a wand'rer in the grove,
A false accomplice, with this Love ;
In the same shade, hast thou not sate,
And seen him work some wretches fate?
Hast thou not sooth'd him in the wrong;
And grac'd the mischeif, with a song,
Tuning thy loud conspiring voyce,
O're falling lovers, to rejoyce ?
If so, thy wicked faults redeem,
In league with me, no truce with him
Do thou admitt, but warn my heart,
And all his sly designs impart,
Least to that breast, by craft he gett,
Which has defy'd and brav'd him yett.
