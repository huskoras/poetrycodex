Arts & Sciences

To Mr. Jervas

by Anne Finch, Countess of Winchilsea 1661–1720

From The Poems of Anne Countess of Winchilsea, ed. Myra Reynolds (1903)

Arts & Sciences

This matchless Picture, Jervas, hide,
Or let it stand alone;
When one does over all preside,
The rest are vainly shown.

The meanest Figures of the Sky,
(Though drawn with handsome Faces,)
Are, when their Goddesses are by,
Th' attending Nymphs and Graces.

For sure, (as Cæsar chose Renown)
'Tis better to be reckon'd,
The Dulcinea of some Town,
Than in a Court, the Second.

Then, let this new Campaspe go,
Or, if thou'lt not resign,
As thou Apelles' Skill doest know,
So, may his Heart be thine.

To praise more equal leave our Choice,
When we thy Works survey,
Nor let each sighing Breast and Voice,
But one Applause betray.
