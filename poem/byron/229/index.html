Song for the Luddites

by Lord Byron 1788–1824

From Epigrams and Jeux d'Esprit

History & Politics

As the Liberty lads o'er the sea
Bought their freedom, and cheaply, with blood,
So we, boys, we
Will die fighting, or live free,
And down with all kings but King Ludd!

When the web that we weave is complete,
And the shuttle exchanged for the sword,
We will fling the winding sheet
O'er the despot at our feet,
And dye it deep in the gore he has poured.

Though black as his heart its hue,
Since his veins are corrupted to mud,
Yet this is the dew
Which the tree shall renew
Of Liberty, planted by Ludd!

Source Lord Byron, ed. Ernest Hartley Coleridge, The Works of Lord Byron: A New, Revised and Enlarged Edition, with Illustrations. Poetry, Poetry, vol. VII (London and New York: John Murray; Charles Scribner's Sons, 1904). Digital text: Project Gutenberg 27577.
