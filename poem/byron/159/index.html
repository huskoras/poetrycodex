Love

Farewell! If Ever Fondest Prayer

by Lord Byron 1788–1824

From Poems 1814–1816

Love

Farewell! if ever fondest prayer
For other's weal availed on high,
Mine will not all be lost in air,
But waft thy name beyond the sky.
'Twere vain to speak--to weep--to sigh:
Oh! more than tears of blood can tell,
When wrung from Guilt's expiring eye,
Are in that word--Farewell!--Farewell!

These lips are mute, these eyes are dry;
But in my breast and in my brain,
Awake the pangs that pass not by,
The thought that ne'er shall sleep again.
My soul nor deigns nor dares complain,
Though Grief and Passion there rebel:
I only know we loved in vain--
I only feel--Farewell!--Farewell!

Source Lord Byron, ed. Ernest Hartley Coleridge, The Works of Lord Byron: A New, Revised and Enlarged Edition, with Illustrations. Poetry, Poetry, vol. III (London and New York: John Murray; Charles Scribner's Sons, 1900). Digital text: Project Gutenberg 21811.
