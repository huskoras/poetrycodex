Death & Dying

Ode Sung on the Occasion of Decorating the Graves of the Confederate Dead

by Henry Timrod 1828–1867

From Poems of Henry Timrod (1899)

Death & Dying

I

Sleep sweetly in your humble graves,
Sleep, martyrs of a fallen cause;
Though yet no marble column craves
The pilgrim here to pause.

II

In seeds of laurel in the earth
The blossom of your fame is blown,
And somewhere, waiting for its birth,
The shaft is in the stone!

III

Meanwhile, behalf the tardy years
Which keep in trust your storied tombs,
Behold! your sisters bring their tears,
And these memorial blooms.

IV

Small tributes! but your shades will smile
More proudly on these wreaths to-day,
Than when some cannon-moulded pile
Shall overlook this bay.

V

Stoop, angels, hither from the skies!
There is no holier spot of ground
Than where defeated valor lies,
By mourning beauty crowned!

Source Henry Timrod, Poems of Henry Timrod; with Memoir and Portrait (Memorial Edition), Memorial Edition (Boston and New York; Richmond: Houghton, Mifflin and Company; B. F. Johnson Publishing Company (Richmond issue), 1899). Digital text: Project Gutenberg 845. (details from catalogue records, not yet checked against the title page)
