Love

Cupid and Campaspe

by John Lyly 1554–1600

From The Golden Treasury, ed. F. T. Palgrave (1861)

Love

Cupid and my Campaspe play'd
At cards for kisses; Cupid paid:
He stakes his quiver, bow, and arrows,
His mother's doves, and team of sparrows;
Loses them too; then down he throws
The coral of his lip, the rose
Growing on's cheek (but none knows how);
With these, the crystal of his brow,
And then the dimple on his chin;
All these did my Campaspe win:
At last he set her both his eyes--
She won, and Cupid blind did rise.
O Love! has she done this to thee?
What shall, alas! become of me?

Source Francis Turner Palgrave, ed., The Golden Treasury of the Best Songs and Lyrical Pieces in the English Language (London and Glasgow: Collins' Clear-Type Press, n.d). Digital text: Project Gutenberg 19221.
