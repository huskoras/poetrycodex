Death & Dying

The Suicide's Argument

by Samuel Taylor Coleridge 1772–1834

From Poems of Coleridge, selected by Arthur Symons (1905)

Death & Dying

Ere the birth of my life, if I wish'd it or no,
No question was asked me--it could not be so!
If the life was the question, a thing sent to try,
And to live on be Yes; what can No be? to die.

NATURE'S ANSWER

Is't returned, as 'twas sent? Is't no worse for the wear?
Think first, what you are! Call to mind what you were!
I gave you innocence, I gave you hope,
Gave health, and genius, and an ample scope.
Return you me guilt, lethargy, despair?
Make out the invent'ry; inspect, compare!
Then die--if die you dare!

Source Samuel Taylor Coleridge, ed. Arthur Symons, Poems of Coleridge, Selected and Arranged with an Introduction and Notes by Arthur Symons (London: Methuen & Co, 1905). Digital text: Project Gutenberg 8208.
