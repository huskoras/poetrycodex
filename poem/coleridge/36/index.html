Fancy in Nubibus; or, The Poet in the Clouds

by Samuel Taylor Coleridge 1772–1834

From Poems of Coleridge, selected by Arthur Symons (1905)

Arts & Sciences

O! It is pleasant, with a heart at ease,
Just after sunset, or by moonlight skies,
To make the shifting clouds be what you please,
Or let the easily persuaded eyes
Own each quaint likeness issuing from the mould
Of a friend's fancy; or with head bent low
And cheek aslant see rivers flow of gold
'Twixt crimson banks; and then, a traveller, go
From mount to mount through Cloudland, gorgeous land!
Or list'ning to the tide, with closed sight,
Be that blind bard, who on the Chian strand
By those deep sounds possessed with inward light,
Beheld the Iliad and the Odyssee
Rise to the swelling of the voiceful sea.

Source Samuel Taylor Coleridge, ed. Arthur Symons, Poems of Coleridge, Selected and Arranged with an Introduction and Notes by Arthur Symons (London: Methuen & Co, 1905). Digital text: Project Gutenberg 8208.
