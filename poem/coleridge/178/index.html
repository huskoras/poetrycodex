Nature

Lines: With many a pause and oft reverted eye

by Samuel Taylor Coleridge 1772–1834

From Complete Poetical Works, ed. E. H. Coleridge (1912)

Nature

With many a pause and oft reverted eye
I climb the Coomb's ascent: sweet songsters near
Warble in shade their wild-wood melody:
Far off the unvarying Cuckoo soothes my ear.
Up scour the startling stragglers of the flock
That on green plots o'er precipices browze:
From the deep fissures of the naked rock
The Yew-tree bursts! Beneath its dark green boughs
(Mid which the May-thorn blends its blossoms white)
Where broad smooth stones jut out in mossy seats,
I rest:--and now have gain'd the topmost site.
Ah! what a luxury of landscape meets
My gaze! Proud towers, and Cots more dear to me,
Elm-shadow'd Fields, and prospect-bounding Sea!
Deep sighs my lonely heart: I drop the tear:
Enchanting spot! O were my Sara here!

Source Samuel Taylor Coleridge, The Complete Poetical Works of Samuel Taylor Coleridge, including poems and versions of…, Vol. I: Poems (with appendix poems from Vol. II), ed. Ernest Hartley Coleridge (Oxford: At the Clarendon Press, 1912). Digital text: Project Gutenberg 29091.

Codex Note
