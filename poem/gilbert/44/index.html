The Contemplative Sentry

by W. S. Gilbert 1836–1911

From Songs of a Savoyard (1920 Macmillan Bab Ballads edition)

Social Commentaries

When all night long a chap remains
On sentry-go, to chase monotony
He exercises of his brains,
That is, assuming that he’s got any.
Though never nurtured in the lap
Of luxury, yet I admonish you,
I am an intellectual chap,
And think of things that would astonish you.
I often think it’s comical
How Nature always does contrive
That every boy and every gal,
That’s born into the world alive,
Is either a little Liberal,
Or else a little Conservative!
Fal lal la!

When in that house M.P.’s divide,
If they’ve a brain and cerebellum, too,
They’ve got to leave that brain outside,
And vote just as their leaders tell ’em to.
But then the prospect of a lot
Of statesmen, all in close proximity,
A-thinking for themselves, is what
No man can face with equanimity.
Then let’s rejoice with loud Fal lal
That Nature wisely does contrive
That every boy and every gal,
That’s born into the world alive,
Is either a little Liberal,
Or else a little Conservative!
Fal lal la!

Source W. S. Gilbert, The Bab Ballads, with Which Are Included Songs of a Savoyard (London: Macmillan and Co., Limited, 1920). Digital text: Project Gutenberg 934. (details from catalogue records, not yet checked against the title page)
