Nature

Mad Song

by William Blake 1757–1827

From The Poets and the Poetry of the Century, ed. Alfred H. Miles

Nature

The wild winds weep,
And the night is a-cold;
Come hither, Sleep,
And my griefs infold:
But lo! the morning peeps
Over the eastern steeps,
And the rustling birds of dawn
The earth do scorn.

Lo! to the vault
Of paved heaven,
With sorrow fraught
My notes are driven:
They strike the ear of night,
Make weep the eyes of day;
They make mad the roaring winds,
And with tempests play.

Like a fiend in a cloud
With howling woe,
After night I do crowd,
And with night will go;
I turn my back to the east,
From whence comforts have increas'd \
For light doth seize my brain
With frantic pain.

Source Alfred H. Miles, ed., The Poets and the Poetry of the Century, Vols. I, II, IV, V, VI, VIII and X (the 222 poems are spread over these volumes), First edition, in ten volumes (1891-97); not the twelve-volume reissue of 1905-07 'The Poets and the Poetry of the Nineteenth Century' (London: Hutchinson & Co, 1891-1897).
