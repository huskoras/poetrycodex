The Immortals

by Isaac Rosenberg

From Poems (1922)

Social CommentariesHistory & Politics

I killed them, but they would not die.
Yea, all the day and all the night
For them I could not rest nor sleep,
Nor guard from them nor hide in flight!

Then in my agony I turned
And made my hands red in their gore.
In vain—for faster than I slew
They rose more cruel than before.

I killed and killed with slaughter mad;
I killed till all my strength was gone;
And still they rose to torture me,
For Devils only die for fun.

I used to think the Devil hid
In women’s smiles and wine’s carouse;
I called him Satan, Balzebub;
But now I call him dirty louse.

✦ ✦ ✦

Codex Note

The speaker has killed his enemies but cannot rest because they will not die. The ending reveals that the enemy, once called Satan and Beelzebub, is the louse.
