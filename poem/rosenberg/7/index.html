Nature

Returning, We Hear the Larks

by Isaac Rosenberg 1890–1918

From Poems (1922)

NatureHistory & Politics

Sombre the night is:
And, though we have our lives, we know
What sinister threat lurks there.

Dragging these anguished limbs, we only know
This poison-blasted track opens on our camp—
On a little safe sleep.

But hark! Joy—joy—strange joy.
Lo! Heights of night ringing with unseen larks:
Music showering on our upturned listening faces.

Death could drop from the dark
As easily as song—
But song only dropped,
Like a blind man’s dreams on the sand
By dangerous tides;
Like a girl’s dark hair, for she dreams no ruin lies there,
Or her kisses where a serpent hides.

✦ ✦ ✦

Codex Note

Coming back through the night, the soldiers hear larks, and the poem sets the sweet song against 'what sinister threat lurks there'. It ends with 'kisses where a serpent hides'.
