Death & Dying

The Dead (I)

by Rupert Brooke

From 1914 and Other Poems (1915)

Death & DyingHistory & Politics

Blow out, you bugles, over the rich Dead!
There's none of these so lonely and poor of old,
But, dying, has made us rarer gifts than gold.
These laid the world away; poured out the red
Sweet wine of youth; gave up the years to be
Of work and joy, and that unhoped serene,
That men call age; and those who would have been,
Their sons, they gave, their immortality.

Blow, bugles, blow! They brought us, for our dearth,
Holiness, lacked so long, and Love, and Pain.
Honour has come back, as a king, to earth,
And paid his subjects with a royal wage;
And Nobleness walks in our ways again;
And we have come into our heritage.

✦ ✦ ✦

Codex Note

'Blow out, you bugles, over the rich Dead!' The first of the two sonnets titled 'The Dead' honours those who gave up their years and their sons, and claims that Honour has come back 'as a king, to earth'.
