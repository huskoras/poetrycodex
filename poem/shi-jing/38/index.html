Relationships

The Ts‘ewen Shwuy

by Shi Jing (The Book of Songs) poems c. 1000–600 BC; compilation traditionally ascribed to Confucius

Translated by James Legge (1876) · From The She King (1876), Part I, Book III

Relationships

As the streamlet from its spring
Flows into the river K‘e,
So my daily thoughts on wing
Fly, my native Wei, to thee;
For I long with cousins there
Counsel sweet and love to share.

For a night, at Tse I stayed;
Drank the cup to Ne when come;
Parents, brothers, farewell bade :—
Such the fate on leaving home.
Parents are not now alive;
Aunts and sister still survive.

Lo! I hasten home again.
Let the rushing chariot-wheel
Pause at Kan, and part at Yen,
Pebbles flashing to its steel.
Does my heart go far astray,
Panting for its native Wei?

By the Fei-ts‘euen’s winding stream
Daily sighing thought will stray.
Seu and Ts‘aou in memory gleam,
Broken glints of childhood’s day.
Spring, my horses! Speed, my wheels!
Gone the grief my bosom feels !

Source Shi Jing, The She King; or, The Book of Ancient Poetry, translated in English verse, with essays and notes, trans. James Legge (London: Trübner & Co, 1876). Digital text: Internet Archive shekingorbookofa0000unse.

✦ ✦ ✦

Codex Note

A daughter of the House of Wei, married in another State, expresses her longing to revisit Wei.
