The Hing Loo

by Shi Jing (The Book of Songs) poems c. 1000–600 BC; compilation traditionally ascribed to Confucius

Translated by James Legge (1876) · From The She King (1876), Part I, Book II

Love

The dew thick on the wet paths lay ;
Thither at early dawn my way
I might have ta’en; but I said, “Nay.
“The dew is thick, at home I’ll stay.”

You say the sparrow has a horn.—
How could it else bore through your house ?
You say this trial is a proof
That I exchanged betrothal vows.
But though you’ve made me here appear in court,
Yet at betrothal what you did fell short.

You say the rat’s teeth are complete.—
How could it else bore through your wall ?
You say this trial proves my vows
Of plighted troth were perfect all,
But though to court you’ve forced me here to come,
My will is firm ;—I’ll not with you go home.

Source James Legge, trans., The She King; or, The Book of Ancient Poetry, Translated in English Verse, with Essays and Notes (London: Trubner & Co, 1876). Digital text: Internet Archive shekingorbookofa0000unse.

Codex Note

A lady resists an attempt to force her to marry, and argues her cause.
