The Héung Choh

by Shi Jing (The Book of Songs) poems c. 1000–600 BC; compilation traditionally ascribed to Confucius

Translated by James Legge (1876) · From The She King (1876), Part III, Book II

History & Politics

Bring water from the distant pool,
And let it settle in a jar.
Millet or rice ’twill steam—such use
May come from things that paltry are.
You are our happy, courteous king ;
On you a noble duty lies.
Father and mother, joined in one,
With all the people sympathize !

Bring water from the distant pool,
And let it settle in a jar.
A spirit-vase ’twill wash ;—such use
May come from things that paltry are.
You are our happy, courteous king,
Born subject to a noble law.
Like centre to which all should turn,
So you the people ever draw.

Bring water from the distant pool,
And let it settle in a jar.
All things ’twill serve to cleanse ;—such use
May come from things that paltry are.
You are our happy, courteous king,
Set to fulfil a noble end.
The centre in which all have rest,
The people’s hearts to you should tend.

Source James Legge, trans., The She King; or, The Book of Ancient Poetry, Translated in English Verse, with Essays and Notes (London: Trübner & Co, 1876). Digital text: Internet Archive shekingorbookofa0000unse.

✦ ✦ ✦

Codex Note

The most unlikely things may by human ingenuity be made useful; how much more should a sovereign fulfil the duties of his position!.
