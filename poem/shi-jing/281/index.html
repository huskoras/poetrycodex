Religion

The Min Yu Séaou Tsze

by Shi Jing (The Book of Songs) poems c. 1000–600 BC; compilation traditionally ascribed to Confucius

Translated by James Legge (1876) · From The She King (1876), Part IV, Book I

Religion

Alas for me, still but a child!
For all too soon I know
The cares of the unsettled State.
Too heavy will they grow.
I shrink in sorrow from the task ;
But, O my mighty sire,
To filial virtue, all thy life,
Thou ever didst aspire.

My great grandsire, though now enshrined,
To thee still living proved,
As if in court-yard and in hall
His royal person moved,
And I, the little child, will be
As reverent night and day.
To you, great kings, such homage I
Now on your throne will pay.

Source Shi Jing, The She King; or, The Book of Ancient Poetry, Translated in English Verse, with Essays and Notes, trans. James Legge (London: Trübner & Co, 1876). Digital text: Internet Archive shekingorbookofa0000unse.

✦ ✦ ✦

Codex Note

Appropriate to the young king Ching, declaring his sentiments in the temple of his father.
