The Lovely Lass O’ Inverness

by Robert Burns 1759–1796

From Poems and Songs, 1794

Love

The lovely lass o’ Inverness,
Nae joy nor pleasure can she see;
For, e’en to morn she cries, alas!
And aye the saut tear blin’s her e’e.

“Drumossie moor, Drumossie day—
A waefu’ day it was to me!
For there I lost my father dear,
My father dear, and brethren three.

“Their winding-sheet the bluidy clay,
Their graves are growin’ green to see;
And by them lies the dearest lad
That ever blest a woman’s e’e!

“Now wae to thee, thou cruel lord,
A bluidy man I trow thou be;
For mony a heart thou has made sair,
That ne’er did wrang to thine or thee!”

Source Robert Burns, The Poems and Songs of Robert Burns, with Introduction, Notes and Glossary (edited by Charles W, Vol. 6 (The Harvard Classics) (New York: P. F. Collier and Son, 1909). Digital text: Project Gutenberg 1279.
