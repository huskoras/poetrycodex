Young Jockie Was The Blythest Lad

by Robert Burns 1759–1796

From Poems and Songs, 1789

Living

Young Jockie was the blythest lad,
In a’ our town or here awa;
Fu’ blythe he whistled at the gaud,
Fu’ lightly danc’d he in the ha’.

He roos’d my een sae bonie blue,
He roos’d my waist sae genty sma’;
An’ aye my heart cam to my mou’,
When ne’er a body heard or saw.

My Jockie toils upon the plain,
Thro’ wind and weet, thro’ frost and snaw:
And o’er the lea I leuk fu’ fain,
When Jockie’s owsen hameward ca’.

An’ aye the night comes round again,
When in his arms he taks me a’;
An’ aye he vows he’ll be my ain,
As lang’s he has a breath to draw.

Source Robert Burns, The Poems and Songs of Robert Burns, with Introduction, Notes and Glossary (edited by Charles W, Vol. 6 (The Harvard Classics) (New York: P. F. Collier and Son, 1909). Digital text: Project Gutenberg 1279.
