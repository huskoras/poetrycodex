Love

There Is No Man on Earth but Dule Doth Dree

by Nejâtî d. 1508

Translated by E. J. W. Gibb (1902) · From A History of Ottoman Poetry, vol. II (1902)

Love

There is no man on earth but dule doth dree;
There whoso drees not dule, no man is he.

Until the heart rain down a flood of tears,
Love's meadow never smileth lovelily.

If winsome beauties' hearts were not of stone,
Love's temple would not strong of structure be.

The rosebud-lipped, the slender-waisted fair,
From buss and clip nor hurt nor evil see.

Each Abraham in Grace's Kacba-shrine
May not God's Intimate or Edhem be.

I cypress-wise was planted, straight I grew,
Nor from thy gate a single step may flee.

'I'll come and pass the even', said yon Moon;
My star it knows no even, woe is me!

Nejati, fain they'd have thee break thy vow,—
The flowers will ne'er keep silence on the lea.
