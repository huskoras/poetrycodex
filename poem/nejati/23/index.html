Love

Yonder Glance, That Showers the Arrow-Lashes on the Soul's Countrie

by Nejâtî d. 1508

Translated by E. J. W. Gibb (1902) · From A History of Ottoman Poetry, vol. II (1902)

Love

Yonder glance, that showers the arrow-lashes on the soul's countrie,
Like that Tartar seems who maketh rain to rain through gramarye.

Now 'tis tears and now 'tis blood mine eyen weep when thou art gone;
Yea, they furnish pearls and coral to bestrew the path of thee.

E'en so much and long have wept mine eyes that all the blood is gone;
Whence, I wonder, will the rushing current now replenished be?

From my sighing's smoke the folk of earth are of my weeping ware;
For they know that rain is present when the black wind bloweth free.

From the cup of that sweet lip of hers the skinker Jesus-breathed,
Crying, 'This the draught!' bescatters o'er the toper-company.

He who sees the hearts a-hanging from thy tress's tip would say,
''Tis a dragon, from his mouths he showereth sparks of fire, ah me!'

O Nejati, is there any price for thy gem-strewing reed?—
Every drop from yonder April cloud becomes a pearl, perdie.
