Religion

Psalms of the Brethren CLXXII: Bakula

by The Brethren of the Theragatha traditionally 6th–3rd century BCE; composition uncertain

Translated by Mrs. C. A. F. Rhys Davids (1913) · From Psalms of the Early Buddhists II: Psalms of the Brethren (1913)

Religion

He who is fain to-morrow to perform
The things that he should yesterday have done,
Forfeit of happy opportunity,
He shall anon repent him fierily.

Let him but talk of that which should be done;
Let him not talk of what should not be done!
Of him who talketh much, but doeth not,
Wise men take stock, and rate him at his worth.

O great, O wondrous is Nibbana’s bliss,
Revealed by Him, the Utterly Awake!
There comes no grief, no passion, haven sure,
Where ill and ailing perish evermore!

Source Thera-gatha, Psalms of the Early Buddhists, II: Psalms of the Brethren, II (Psalms of the Early Buddhists, vol. 2), trans. Mrs. C. A. F. Rhys Davids (London: Henry Frowde, Oxford University Press Warehouse, for the Pali Text Society, 1913). Digital text: Internet Archive psalmsofearlybud02davi.
