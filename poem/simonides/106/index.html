Fragment 55-56

by Simonides of Ceos c. 556 – 468 BC

Translated by J. M. Edmonds (1924) · From Lyra Graeca, vol. II (Loeb, 1924): Simonides

Arts & Sciences

Plutarch If the Oracles are no longer in Metre: So we went round and sat down on the southern basement of the temple, looking at the chapel of Earth and the water there. Whereupon Boethus exclaimed that the place contributed to the stranger's bewilderment. For there was a chapel of the Muses there, where the spring rises, which is why they used this water for libations; compare Simonides:

Where they draw the pure lustration-water from beneath the place of the fair-tressed Muses.

And again Simonides addressing Clio says a little more elaborately:

Thou overseer of the pure lustration-water, receiver of the prayers of many a pitcher-carrier, who givest free course through a golden pipe to the lovely liquor that comes of the ambrosial cave;

Eudoxus, therefore, is wrong in agreeing with the writers who make out that it is the water of the Styx that is so called.
