To a Lady: She Refusing to Continue a Dispute with Me

by Matthew Prior 1664–1721

From Poems on Several Occasions

LoveRelationships

SPARE, gen'rous Victor, spare the slave.
Who did unequal war pursue ;
That more than triumph he might have,
In being overcome by you.

In the dispute whate'er I said,
My heart was by my tongue belied ;
And in my looks you might have read
How much I argu'd on your side.

You, far from danger as from fear,
Might have sustain'd an open fight:
For seldom your opinions err ;
Your eyes are always in the right.

Why, fair one, would you not rely
On Reason's force with Beauty's join'd ?
Could I their prevalence deny,
I must at once be deaf and blind.

Alas ! not hoping to subdue,
I only to the fight aspir'd :
To keep the beauteous foe in view
Was all the glory I desir'd.

But she, howe'er of vict'ry sure,
Contemns the wreath too long delay'd ;
And, arm'd with more immediate power,
Calls cruel silence to her aid.

Deeper to wound, she shuns the fight :
She drops her arms, to gain the field :
Secures her conquest by her flight :
And triumphs, when she seems to yield.

So when the Parthian turn'd his steed,
And from the hostile camp withdrew ;
With cruel skill the backward reed
He sent ; and as he fled, he slew.

Source Matthew Prior, The Poetical Works of Matthew Prior, with Memoir by the Rev. John Mitford, Volume I, I, Aldine Edition of the British Poets (The Aldine Edition of the British Poets) (London: Bell and Daldy, 1866). Digital text: Internet Archive poeticalworks01prio. Codex Note

An ode in which a man who has lost an argument pleads that he never meant to win, and likens the lady's silence to the backward arrow of a Parthian horseman.
