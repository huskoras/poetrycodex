Epigram 17: What matter ist, how men their dayes

by John Taylor 1580–1653

From The Sculler (1612)

Social Commentaries

What matter ist, how men their dayes do spend,
So good report doe on their deaths attend:
Though in thy former life thou nere didst good,
But mad'st Religion for thy faults a hood,
And all blacke sinnes were harbourd in thy breast,
And tooke thy Conscience for their natiue nest:
Yet at thy buriall for a noble price,
Shalt haue a Sermon made, shall hide thy vice.
A threed-bare Parson shall thy prayse out-powre,
And in the expiration of an howre,
Will make the world thy honesty applaud,
And to thy passed life become a Baud.
Our Christian brother heere lyes dead (quoth he)
Who was the patterne of true Charity:
No Drunkard, Whoremonger, nor no vile swearer,
No greedy Vsurer, nor no Rent-rearer.
O deare beloued his example take,
And thus an end at this time doe I make.
Thus Mr. Parson nobly spends his breath,
To make a Villaine honest after death.
And for one noble, freely he affoords,
Much more then twenty shillings-worth of words.
