Love

To Lucasta, on Going Beyond the Seas

by Richard Lovelace 1617–1657

From The Golden Treasury, ed. F. T. Palgrave (1861)

Love

If to be absent were to be
Away from thee;
Or that when I am gone
You or I were alone;
Then, my Lucasta, might I crave
Pity from blustering wind, or swallowing wave.

Though seas and land betwixt us both,
Our faith and troth,
Like separated souls,
All time and space controls:
Above the highest sphere we meet
Unseen, unknown; and greet as Angels greet.

So then we do anticipate
Our after-fate,
And are alive i' the skies,
If thus our lips and eyes
Can speak like spirits unconfined
In Heaven, their earthy bodies left behind.

Source Francis Turner Palgrave, ed., The Golden Treasury of the Best Songs and Lyrical Pieces in the English Language (London and Glasgow: Collins' Clear-Type Press, n.d). Digital text: Project Gutenberg 19221.
