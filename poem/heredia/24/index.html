The Ravishment of Andromeda

by José-Maria de Heredia 1842–1905

Translated by Edward Robeson Taylor (1913) · From Greece and Sicily

Arts & Sciences

The splendid winged horse, in noiseless flight,
From out his nostrils blowing clouds of fume,
Bears them, with quivering of his every plume,
Across the starry ether and blue night.

Now Afric plunges from their soaring height,
Then Asia ... desert ... Libanus in tomb
Of mist and fog ... and here, all white with spume,
The mystic sea that closed sweet Helle's sight.

Like two enormous cloaks the wind swells wide
The pinions which, as through the stars they glide,
Keep the clasped lovers nested from the cold;

While, as their throbbing shadows they descry,
From Aries to Aquarius they behold
Their Constellations dawning in the sky.

Source José-Maria de Heredia, Sonnets from the Trophies of José-Maria de Heredia, trans. Edward Robeson Taylor, Fifth edition (San Francisco: Printed for the author (Taylor, Nash and Taylor), 1913). Digital text: Internet Archive sonnetsfromtroph00hererich.
