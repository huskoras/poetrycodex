Book I, 14: On a Feast

by Francis Quarles 1592–1644

From Divine Fancies (1633), Book I

Religion

The Lord of Heav'n and Earth ha's made a Feast,
And ev'ry Soule is an invited Guest:
The Word's the Food; the Levits are the Cookes;
The Fathers Writings are their Dyet-bookes;
But seldome us'd; for 'tis a fashion growne,
To recommend made Dishes of their owne:
What they should boyle, they bake; what rst, they broyle;
Their lushious Sallats are too sweet with Oyle:
In briefe, 'tis now a dayes too great a fault,
T'have too much Pepper, and too little Salt.

Source Francis Quarles, Divine Fancies: Digested into Epigrammes, Meditations, and Observations, Book I (of 4) (London: Printed by M. F. for John Marriot, and are to be sold at his shop in St Dunstan's Churchyard in Fleet Street, 1633). Digital text: EEBO-TCP A10251.
