Riddle XXIX. Moon and Sun

by Anonymous (Old English)

Translated by R. K. Gordon, Anglo-Saxon Poetry (Everyman’s Library, 1926) · From Exeter Book Riddles (selection)

Arts & Sciences

I saw a wondrous creature, a radiant air-vessel artfully adorned, bringing booty between her horns, spoil homewards from the foray. She wished to build herself a bower in the stronghold, set it up with skill, if so it might be. Then came a wondrous creature over the mountain-top — he is known to all dwellers on earth — snatched the spoil and drove the wanderer to her home against her will. Thence she departed westward on a journey of vengeance; she hastened forth. Dust rose to the heavens; dew fell on the earth; night went hence. No man afterwards knew where the creature journeyed.

Codex Note

One of roughly ninety riddles preserved in the Exeter Book; this is one of a further selection translated by R. K. Gordon. The manuscript gives no solutions — the title here follows the translator's own guess (numbering follows Tupper's edition, converted to this archive's Wyatt-based numbering).
