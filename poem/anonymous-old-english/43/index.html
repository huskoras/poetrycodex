Riddle XXXV. A Coat of Mail

by Anonymous (Old English) c. 7th–11th century

Translated by R. K. Gordon, Anglo-Saxon Poetry (Everyman’s Library, 1926) · From Exeter Book Riddles (selection)

Arts & Sciences

The dewy earth, wondrous cold, first brought me forth from its womb. I know in my thoughts I am not made of the fleeces of wool, of hairs, through excellent skill. Woofs are not wound round me, nor have I warp, nor does a thread of mine resound through the thrust of many strokes, nor does the whirring shuttle move in me, nor shall a weaver's rod smite me from anywhere. Silkworms, who deck the yellow precious web with adornments, did not weave me with the strength of the fates. Yet far and wide over the earth before men they will call me delightful. O thou skilled in cunning thoughts, learned in words, tell in true speech what is this garment.

This translation is printed as prose in its source edition.

✦ ✦ ✦

Codex Note

One of roughly ninety riddles preserved in the Exeter Book; this is one of a further selection translated by R. K. Gordon. The manuscript gives no solutions — the title here follows the translator's own guess (numbering follows Tupper's edition, converted to this archive's Wyatt-based numbering).
