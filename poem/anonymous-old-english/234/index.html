Religion

The Ruthwell Cross

by Anonymous (Old English) c. 7th–11th century

Translated by Albert S. Cook, in Cook & Tinker, Select Translations from Old English Poetry (1902) · From Select Translations from Old English Poetry, ed. Albert S. Cook & Chauncey B. Tinker (1902)

Religion

(Un)clothed Himself God almighty when He would
mount the cross, courageous in the sight of all men;
bow . . . .

. . . I the powerful King, the Lord of heaven; I
durst not bend. Men mocked us both together. I was
bedewed with blood, shed from . . . .

Christ was on the rood. But hastening thither came
noble ones from afar to the One (or, to the solitary One);
all that I beheld. Grievously was I afflicted with sorrows;
bowed . . . .

. . . wounded with arrows. They laid Him down,
weary of limb, stood at His body's head; beheld Him
there heaven . . . .

Source Albert S. Cook and Chauncey B. Tinker, ed., Select Translations from Old English Poetry, trans. Albert S. Cook (Boston: Ginn and Company, 1902). Digital text: Internet Archive selecttranslatio01cookuoft.
