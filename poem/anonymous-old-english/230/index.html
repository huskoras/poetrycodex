Metrical Charm 8: For a Swarm of Bees

by Anonymous (Old English) c. 7th–11th century

Translated by Albert S. Cook & Chauncey B. Tinker (eds), Select Translations from Old English Poetry (1902) · From Select Translations from Old English Poetry, ed. Albert S. Cook & Chauncey B. Tinker (1902)

Mythology & FolkloreNature

I take under foot, I have found it.
Verily earth avails against every creature,
And against mischief and mindlessness,
And against the great tongue of man.

Sit ye, victor-dames, sink to earth.
Never to fly wild to the wood!
Be as mindful of my good
As every man is of food and estate.

Source Albert S. Cook and Chauncey B. Tinker, ed., Select Translations from Old English Poetry, trans. William O. Stevens (Boston: Ginn and Company, 1902). Digital text: Internet Archive selecttranslatio01cookuoft. (details from catalogue records, not yet checked against the title page)

✦ ✦ ✦

Codex Note

A charm spoken to settle a swarming hive, with earth thrown over the bees. The translator is not named at the piece, so the editors are credited. ASPR Metrical Charm 8.
