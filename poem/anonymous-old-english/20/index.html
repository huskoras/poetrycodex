Riddle V. A Shield

by Anonymous (Old English) c. 7th–11th century

Translated by Cosette Faust Newton & Stith Thompson (1918) · From Exeter Book Riddles (selection)

Arts & Sciences

A lonely warrior, I am wounded with iron,
Scarred with sword-points, sated with battle-play,
Weary of weapons. I have witnessed much fighting,
Much stubborn strife. From the strokes of war
I have no hope for help or release
Ere I pass from the world with the proud warrior band.
With brands and billies they beat upon me;
The hard edges hack me; the handwork of smiths
In crowds I encounter; with courage I endure
Ever bitterer battles. No balm may I find,
And no doctor to heal me in the whole field of battle,
To bind me with ointments and bring me to health,
But my grievous gashes grow ever sorer
Through death-dealing strokes by day and night.

Source Cosette Faust and Stith Thompson, Old English Poems, Translated into the Original Meter, Together with Short Selections…, trans. Cosette Faust Newton and Stith Thompson (Chicago and New York: Scott, Foresman and Company, 1918). Digital text: Project Gutenberg 31172.

✦ ✦ ✦

Codex Note

One of roughly ninety riddles preserved in the Exeter Book; this is one of a small selection translated by Faust & Thompson. The manuscript gives no solutions — titles here follow the translators' own guesses.
