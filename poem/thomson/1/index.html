Love

For ever, Fortune, wilt thou prove

by James Thomson 1700–1748

From The Golden Treasury, ed. F. T. Palgrave (1861)

Love

For ever, Fortune, wilt thou prove
An unrelenting foe to Love,
And when we meet a mutual heart
Come in between, and bid us part?

Bid us sigh on from day to day,
And wish and wish the soul away;
Till youth and genial years are flown,
And all the life of life is gone?

But busy, busy, still art thou,
To bind the loveless joyless vow,
The heart from pleasure to delude,
To join the gentle to the rude.

For once, O Fortune, hear my prayer,
And I absolve thy future care;
All other blessings I resign,
Make but the dear Amanda mine.

Source Francis Turner Palgrave, ed., The Golden Treasury of the Best Songs and Lyrical Pieces in the English Language (London and Glasgow: Collins' Clear-Type Press, n.d). Digital text: Project Gutenberg 19221.
