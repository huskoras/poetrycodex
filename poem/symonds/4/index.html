Love

Love in Dreams

by John Addington Symonds 1840–1893

Love

Love hath his poppy-wreath,
Not Night alone.
I laid my head beneath
Love's lilied throne:
Then to my sleep he brought
This anodyne —
The flower of many a thought
And fancy fine:
A form, a face, no more;
Fairer than truth;
A dream from death's pale shore;
The soul of youth:
A dream so dear, so deep,
All dreams above,
That still I pray to sleep —
Bring Love back, Love!

Source Alfred H. Miles, ed., The Poets and the Poetry of the Century, Vol. 6: William Morris to Robert Buchanan (London: Hutchinson & Co, 1892 (1st ed.) or 1896 (2nd ed.)).
