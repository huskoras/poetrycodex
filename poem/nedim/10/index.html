Love

To Moorish Fez Her Locks and Cap Have Wroughten Mickle Woe

by Nedim c. 1681–1730

Translated by E. J. W. Gibb (1905) · From A History of Ottoman Poetry, vol. IV (1905)

Love

To Moorish Fez her locks and cap have wroughten mickle woe;
Her bright blue eyes have forayed in Circassian lands, I trow.

Her dusky tresses hang across her cheek; alack, O heart,
How passing fair doth sable o'er yon blush-pink satin show?

How should not roses, when they see her, rend their verdant wedes?
One glance upon that sendal green, that crimson fez bestow.

The flautist alway views askance the songster at the feast;
Whence comes this rivalry betwixt the twain, I ne'er can know.

Her curling tresses overfall her eyebrows, O Nedim,
And deck yon Ferrand Dome with Chinese pictury arow.

✦ ✦ ✦

Codex Note

A ghazel on a beloved whose dark locks, red fez and blue eyes are set beside Moor, Circassian and sable.
