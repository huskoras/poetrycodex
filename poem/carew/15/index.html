Love

SONG. Perswasions to enjoy.

by Thomas Carew 1595–1640

Love

IF the quick spirits in your eye
Now languish, and anon must dye▪
If every sweet, and every grace,
Must fly from that forsaken face:
Then (Celia) let us reape our joyes,
E're time such goodly fruit destroyes.

Or, if that golden fleece must grow
For ever, free from aged snow:
If those bright Suns must know no shade,
Nor your fresh beauties ever fade;
Then feare not (Celia) to bestow,
What still beirg gather'd, still must grow.
Thus, either Time his Sickle brings
In vaine, or else in vaine his wings.

Source Thomas Carew, Poems. By Thomas Carew Esquire, One of the Gentlemen of the Privie-Chamber, and Sewer in… (London: Printed by I.D. [John Dawson] for Thomas Walkley, and are to be sold at the signe of the flying Horse, between Brittains Burse, and York-House, 1640). Digital text: EEBO-TCP A17961.
