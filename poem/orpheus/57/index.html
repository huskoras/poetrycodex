Hymn LVII: To the Terrestrial Hermes

by Orpheus (Orphic Hymns) hymns c. 2nd–3rd c. AD (ascribed to the legendary Orpheus)

Translated by Thomas Taylor (1824) · From The Mystical Hymns of Orpheus (1824)

Religion

Hermes, I call, whom Fate decrees to dwell
Near to Cocytos, the fam'd stream of hell,
And in Necessity's dread path, whose bourn
To none that reach it e'er permits return.
O Bacchic Hermes, progeny divine
Of Dionysius, parent of the vine,
And of celestial Venus, Paphian queen,
Dark-eyelash'd Goddess, of a lovely mien :
Who constant wand'rest thro' the sacred seats
Where Hell's dread empress, Proserpine, retreats ;
To wretched souls the leader of the way,
When Fate decrees, to regions void of day.
Thine is the wand which causes sleep to fly,
Or lulls to slumb'rous rest the weary eye ;
For Proserpine, thro' Tart'rus dark and wide,
Gave thee for ever flowing souls to guide.
Come, blessed pow'r, the sacrifice attend,
And grant thy mystics' works a happy end.

Source Orpheus, The Mystical Hymns of Orpheus, Translated from the Greek, and Demonstrated to Be the…, trans. Thomas Taylor (London: Bertram Dobell; Reeves and Turner, 1896). Digital text: Internet Archive mysticalhymnsor00taylgoog.
