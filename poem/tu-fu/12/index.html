Time & Brevity

Dum Res et Aetas

by Tu Fu 712–770

Translated by Herbert A. Giles (1898) · From Chinese Poetry in English Verse (1898)

Time & Brevity

From the court every eve to the pawnshop
I pass,
To come back from the river the drunkest of men;
As often as not I'm in debt for my glass; —
Well, few of us live to be threescore and ten.
The butterfly flutters from flower to flower.
The dragon-fly sips and springs lightly away.
Each creature is merry its brief little hour.
So let us enjoy our short life while we may.

Source Herbert A. Giles, trans., Chinese Poetry in English Verse (London / Shanghai: Bernard Quaritch; Kelly & Walsh, Ltd, 1898). Digital text: Internet Archive cu31924008867222.
