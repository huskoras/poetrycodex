Love

A Valentine

by James T. Fields 1817–1881

From The Poets and Poetry of America (1855)

Love

She that is fair, though never vain or proud,
More fond of home than fashion's changing crowd;
Whose taste refined even female friends admire,
Dress'd not for show, but robed in neat attire;
She who has learn'd, with mild, forgiving breast,
To pardon frailties, hidden or confess'd;
True to herself, yet willing to submit,
More sway'd by love than ruled by worldly wit:
Though young, discreet — though ready, ne'er unkind,
Blest with no pedant's, but a woman's mind:
She wins our hearts, toward her our thoughts incline,
So at her door go leave my Valentine.

Source Rufus Wilmot Griswold, ed., The Poets and Poetry of America, Sixteenth edition, carefully revised, much enlarged, and continued to the present time (Philadelphia: Parry and McMillan (successors to A. Hart, late Carey & Hart), 1855). Digital text: Internet Archive poetspoetryofame00gris.
