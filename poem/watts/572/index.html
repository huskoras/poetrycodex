Hymn II.67: God's eternal dominion

by Isaac Watts 1674–1748

From Hymns and Spiritual Songs, Book II (1707)

Religion

Great God! how infinite art thou!
What worthless worms are we!
Let the whole race of creatures bow,
And pay their praise to thee.

Thy throne eternal ages stood,
Ere seas or stars were made;
Thou art the ever-living God,
Were all the nations dead.

Nature and time quite naked lie
To thine immense survey,
From the formation of the sky
To the great burning day.

Eternity, with all its years.
Stands present in thy view;
To thee there's nothing old appears,
Great God! there's nothing new.

Our lives through various scenes are drawn,
And vexed with trifling cares,
While thine eternal thought moves on
Thine undisturbed affairs.

Great God! how infinite art thou!
What worthless worms are we!
Let the whole race of creatures bow,
And pay their praise to thee.

Source Isaac Watts, Hymns and Spiritual Songs, Book II (London, 1707). Digital text: Christian Classics Ethereal Library psalmshymns. (details from catalogue records, not yet checked against the title page)
