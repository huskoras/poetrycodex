Psalm 90: The frailty and shortness of life

by Isaac Watts 1674–1748

From The Psalms of David Imitated (1719)

Religion

Lord, what a feeble piece
Is this our mortal frame!
Our life how poor a trifle 'tis,
That scarce deserves the name!

Alas, the brittle clay
That built our body first!
And every month, and every day,
'Tis mould'ring back to dust.

Our moments fly apace,
Nor will our minutes stay;
Just like a flood, our hasty days
Are sweeping us away.

Well, if our days must fly,
We'll keep their end in sight;
We'll spend them all in wisdom's way,
And let them speed their flight.

They'll waft us sooner o'er
This life's tempestuous sea;
Soon we shall reach the peaceful shore
Of blest eternity.

Source Isaac Watts, The Psalms of David Imitated in the Language of the New Testament, and Apply'd to the… (London: J. Clark, R. Ford and R. Cruttenden, 1719). Digital text: other CCEL, Psalms and Hymns of Isaac Watts. (details from catalogue records, not yet checked against the title page)
