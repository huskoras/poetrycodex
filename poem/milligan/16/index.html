Death & Dying

The Wake Feast (A Young Man Dead)

by Alice Milligan 1865–1953

From Hero Lays (1908)

Death & Dying

I cannot remember
That we ever told
Half the things we thought of you,
Half the praise that was your due,
Oh, heart of gold!
Or ever showed you reverence, when
You walked the land of living men,
And talked as none shall talk again,
Now your lips are cold.

The last time that we passed you by
Sad was your face,
As if across your sunlit soul
A shadowing cloud had risen to roll
For a little space.
If we had paused to praise you then,
I think you would have smiled again
With kindly grace.

But we were niggards of our praise;
And so until an end of days
We must think at word of you always,
“How sad was his face!”

Now to your father’s house
The folk come flocking in,
The neighbours and kindred
And comrades more than kin.
The candles are lit there,
The wake-feast is spread;
They will sit the whole night long
Praising the dead.

They will praise your comeliness,
The strength of your hands,
Say “There was not known his like
In nine townlands.”
They will not tell how nobleness
Had won you hate,
And that you had no praise of friends
Till now, too late.
